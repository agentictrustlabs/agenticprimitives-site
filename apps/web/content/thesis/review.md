*This is the project's own critical reading of its thesis, written against the substrate's contracts, packages and the running estate. It is here because a thesis that cannot survive its own hardest questions is not worth a stranger's time, and because the people who doubt it are usually right about something. Where a claim does not hold, this page says so and says what would make it hold.*

## What the bet is, and what can actually be tested

The thesis is stated as a vision: *build an ecosystem where every participant expands what everyone can accomplish.* A vision cannot be wrong, which is a problem for a critical reading. So this review holds the thesis to the three claims inside it that can be: that a **shared domain foundation** makes each additional participant cheaper to coordinate than rebuilding meaning, permissions and evidence at every connection (§3); that the next participant is **easier to connect** and its capabilities become usable in **more than one journey** (the close); and that every participant **retains control** of what it contributes while the journey still completes (bet 13). Everything below is a reading of whether the substrate, as built, can make those three true — and in which of the five markets.

One distinction first, because the thesis depends on it. A person is an agent — a Person agent, `alice.me`, the account that can sign for her — and the agents that act for her in her community, her fitness, her travel or a game are software agents chartered under that account: they belong to her and act only under her grants, and none of them is her. The agents across the table — a company's Organization agent, an app's Workspace agent, a Treasury — belong to someone else. "Participant" below means a principal and the agents it has chartered; "act" means one of them acting under its principal's authority against an agent that is not its own.

## The competitor the thesis faces is the platform

The thesis grants that identity providers, payment rails, systems of record and agent frameworks all have roles in the architecture, and it names the ecosystem builder as its customer. That choice decides who the competitor is. A builder assembling a care network, a fitness community or a travel marketplace has a proven option the thesis's own table names first: the **comprehensive platform** — one operating environment that brings participants, workflows and records inside its walls and makes coordination a feature rather than a protocol. Platforms win because the builder gets to define the rules, because the first ten participants are onboarded by the platform's own staff, and because a bounded journey is simpler inside one system than across many.

The substrate is not that, and the thesis is right to keep the difference sharp. It is a kit and a domain ontology deployed as **estates** — several per vertical, each governed by that vertical's own institutions — with identities that belong to their principals rather than to any estate. A person's Home lives in one estate; her account, her agents, her grants and her receipts are hers, and she can participate in any estate that admits her. A platform's participants exist inside its walls; an estate's participants bring their walls with them. That self-sovereignty is what lets parties who would never join one operator's system share a foundation at all.

Two honest qualifications. The multi-estate life the thesis describes is not what the product does today: an identity's Home, its grants and its relationships are resolved inside one estate, and a verifier in a second estate cannot read the first estate's chain. Participating across estates needs the public projection of a private grant — a revocation root and a membership root a stranger can verify — and a Home-to-Home rotation ceremony, both of which are tracked findings, not features. And a shared domain foundation beats a platform only where the platform cannot go: where participants are **genuinely separately governed** and will not join one operator's environment — a congregation and a mission agency, a clinic and a hospital system, a cruise line and an independent excursion operator. That is the honest scope of the bet, and it is narrower than "five markets." In each market the question is not "could the substrate coordinate this journey" but "are the participants unwilling to be coordinated by a platform, and is there a builder who wants to serve them anyway." Where the answer is no, the platform wins and should.

The thesis's own table is right about the responsibility a shared foundation takes on: *govern the common model, make integrations dependable, and prove reuse.* The first of those is the one to watch. A foundation that governs the common model for five markets is a central actor with a great deal of say over what a referral, a reservation or a mandate means. The thesis mitigates this with domain stewards and open vocabularies; it does not yet say who those stewards are in any market but one.

## Five markets, read against what exists

The thesis says the five markets "do not imply that every domain integration is already implemented." A critical reading has to say which are.

| Market | Domain model in the substrate | Running proof | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Faith | Yes — Global.Church and ChurchCore ontologies; church, household and circle agent types; a mission-field workspace | An estate with people, congregations, treasuries, agents, mandates and receipts; the Home, Gather27 and Field applications | The only market with both a model and evidence. The two-organization proof the thesis names has not been run. |
| Finance | Partially — treasury as a service agent, payment mandates, payment and value enforcers on chain | The buy-in flow in the card room: a person's treasury pays a table under a mandate, single-use, enforced at commit | The §10 proof — an organization's planning workflow paying through its treasury — already runs in-estate. That is a proof of the mechanics, not of a financial *market*: a bank will not accept a substrate mandate as authorization. Open-banking consent regimes already federate that case, and the substrate is an adapter under them, not a replacement. |
| Fitness | No domain model | None | Low-consequence acts, high value in continuity. The competition is not an authority stack; it is the health-data export formats and the coaching platforms that already hold the goal. The right reason to do it early is that it is the cheapest possible test of reuse — and the thesis's reuse claim is the one that most needs testing. |
| Healthcare | No domain model; FHIR and its consent and purpose-of-use vocabularies exist and must be mapped, never reinvented | None | The referral handoff the thesis names is a real cross-organization act, and it is the market where "clinical responsibility remains with the clinician" and institutional recordkeeping duties are hardest. Nothing here should be attempted without a partner institution that already owns the compliance obligation. |
| Travel | No domain model | None | The missed-port change across a cruise line and an excursion operator is the best cross-organization proof in the table, because the two providers are strangers to each other. It is also the market with the strongest incumbents in the crossing — payment networks, distribution systems, the lines' own booking programmes. Test at the long tail, where an excursion operator already wants the direct relationship. |

One market has evidence, one has the mechanics, three have neither yet. The thesis is candid about this in words; the shape of the page — five equal rows — invites a reader to weigh them equally, and a doubter will notice. The ordering the thesis gives its proofs is the right one: faith first, because it runs; then a cheap reuse test; then the regulated and intermediated markets only with a partner who carries the obligation.

## The thirteen bets, against the code

Each bet names its own test. This is what the substrate can bring to each test today.

| # | Bet | What exists | What is missing for the test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Coordination through explicit commitments | A coordination package with plans, commitments and endeavours; a separate namespace from orchestration; signed contribution commitments against a plan revision | A plan completed by two separately governed organizations. Never run. |
| 2 | Shared meaning makes capabilities reusable | 39 substrate modules with SHACL shapes; PROV-O, P-Plan, DOLCE beneath; domain ontologies for faith, family office, household and a card room; SKOS crosswalks to PROV, W3C Organization, ActivityStreams, A2A, ERC-8004, ValueFlows, schema.org | Any measurement of integration effort or semantic defects against a baseline. The best-argued bet and the least-measured. |
| 3 | Identity survives changes in tools and operators | Smart accounts; custody policies; the behaviour definition's digest on every receipt | A rotation ceremony that re-approves standing grants. The contract voids every grant on any credential retirement, by design; the Home has no ceremony for a person to rotate without losing them. Tracked. |
| 4 | Discovery leads to a capability someone can responsibly engage | Registry kit with pluggable admission and signed admission receipts; agent cards projected from the profile; resolution as signed, expiring publications | Nothing structural. Live on the estate. |
| 5 | Records remain useful across applications | Per-record vault with data keys released under grant; ontology-shaped records; organization vaults | A demonstrated migration of a live person between two Homes; the vault form of a relationship (today chain-only); shared-record reconciliation. |
| 6 | Discovery coexists with privacy | Two knowledge tiers never joined in an engine; private-with-audience and pairwise publications; the graph on a private chain | The public projection: a membership or revocation root and a proof against it, so a verifier outside the estate can check without reading the graph. Tracked. |
| 7 | Reusable primitives produce a builder advantage | 77 packages, a developer kit, a scaffold that builds a relying app in minutes | The measurement the thesis itself demands: the next capability costs less and serves two journeys. |
| 8 | Enforceable limits make autonomy practical | Per-step verification; re-verification after approval; mandates enforced at commit; the revoke-mid-run live gate | Aggregate limits across delegates (a per-treasury enforcer); the adapter's observation on the receipt for effects the chain does not order. Tracked. |
| 9 | Trust depends on relationship, purpose and evidence | Signed relationships, attestations, verification and interaction receipts, admission receipts, as separate edges; no score at the index | The relational query as a first-class operation; a statement of which edges count against collusion. |
| 10 | Conversation and screens invoke the same capabilities | Capability definitions from which the interface, the planner's tools and the agent card are generated; a build gate on invented terms | Nothing structural. This is the estate's most productive discipline and the reason its recent failures were knowledge failures rather than authority failures. |
| 11 | Broad goals become specific, accountable actions | Closed mandates — intent digest, single-use nonce, pinned payee and ceiling — enforced at commit; fan-out compiled into one mandate per item | A conformance check for open intents; version binding of the schema and adapter into the mandate; a nonce derived from a logical request identity. Tracked. |
| 12 | A usable place to manage participation | The Home: passkeys, ceremonies, linked apps with their grants, revoke, the vault | The rotation ceremony; a working Home-to-Home migration; participation in a second estate from one Home. Passkeys are scoped to one relying party, so moving Homes is a custody ceremony that must be built as a rotation, never a recovery. |
| 13 | Every participant retains authority over its resources | Admission at every edge; entitlements; relationships defined as never authority; a receiver that re-verifies a grant before executing | Nothing structural. This is the bet the substrate is best at, and it should be said louder: a shared plan grants no one anything. |

Four of the thirteen need nothing structural; five wait on work that is designed and tracked; four wait on measurement or on a demonstration between separately governed parties. None is refuted by the code. None of the economic ones is yet supported by it.

## The eleven principles, judged

The principles carry their own caveats — a private chain does not establish neutrality; identity continuity is not competence; a permission check does not make an external action atomic; receipts must say what they attest. That is the right way to write them, and it moves the question from "is the principle true" to "does the product do what the principle says." Where it does not yet, the gap is a tracked finding in the ledger with a file-and-symbol anchor.

| # | Principle | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Separate private state from public claims | **Right, and half built.** Private enforcement exists on the estate chain; the public projection — a root and a proof — does not. Until it does, no verifier outside the estate can check anything, and "neutrality" is one operator's promise: the estate chain is four validators on one machine. |
| 2 | An identity that can sign and delegate | **Holds.** The firmest ground: an ERC-7710 delegation needs a delegator a stranger can check at redemption, and a pointer cannot be that. |
| 3 | Distinguish rotation from recovery | **Right in the contract, absent in the product.** Every credential retirement bumps the custody epoch and voids grants signed by that credential — correct after a compromise, and the thesis says a planned rotation must re-approve. The Home has no such ceremony; today, replacing a device is a silent revocation. |
| 4 | Authority as a bounded, revocable grant | **Holds, stated precisely.** The thesis does not claim that tokens cannot express specific authority; it claims principal issuance, portability, verifiable attenuation and enforcement at commit — the four things federation cannot supply together. |
| 5 | Bind consequential effects to the approved intent | **Holds for closed acts.** Open goals decomposing into bounded actions is designed, not shipped: the conformance check and the version binding do not yet exist. |
| 6 | Verify outside the model and at the point of execution | **Holds for chain-ordered and estate-ordered effects.** The receiving edge re-verifies before executing. For external adapters the thesis demands a stated window, retry behaviour and evidence; the invoker port returns an untyped value, so the receipt cannot yet carry the adapter's observation. |
| 7 | People, organizations and software agents accountable differently | **A convention, well defended.** The thesis calls it a model rather than a law, and names the domain acts — a clinician's judgment, a ministry's commitment, a traveller's consent — it must keep distinct. |
| 8 | One governed vocabulary from intent to receipt | **Holds, and is the least-measured principle.** The stack exists, the crosswalks exist, the gate exists; the thesis's own caveat — generated surfaces can share a mistake — is why the negative-twin live gates are load-bearing and should be described as such. |
| 9 | Durable records under participant control | **Holds for personal records.** Shared records, institutional recordkeeping duties in healthcare and finance, and the vault form of a relationship are specified, not built. |
| 10 | Evidence portable and precise about what it proves | **Holds as statement evidence.** Anchoring exists. The four-way distinction — attempt, acceptance, state change, confirmed outcome — is on the tool contract as an outcome class and not yet on the receipt itself. |
| 11 | Participants evaluate trust from relevant evidence | **Holds.** Reader-side evaluation is the principle; the missing piece is naming which edges cost enough to count against collusion. |

## Two things the thesis does not say

**Five markets is a lot for a small team.** The thesis says readiness must be assessed per domain and that not every domain should launch at once. It does not say that four of the five have no domain model, that healthcare and finance each carry compliance obligations the substrate cannot own, and that a builder in any of them will ask "who else is on this" before "what are the primitives." Breadth is the right shape of the *architecture*; it is the wrong shape of a roadmap. The candid version of §10 is: faith, because it runs; fitness, because it is the cheapest test of reuse; finance in-estate, because the mechanics exist; healthcare and travel only with a partner who already carries the obligation and wants the direct relationship.

**The ecosystem builder mostly does not exist yet.** The thesis's customer is someone building a marketplace, a care network or a partnership on a foundation they do not operate. Few such builders exist today; most people with the coordination pain the thesis describes are an *organization* with a concrete problem — a mission agency that cannot coordinate three partners, a clinic that cannot hand off cleanly — not a builder of ecosystems. The nearer customer is that organization, and the product it buys is one journey that works across two parties. The ecosystem is what accumulates if enough of those are built on the same foundation. The thesis has this sequence in §4; it should lead with it.

## What would prove it

The thesis's §10 is the right experiment and its metrics are the right metrics: time to onboard the next participant, effort to reuse a capability in a second workflow, manual coordination remaining, outcomes completed, unauthorized actions refused, provider change without lost records. Two additions make it decisive.

Run the same journey a second way. Build the first faith-domain workflow — an invitation, a shared service effort, a payment — on the substrate and on a network-of-integrations baseline, and compare the full cost including governance and exception handling. The thesis's economic claim is a comparison; it needs a comparator.

And run it between two estates, not inside one. Two separately governed organizations, on separately governed deployments, completing the journey, limiting each other's authority, surviving a revoked grant, a compromised key with descendants, a failed provider action and a migration — and preferring it. Until that exists, the substrate has demonstrated an integrated platform, and the thesis should be read as a well-argued bet.

## To the doubter

You are right that a platform is simpler for a bounded journey and that a few direct integrations serve a small partner network. You are right that "every participant expands what everyone can accomplish" is a vision, not a claim, and that the claims inside it are unmeasured. You are right that four of the five markets have no domain model, that a bank will not take a mandate from a substrate as authorization, that a private chain run by one operator is not neutral, and that the ecosystem builder the thesis addresses is rare. The thesis is better for each of those, and this page exists so that none of them has to be said from outside.

What you may not yet have seen is the thing a platform cannot do and a network of integrations cannot afford: let two parties who will not join one operator's system complete a journey together, each under its own authority, with a receipt either can show a stranger — and do it again, more cheaply, when a third party joins. That is the claim worth defending, and the experiment above is how it gets defended.
