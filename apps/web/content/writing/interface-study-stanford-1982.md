## The study and why we depend on it

**A. Akinwande, C. Ho and J. Plummer, “Si/SiO2 Interface Charges”, section 5 of Stanford Integrated Circuits Laboratory, Computer-Aided Design of Integrated Circuit Fabrication Processes for VLSI Devices, semi-annual report for 1 April to 30 September 1982, DTIC AD-A130105 (June 1983), printed pages 42 to 56.**

This report is where the fixed-charge program learned what happens without oxygen. Its argon time series at 800 and 1000 °C (Figure 5) fixed the program's argon clock and the (100) equilibrium; the (111) thermal cycling (Figure 8) fixed the (111) equilibrium (`scripts/run_fixed_charge_two_lines.py`, `evaluations/fixed_charge_two_lines_report.json`). So agreement on those 20 numbers is not a test of anything: it shows only that the history we write here reproduces the one the fit used. The as-grown values (3) and the (100) cycling (4) were not fitted. The page's real purpose is the history between the steps, which the report does not state, and the cooling after each pull.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Moderate.** The oxidation is stated in full (pre-heat, HCl etch, purge, 75 min dry oxygen at 1000 °C, a 2 s pull), and so are the anneal temperatures and gas. What is not stated is everything between the steps: whether a wafer left the furnace between oxidation and anneal, how it went in and out of each anneal, how it cooled, and the anneal after metallization. Every value is on a figure, none in a table.

| | |
|---|---|
| Specimen described | 2 of 5 properties we need are stated and encoded the same |
| History described | 2 of 10 steps are stated and encoded the same; 3 are stated by the paper and absent from our history; 1 are ours because the paper is silent |
| Timing | 4 of 6 simulated steps have a stated duration; 3 of 3 other steps have none; 7 timing gaps listed below |
| How the results are given | Figures only: Figure 5 (charge against argon time, log axis) and Figure 8 (cycling), read by eye by an AI agent as cluster means; no table |
| Repeatability stated by the authors | Not stated; Figures 5 and 8 show 2 to 5 points per condition, spread up to about 1.5 × 10^10 cm^-2 |
| Internal consistency | Good between the figures (Figure 5 and Figures 6, 7 agree in order of magnitude on the time constants). The text promises 900 °C anneals that no figure shows; the earlier report's argon is “< 2 ppm” water, this one's “< 1 ppm” |
| Is our model independent of this paper's data? | No. The fixed-charge program's argon clock and both argon equilibria (six numbers) were fitted to exactly these Figure 5 and Figure 8 points. Agreement on this page is not a test; only the as-grown values and the (100) cycling were not fitted |
| Outcome | 17 of 27 numbers within tolerance |

The grade is ours and is about the paper as a source for simulation, not about the quality of the experiment: **high** means the specimen, every step and its timing are stated well enough that a simulation needs no choices of ours; **moderate** that some choices are ours and are listed; **low** that the result depends on choices the paper does not constrain.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- Whether the anneal wafers were pulled after oxidation and pushed again into an argon furnace, or annealed in the same tube with the gas switched.
- Push and pull of every anneal: time, gas and whether fast. Our program's argon clock is 1.6 min at 1000 °C and 0.5 min at 1100 °C, so a slow pull out of an 1100 °C anneal is part of the anneal.
- How the wafer cools after the 2 s pull, and in what gas: the report says “from the oxidizing ambient”, and Figure 4 shows a nitrogen flush at the mouth.
- Whether the second 800 °C and 1100 °C cycles lasted 25 h and 20 min like the first; whether the cycling used one set of wafers measured between steps.
- Whether the Figure 5 wafers had the forming-gas anneal that Figures 6 and 7 state, and its temperature, time and gas.
- Where time zero of the argon anneal is counted from: the push, or reaching temperature.
- Push rate and gas of the load into the oxidation furnace; oxide thickness.

### What the second reading changed

- The stored recipes (data/derived/akinwande_1982_nf.csv through scripts/canonical_measurements.py) begin with the 2 s pull. The 60 min of argon pre-heat, HCl etch and purge and the 75 min oxidation are not steps; the oxidation enters the program only as a constant (T_ox = 1000 °C). For the charge this changes nothing, because the program starts from the steady value at the oxidation temperature.
- Every argon anneal is stored as AMBIENT inert_N2. The report says argon with under 1 ppm water. Both have no oxygen, so the numbers are the same; the record is wrong.
- The stored recipes go from the pull's end at 400 °C straight into the anneal at 800 or 1000 °C, with no push, and end with the anneal: there is no pull after it. Here each anneal has a push and a pull, as unknowns.
- The stored cycling recipes run the four anneals back to back with no pull between them, and repeat 25 h and 20 min although the figure prints each once.
- The forming-gas anneal after metallization, stated on Figures 6 and 7, is in no stored recipe. The program would ignore it.
- Both readings agree on the values: the second reading's Figure 8 clusters are within 0.05 × 10^11 cm^-2 of the dataset's, and its Figure 5 values match within the reading of a log time axis (it gives 0.09 h where the dataset has 0.092 h; it lists no points at 0.178 and 0.55 h).

## The simulation in our language

Each specimen is one history file: a `SPECIMEN` and a `CONTROL_TRAJECTORY`. This is the whole input; run it with `PYTHONPATH=. python -m isl_harness.predict <file>`.

#### (100), as grown (Figure 5)

```isl
# (100), as grown (Figure 5)
SPECIMEN Wafer_series_100_asgrownC_0h {
    FAMILY: Si_Planar
    ORIENTATION: Si100
    CLEAN: HCl_in_situ
}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY History {
    EVENT Load KIND load TIMING assumed                          # Pushed into the fast-pull furnace
    RUN PreHeat DURATION 1200 TEMP 1000->1000 AMBIENT inert_Ar TIMING stated
    RUN HClEtch DURATION 1200 TEMP 1000->1000 AMBIENT inert_Ar TIMING stated
    RUN Purge DURATION 1200 TEMP 1000->1000 AMBIENT inert_Ar TIMING stated

    RUN FGA DURATION 1800 TEMP 400->400 AMBIENT forming_gas_H2 TIMING assumed
    EVENT CV KIND measurement TIMING assumed                     # C-V: flatband voltage
}
```

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: FIXED CHARGE WITH AN EQUILIBRIUM IN EACH GAS
#
# In oxygen the charge moves toward a steady value that falls with temperature (the
# line of Deal et al. 1967, unchanged, with the oxygen kinetics fitted there).
# In a gas without oxygen it moves toward a lower equilibrium that also falls with
# temperature, Qi_ref * exp(E_q / k_B * (1/T - 1/T_iref)), at a rate of its own.
#
# The inert clock and the (100) inert equilibrium are fitted to the argon time series
# of Akinwande, Ho and Plummer (1982). The (111) inert equilibrium is fitted to their
# cycling experiment, with the clock carried over from (100). The model is then
# tested, unchanged, on the Fairchild wafers of 1967 and 1980.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_fixed_charge_two_lines.py.
# ==============================================================================
SPECIMEN Wafer_{tag} {{
    FAMILY: Si_Planar
    ORIENTATION: Si{orientation}
}}
# The wafer starts at the steady value for its oxidation temperature.
STATE Q_f {{ UNIT: cm^-2; INITIAL: Q_hot + slope * (T_hot - T_ox); MIN: 0; MAX: 1.0e14 }}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_O2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER Q_hot {{ VALUE: {Q_hot}; UNIT: cm^-2; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal et al. 1967 Table II, steady value after dry oxidation at 1200 C }}
PARAMETER slope {{ VALUE: {slope}; UNIT: cm^-2 K^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal et al. 1967 Table II, line through the 1200 C and 920 C steady values }}
PARAMETER T_hot {{ VALUE: 1473.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER T_ox {{ VALUE: {T_ox_K}; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
# ---- fitted to the pull-rate series ------------------------------------------------
PARAMETER k0 {{ VALUE: 1.0e3; UNIT: s^-1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_a {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
# ---- fitted to the argon anneals of Akinwande, Ho and Plummer (1982) -----------------
PARAMETER k_i0 {{ VALUE: 1.0e1; UNIT: s^-1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_i {{ VALUE: 0.9; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER Qi_ref {{ VALUE: 2.5e10; UNIT: cm^-2; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_q {{ VALUE: 0.4; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER T_iref {{ VALUE: 1273.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
QUANTITY rate {{ UNIT: s^-1; EQUATION: rate = k0 * exp(-E_a / (k_B * T)) }}
QUANTITY rate_inert {{ UNIT: s^-1; EQUATION: rate_inert = k_i0 * exp(-E_i / (k_B * T)) }}
QUANTITY Q_inert {{ UNIT: cm^-2; EQUATION: Q_inert = Qi_ref * exp((E_q / k_B) * (1 / T - 1 / T_iref)) }}
QUANTITY oxidizing {{ UNIT: 1; EQUATION: oxidizing = p_O2 / p_ref }}
QUANTITY Q_steady {{ UNIT: cm^-2; EQUATION: Q_steady = Q_hot + slope * (T_hot - T) }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY History_{tag} {{
{runs}
}}
# In oxygen the charge follows the temperature-dependent steady value.
OPERATOR F_OxidizingEquilibration : Relaxation {{
    EQUATION: d(Q_f)/dt = -rate * oxidizing * (Q_f - Q_steady)
}}
# Without oxygen it relaxes, at its own rate, toward an equilibrium of its own that falls as temperature rises.
# Akinwande, Ho and Plummer (1982) show the annealed charge cycling reversibly with temperature in argon.
OPERATOR F_InertRelaxation : Relaxation {{
    EQUATION: d(Q_f)/dt = -rate_inert * (1 - oxidizing) * (Q_f - Q_inert)
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Fixed_Charge {{ TARGET: Qss; UNIT: cm^-2; EQUATION: Qss = Q_f }}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT Contract_{tag} {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/deal_1967_qss.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{dataset_sha256}
    SPLIT: {split}
    TARGET: Qss = {target} +/- {tolerance} abs
}}
```

**Push and pull of each anneal.** Every scan is flat: across 5 s to 10 min for a push and 2 s to 10 min for a pull, the fitted cases pass 14 or 15 of 20. The search takes the shortest push (5 s) and the shortest first pull (2 s), which is the stored recipe's instant change of temperature. That is not evidence about Stanford's furnace. The program's argon clock was fitted with no push and no pull, so it already contains whatever they did. The third pull went to 10 min, which moves a wafer leaving 800 °C by under 1%.

**End of the oxidation pull.** The as-grown charge falls from 1.40 to 1.32 × 10^11 cm^-2 (100) as the end of the 2 s straight-line pull moves from 400 to 700 °C (measured 1.16 and 1.30). The search takes 700 °C, the top of the range.

**Cooling after the pull: a model finding.** Written as a realistic cool (PROFILE exponential, TAU 30 s toward 25 °C, over 5 min) in the oxygen the wafer was pulled from, the as-grown (100) charge goes from 1.40 to 6.36 × 10^11 cm^-2 and (111) from 4.1 to 14 × 10^11, 4.5 and 3.4 times the report. TAU 10 s gives the same number. A 60 s cool gives 4.2 instead of 6.4, so the result depends on how long we let the simulation run, not on the cooling. The cause is the program's oxygen rate. It was fitted to Deal's 1967 pull series with an activation energy of 0.05 eV, so it is nearly as fast at room temperature as at 1000 °C, and it drives the charge toward Deal's line extrapolated to 25 °C. The 400 °C end of a straight-line pull is a cut-off that hides this. It is not a furnace parameter. Scored across all 27 numbers, the oxygen cool drops the result from 17 to 14 within tolerance, and every wafer cooled in oxygen is 3 to 5 times too high, except the cycling wafers whose last anneal erased the start.

The as-grown (100) value becomes 1.25 against 1.16 and 1.30 measured. The (111) value stays high (3.8 against 2.7). What alignment cannot fix. The as-grown (111) charge is 1.5 times the report's (4.1 or, cooled in nitrogen, 3.8 against 2.7 × 10^11 cm^-2). The program starts every wafer at Deal's 1967 steady value for the oxidation temperature, a Fairchild line. Stanford's wafers start lower, and no history after the oxidation can lower a starting point. The (100) cycling after 800 °C is 35–45% low (5.4 against 8.3 and 9.7). The (100) 800 °C equilibrium was fitted on the time series to 5.4, and the cycling wafers (Cz, a separate batch) settle higher. That is batch to batch within one laboratory, the difference the authors flag when they propose repeating the cycling on CZ and FZ wafers.

The cooling result is a defect in the program, not in the report. A rate with almost no activation energy cannot be given a realistic cooling curve, so the program needs a cut-off temperature that is not physics. Until its oxygen kinetics are refitted against pulls written as cooling curves, every history must end its pulls at 400 °C in a straight line to be scored as the program was.

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **A rate that freezes.** PROFILE exponential now lets a history say how a wafer cools. This study shows the language cannot yet stop a program from moving charge at room temperature. The ontology could carry, per operator, a temperature (or activation energy) below which it is declared inactive, and the predictor could refuse a history that runs an operator outside it. That would turn the hidden 400 °C cut-off into a stated, testable property.
- **The gas a wafer cools in.** A pull leaves one gas and cools in another (oxygen in the tube, nitrogen at the flushed mouth, air in the room). A RUN has one AMBIENT. A PROFILE that names the gas after withdrawal, or a WITHDRAW step with its own gas, would make the variant that scores best here a statement.
- **HCl in a mixture.** “20 min 1% HCl/Ar etch” cannot be written: the mixture gases are O2, N2, H2, H2O and Ar. Adding HCl (and Cl2) would record the etch and, later, the HCl oxidations of Razouk and Deal's Figure 9.
- **One wafer measured several times.** The cycling is one sequence of anneals with a measurement after each. Five separate histories hide that they are prefixes of one. A MEASURE event inside a history whose readings are the scored values would state it once and would show that the metal must come off between measurements.
- **Where a starting value comes from.** The charge program's initial state is Deal's 1967 line for the oxidation temperature. Stanford's as-grown values sit below it on (111). The starting point is a laboratory property hidden in a program, and should be a stated input (for example `INITIAL Q_f FROM laboratory` with the 1967 line as one choice).
- **Time zero of an anneal.** Figure 5 times count from an unstated moment (the push, or reaching temperature). TIMING says whether a duration is stated, not what it is counted from. An `ORIGIN push|at_temperature|gas_switch` on a RUN would carry it.
- **Repeat durations.** The second 25 h and 20 min of the cycling are assumed equal to the first. A `TIMING assumed_same_as <step>` would make the assumption visible.
