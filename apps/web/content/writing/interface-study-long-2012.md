## The study and why we depend on it

**E. Long, A. Azarov, F. Kløw, A. Galeckas, A. Yu. Kuznetsov and S. Diplas, “Ge redistribution in SiO2/SiGe structures under thermal oxidation: Dynamics and predictions”, J. Appl. Phys. 111, 024308 (2012), doi:10.1063/1.3677987.**

Long 2012 is the source the conserved-germanium program follows: “under a pure SiO2 oxide every silicon atom in the oxide came out of the alloy and every germanium atom stayed”, with the paper's N_ox = 2.21 × 10²² cm⁻³ and its fit of the alloy density. It is also the only paper we hold with germanium depth profiles of dry-oxidized SiGe at five temperatures and three times, and its central claim (at a given oxide thickness the profile does not depend on temperature from 800 to 1000 °C) is a direct test of a program in which the oxide thickness alone fixes the germanium dose.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Moderate.** Two wafers (80 nm Si0.85Ge0.15 and 70 nm Si0.80Ge0.20 on a 100 nm Si buffer), one gas (dry O2, 1 atm), temperature and time at temperature for every profile, and the oxide thickness printed in every legend. A simulation must supply the furnace push and pull, the starting surface (a native oxide that holds some GeO2) and the RBS depth resolution. The germanium profiles exist only as graphs; Figure 5 is raw counts.

### What the second reading changed

- SOURCE.md and ontology/library.json describe the paper as “photoemission depth profiles at two temperatures (Figures 1 to 3)”. The profiles that carry the paper are RBS (Figures 5-7) at five temperatures; XPS (Figures 1-3) shows chemistry at two.
- The inventory program's comment says it follows Long p. 6. Long's Eq. 7 counts the whole germanium in a box-shaped pile-up of uniform X_pileup; the program puts the same dose, x N_ox X/(1−X), as the excess above X in an exponential. The two count different germanium (see the reading).
- The inventory program cites N_ox and the alloy density at “p. 6” and “p. 7”; both are on article page 024308-6, PDF p. 7.
- No dataset holds this paper; library.json lists it as held, unused. Every number here is graph-read or a printed legend.
- The native oxides (51 and 55 Å) are part of every measured oxide thickness; both SiGe programs start from 1 nm.

## Their terms and ours

| The paper says | We write | Kind in our vocabulary | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| pile-up / snow-plow / germanium-rich layer (GRL) | `Ge_at_interface; Enriched_layer_efold_width; Ge_at_10nm` | observables (target: composition_profile) | Long: “poorly defined in the literature and can only be described reliably as the tendency to increase the Ge concentration in a region adjacent to the oxidation front” (PDF p. 6). |
| t_pileup (Eq. 7: a box of uniform X_pileup) | `Enriched_layer_efold_width (an exponential)` | observable, different shape | A box of height X_p holding the dose has the thickness of an exponential's e-folding depth only if they count the same germanium; see the reading. |
| X_pileup | `Ge_at_interface` | observable | The program's interface value; RBS reports a smoothed peak about 10 nm below the front. |
| N_ox = 2.21e22 Si atoms/cm³ | `PARAMETER N_ox (sourced)` | parameter | Taken from this paper by the inventory program. |
| N_SiGe = (5.0214 − 0.5286X − 0.0517X²)e22 | `PARAMETERs N_a, N_b, N_c (sourced)` | parameter | Taken from this paper (after Dismukes 1964). |
| Q_sub: silicon dose from the substrate | `(none)` | not modelled | The inventory program excludes it explicitly. |
| tube furnace at ambient pressure flushed with dry O2 | `AMBIENT dry_O2; RUN RampUp / Oxidation / RampDown` | ambient and RUNs | The push and pull are ours. |
| native oxide not removed | `STARTING_OXIDE_NM; CLEAN` | sample fields | Not read by any SiGe program. |
| RBS depth profile (2 MeV He, 100° detector) | `Ge_at_10nm, Ge_at_30nm` | observable | No observation model of RBS depth resolution is written; profiles are compared raw and after a 4 nm Gaussian. |
| Massoud model with Si constants (the paper's own simulation) | `(none)` | not used | Our dry SiGe growth is Rabie's wet program or Deal and Grove's dry silicon law. |

The model itself, `experiments/sige_inventory/conserved_germanium.isl.template`, as it is written. Every equation the simulation uses is in this text; the runtime holds no physics.

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: GERMANIUM CONSERVED UNDER A GROWING OXIDE ON A SiGe ALLOY
#
# Under a pure SiO2 oxide every silicon atom in the oxide came out of the alloy and every germanium atom stayed
# (Long et al., J. Appl. Phys. 111, 024308 (2012), p. 6). The germanium released by an oxide of thickness x on an
# alloy of fraction X is therefore
#     dose = x N_ox X / (1 - X)        atoms per cm^2,
# and it sits in a layer under the oxide. The layer is taken to fall off exponentially with depth from its peak value
# ge_i at the interface:  X(z) = X + (ge_i - X) exp(-z / width), so its dose is N (ge_i - X) width, which fixes the width.
#
# What sets the peak ge_i is not derived here. Where the dose is large it is the relation fitted to Mueller 2023
# (a floor plus a share of the starting fraction; experiments/sige_interface); where the dose is small it is limited by
# what the dose can fill in a layer no thinner than min_width. So a start of zero germanium gives zero germanium everywhere.
#
# Oxide growth is the silicon law, with the alloy's effect not modelled: dry from Deal and Grove 1965, steam and wet
# oxygen from the oxidant solubility and diffusivity of Rabie et al. 2005. Neither is a SiGe growth law (the transport
# program of Rabie runs 6 to 8 times slow away from its one case), so thickness here is an assumption, and the width and
# the dose scale with it.
# Not included: germanium lost as GeO2, silicon supplied from the substrate, oxidation of the layer itself (all
# of which matter once the alloy is consumed), and the effect of strain.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_sige_inventory.py.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE {sample_id} {{
    FAMILY: SiGe_oxide
    COMPOSITION: {composition}
}}
STATE x_ox {{ UNIT: cm; INITIAL: x_native; MIN: 0; MAX: 1.0e-3 }}
STATE ge_i {{ UNIT: 1; INITIAL: ge_start; MIN: 0; MAX: 1 }}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_O2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
CONTROL p_H2O {{ UNIT: atm }}
CONTROL p_N2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
CONTROL p_Ar {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER ge_start {{ VALUE: {ge_start}; UNIT: 1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: the sample's stated composition }}
# ---- conservation (sourced) ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PARAMETER N_ox {{ VALUE: 2.21e22; UNIT: cm^-3; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Long et al. 2012, p. 6, silicon atoms per cm3 of oxide, agent-read }}
PARAMETER N_a {{ VALUE: 5.0214e22; UNIT: cm^-3; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Long et al. 2012, p. 7, alloy atomic density after Dismukes, constant term, agent-read }}
PARAMETER N_b {{ VALUE: 0.5286e22; UNIT: cm^-3; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Long et al. 2012, p. 7, linear term in the germanium fraction, agent-read }}
```

What alignment cannot fix: the oxide thickness, and through it the germanium. This is case (d): programs exist and miss, and no history unknown can repair them. (1) **No dry SiGe growth law.** The predictor's SiGe oxide comes from the Rabie program, whose constants were chosen for wet oxygen at 800 °C and which does not read the gas: it gives 30, 65 and 134 nm at 900 °C for 20.4, 35.4 and 59.0 nm measured, 197 nm at 800 °C (4 h) for 40.8 nm, and too little at 950 and 1000 °C (30 and 26 nm for 39 and 38 nm). The inventory program grows its own oxide by Deal and Grove's dry silicon law: 8.1, 18.5 and 37.1 nm at 900 °C, 11 nm at 800 °C, 7.8 nm at 850 °C. Long's dry SiGe oxide is 1.5 to 4 times thicker than that silicon law gives, from a 5 nm native oxide. On one history the two programs disagree by a factor of 3.6 (134 against 37 nm at 150 min), and the measured value lies between them.

(2) **The germanium follows the wrong oxide.** Because the inventory width is the dose over the peak excess and the dose is proportional to the oxide, the 10 nm values at 800 and 850 °C (0.16 and 0.15 against 0.24 and 0.235) are low because the program grew 11 and 8 nm of oxide where 41 and 32 nm grew. Through its own growth law it predicts a spread of 0.152 to 0.216 at 10 nm across Figure 7 where Long measures 0.215 to 0.245.

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **One oxide per history, from a growth law for the gas.** Two programs grow 134 and 37 nm on the same 150 min history (59 nm measured); the inventory's germanium dose follows its own oxide. The predictor should take one oxide thickness (or a stated one: `MEASURED_OXIDE_NM` as an input, as done for the nanowire program's RUN_FLAT_OXIDE_NM) and feed it to every composition program. With the stated oxide the inventory program's width is a prediction of Long's profiles with nothing grown.
- **A dry-oxygen SiGe growth program, or a refusal.** The Rabie program runs dry histories at its wet 800 °C constants and labels them “direction only”. Long gives eleven dry oxide thicknesses at 800 to 1000 °C on two compositions; they would fit or test one.
- **STARTING_OXIDE_NM read by the SiGe programs.** The native oxide is 5 nm here (a quarter of the 25 min oxide), and it carries GeO2 (XPS). Neither SiGe program reads it.
- **Layered sample with a finite alloy.** `LAYER SiGe { COMPOSITION: Si0.85Ge0.15; THICKNESS: 80 nm } on LAYER Si { THICKNESS: 100 nm }`: needed to say when the pile-up consumes the layer (Long's Figure 6 at 150 min, and his Eq. 8).
- **An observation model for the instrument's depth resolution.** RBS and XPS report a profile smoothed by about 5 nm (estimated here from the un-oxidized edge) and 3.5 or 11.7 nm (Ge2p, Ge3d). A statement such as `OBSERVATION_MODEL ... RESOLUTION gaussian 5 nm` would let a sharp predicted profile be compared with a measured one without the hand-written smoothing in this study file.
- **A depth profile as a target.** Results are scalars at chosen depths (10 nm, 30 nm). Long's data are profiles with the front aligned at zero by the authors; a profile target (depth series, with the front position as an unknown) would score the whole shape.
- **The conserved dose in the inventory program.** Its comment follows Long's Eq. 7 (the germanium of the consumed alloy, x N_ox X/(1−X)) but places that dose as the excess above X in an exponential. Counting atoms with the alloy shrinking by the silicon removed gives an excess of x N_ox X, as scripts/analyse_germanium_inventory.py already uses. The program's width is 1/(1−X) too large: 18% at X = 0.15, 100% at X = 0.5. A predictor change, not a language one; it is listed here because this paper is its source.
- **A clean word for “native oxide not removed”.** The CLEAN vocabulary has no entry for an uncleaned surface; not_stated is wrong here, because the paper does state it.

## What the reproduction shows

**The measured profiles against the two programs** (`PYTHONPATH=. python -m reproductions.long_2012`; numbers as that function prints them, with the push and pull as written above). The RBS depth resolution is not stated. Fitting the un-oxidized sample's edge at the front (Figure 6) as a step seen through a Gaussian gives σ = 5.0 nm. Both programs' profiles were smoothed with it before comparison; Long's depth zero is his own alignment of the oxidation front.

**Germanium is conserved in the samples.** The area under the un-oxidized and the 150 min profiles of Figure 6 is 9.57 and 9.47 nm (germanium fraction × depth): 1% apart, inside the graph reading. This is the premise of the inventory program and Long's “little or no diffusion of Ge into the substrate”, and it holds.

**At 900 °C, with each program's own oxide.** At 10 nm below the front, after smoothing: 25 min, measured 0.180, Rabie 0.198, inventory 0.163; 67 min, 0.215, 0.239, 0.202; 150 min, 0.330, 0.280, 0.261. The root-mean-square difference over 5 to 30 nm is 0.017, 0.036, 0.052 for Rabie and 0.010, 0.023, 0.052 for the inventory, against 0.017, 0.034, 0.115 for no redistribution at all (a flat 0.15). So at 25 and 67 min the measured enrichment is small enough that a flat profile does about as well as either program; at 150 min both programs capture half of the departure from flat. Both programs put the peak at the front (0.53 and 0.45 before smoothing, 0.31 and 0.28 after) where Long's profile peaks at 0.33 about 10 nm below it, and both are narrower: the measured germanium stays above 0.165 from about 3 to 28 nm below the front, while the inventory's e-folding depth is 9.8 nm and Rabie's 9.5 nm.

**The inventory width, given Long's oxide instead of its own.** With the thermal part of the 150 min oxide (59.0 − 5.1 = 53.9 nm) the program's dose x N_ox X/(1−X) is 4.25 nm of excess germanium and its peak 0.448, so its e-folding depth would be 14.3 nm (3 e-folds 43 nm). The exact atom balance (the alloy loses only the oxidized silicon) needs 3.62 nm of excess, giving 12.1 nm. Long's own Eq. 7 box (X_pileup = 0.33) is 13.2 nm. The measured excess above 0.15 in the top 40 nm, read from the smoothed profile, is 2.97 nm; the rest is spread across the front by the resolution and into the depleted bottom of the finite layer. With the measured oxide the inventory program's width would be within 20% of what Long's data and Long's own relation imply; with its own oxide it is 9.8 nm at 150 min (20 to 30% narrower), and at 800 and 850 °C (2.9 and 2.1 nm, against 8.0 and 5.9 nm from the exact balance with the measured oxide) about 3 times too narrow.

**How faithfully the inventory program follows Long.** It takes N_ox (2.21e22 cm⁻³) and the alloy density fit exactly as printed (PDF p. 7) and the premise that the thermal oxide holds no germanium (Long's XPS). It departs in four places: (1) the dose it places as excess is Long's total germanium of the consumed alloy, which over-counts by 1/(1−X); (2) the shape is an exponential from a peak at the front, where Long's Eq. 7 is a uniform box and his Figure 6 shows a peak about 10 nm in; (3) the peak value is the relation fitted on Mueller's wet oxidations (0.45 for a 15% alloy), where Long measures “roughly double” (XPS) and 0.18 to 0.33 by RBS, and computes in Figs. 9-11 a peak set by the balance of oxidation and silicon diffusion, which the program does not have; (4) growth is Deal and Grove's dry silicon law, where Long's paper uses Massoud's silicon model for its simulations and its measured SiGe oxides are 1.5 to 4 times thicker than the program's silicon law gives.
