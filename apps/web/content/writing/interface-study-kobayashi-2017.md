## The study and why we depend on it

**T. Kobayashi, J. Suda and T. Kimoto, “Reduction of interface state density in SiC (0001) MOS structures by post-oxidation Ar annealing at high temperature”, AIP Advances 7, 045008 (2017).**

The only electrical data we hold on SiC: 27 values of interface state density at three energies, taken from the vector drawing of its Figures 2 and 3. The SiC interface-state program (a one-way reduction set by the argon anneal temperature) was fitted to these and nothing else, so this page is a check on our reading, not a test of the model.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Low.** The process is given as ranges for the whole sample set: the argon anneal is “1 - 10 min”, the 1300 °C oxidation “40 min or 1h 20 min”, the cooling rates are bounds, and what happens between the two furnaces is not described. Agreement here is with a program fitted to these same points.

| | |
|---|---|
| Specimen described | 2 of 4 properties we need are stated and encoded the same |
| History described | 2 of 4 steps are stated and encoded the same; 1 are stated by the paper and absent from our history; 1 are ours because the paper is silent |
| Timing | 1 of 1 simulated steps have a stated duration; 3 of 3 other steps have none; 6 timing gaps listed below |
| How the results are given | Only in figures; taken from the vector drawing in the PDF, so exact to the drawing |
| Repeatability stated by the authors | Not stated |
| Internal consistency | Fair: the Figure 1 caption says the anneal temperature was varied between 1300 and 1500 °C, the text says either one or the other. The paper's statement that cooling rate does not matter is not borne out by its own Figure 2 |
| Is our model independent of this paper's data? | No. The program was fitted to these 27 values and to nothing else |
| Outcome | 21 of 21 numbers within tolerance |

The grade is ours and is about the paper as a source for simulation, not about the quality of the experiment: **high** means the specimen, every step and its timing are stated well enough that a simulation needs no choices of ours; **moderate** that some choices are ours and are listed; **low** that the result depends on choices the paper does not constrain.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- The argon anneal time for each sample: only “1 - 10 min” for the whole set.
- Which 1300 °C oxidation time (40 min or 1 h 20 min) each sample had.
- Everything between the oxidation furnace and the anneal furnace: cooling, unloading, time in air, reloading and the ramp to 1300 or 1500 °C.
- The cooling rates are bounds (“< 70”, “> 600” °C/min), with no temperature interval and no gas. The two rates are also two different furnaces.
- Whether the twice-annealed sample was unloaded or measured between its two anneals.
- The temperature of the C-V measurement.

### What the second reading changed

- Oxidation and argon anneal were done in different furnaces, so each annealed sample was cooled, unloaded and reheated in between. Our stored history runs the anneal straight after the oxidation.
- Five minutes of argon and forty minutes of oxidation at 1300 °C are our choices from the ranges the paper gives (1 to 10 min; 40 min or 1 h 20 min). The stored records present them as the recipe.
- Cooling rate is tied to furnace type: the slow rate is the resistive furnace (oxygen measured under 1 ppm), the fast rate the induction furnace (oxygen not reported). A difference between cooling rates is also a difference between furnaces.
- The substrate is 4° off-axis; the growth data we use for SiC (Song 2004) are on-axis. The specimen records did not distinguish them.

The model itself, `experiments/sic_interface_states/one_way_anneal.isl.template`, as it is written. Every equation the simulation uses is in this text; the runtime holds no physics.

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: INTERFACE STATES OF SiC/SiO2 THROUGH ARGON ANNEALS
#
# Two parts. One is stable. The other is removable: a hold in argon takes it down to a level
# set by the anneal temperature, and it does not come back at a lower temperature. The density
# at each energy below the conduction band is the stable part plus the removable part, each
# with its own weight at that energy.
#
# The form was chosen among three (decay to nothing, a reversible equilibrium, a one-way level)
# by scripts/run_sic_interface_states.py, on the samples oxidized at 1150 C. How fast the level
# is reached is not resolved by any source we hold (the anneals are "1 to 10 min"), so the rate
# is a hypothesis: fast enough to finish within a minute.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by the runner.
# ==============================================================================
SPECIMEN {specimen_id} {{
    FAMILY: SiC_SiO2
    ORIENTATION: 4H_SiC_Si_face
    CLEAN: not_stated
}}
STATE removable {{ UNIT: 1; INITIAL: 1; MIN: 0; MAX: 1 }}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_Ar {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER log_level {{ VALUE: -0.3; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_level {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_S_0p3 {{ VALUE: 11.8; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_S_0p4 {{ VALUE: 11.5; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_S_0p5 {{ VALUE: 11.2; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_R_0p3 {{ VALUE: 12.4; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_R_0p4 {{ VALUE: 12.3; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_R_0p5 {{ VALUE: 12.1; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER settle_rate {{ VALUE: 0.1; UNIT: s^-1; STATUS: hypothesis }}
PARAMETER way {{ VALUE: {way}; UNIT: 1; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER soft {{ VALUE: 1.0e-3; UNIT: 1; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER T_ref {{ VALUE: 1573.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER D_unit {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: cm^-2 eV^-1; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER ln10 {{ VALUE: 2.302585092994046; UNIT: 1; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
# The level the anneal temperature allows, as a fraction of the as-oxidized removable part.
QUANTITY raw_level {{ UNIT: 1; EQUATION: raw_level = exp(ln10 * log_level) * exp((E_level / k_B) * (1 / T - 1 / T_ref)) }}
# Never more than the as-oxidized amount.
QUANTITY level {{ UNIT: 1; EQUATION: level = (raw_level + 1 - sqrt((raw_level - 1) * (raw_level - 1) + soft * soft)) / 2 }}
# way = 1: one-way (acts only while above the level). way = 0: reversible (moves to the level from either side).
QUANTITY above {{ UNIT: 1; EQUATION: above = (1 - way) + way * (1 + (removable - level) / sqrt((removable - level) * (removable - level) + soft * soft)) / 2 }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY {trajectory_id} {{
{runs}
}}
OPERATOR F_ArgonAnneal : Relaxation {{
    EQUATION: d(removable)/dt = -settle_rate * (p_Ar / p_ref) * above * (removable - {target})
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_0p3 {{ TARGET: Dit_at_0p3eV_below_Ec; UNIT: cm^-2 eV^-1; EQUATION: Dit_at_0p3eV_below_Ec = D_unit * (exp(ln10 * log_S_0p3) + removable * exp(ln10 * log_R_0p3)) }}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_0p4 {{ TARGET: Dit_at_0p4eV_below_Ec; UNIT: cm^-2 eV^-1; EQUATION: Dit_at_0p4eV_below_Ec = D_unit * (exp(ln10 * log_S_0p4) + removable * exp(ln10 * log_R_0p4)) }}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_0p5 {{ TARGET: Dit_at_0p5eV_below_Ec; UNIT: cm^-2 eV^-1; EQUATION: Dit_at_0p5eV_below_Ec = D_unit * (exp(ln10 * log_S_0p5) + removable * exp(ln10 * log_R_0p5)) }}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT {contract_id} {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/kobayashi_2017_sic_dit.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{dataset_sha256}
    SPLIT: {split}
{targets}
}}
```

## Aligning the simulation: what the unstated things would have to be

Every step whose timing or temperature the paper leaves open carries a range in the history above (`VARY`). `scripts/align_study.py` searched those ranges against the paper's numbers through the same predictor, first one unknown at a time, then all together. No parameter a program declares `fitted` was touched. The unknowns were chosen on ox1150C, ox1150C_Ar1300C_cool600, ox1150C_Ar1500C_cool600, ox1150C_Ar1500C_cool70 only; every other number is a test they played no part in.

Exponential cooling at the bound rate and a furnace already hot at loading are variants.

**Read this as an upper bound.** The unknowns were chosen on the four samples oxidized at 1150 °C (12 values), the same samples the program's eight fitted numbers were fitted on (`evaluations/sic_interface_states_report.json`). The three samples oxidized at 1300 °C (9 values) were not used in that fit or here, and are the test; they carry the same argon anneals at 1500 °C, so they test the oxidation temperature, which the program does not read.

**Nothing in the history moves the numbers.** On the chosen samples every unknown is flat: 12 of 12 within tolerance at every value searched, the median error between 4.2% and 4.5%. The anneal time does not matter between 1 and 10 min because the program's settling rate (0.1 s^-1, a hypothesis, not a fit) finishes the reduction in under a minute. The oxidation time and the cooling rates are not read: the as-oxidized level is a constant, and the reduction is one-way, so a slower cooling only lets it go on a little longer (median 4.5% at 600 s, 4.2% at 2400 s for the standard cooling). The combined search, which breaks ties on median error, settles on a 1 min anneal and a standard cooling of 35 min (20 °C/min) with nothing else changed. Aligned: 21 of 21, median error 4.6% (4.7% as reproduced); on the 1300 °C samples 9 of 9 both ways, median 4.7% to 4.6%. **The search found a fault in the program instead.** With a ramp of 9.7 min or longer from room temperature in argon, the predictor refuses every annealed sample: the state `removable` reaches 1.00 and is reported as outside its bounds [0, 1]. At low temperature the program's level is softly capped just below 1, and a long time there lets the solver step over it. At 9.7 min 9 of the 12 chosen values are still simulated, at 17 and 30 min only the 3 as-oxidized ones. The search did not choose those values only because it counts values within tolerance, not values refused; it would have been wrong for it to choose them on median error.

This paper cannot discriminate between histories, so alignment cannot test the program here. What the data do show and the program does not have is the cooling rate: at 0.4 and 0.5 eV the 1500 °C samples differ by 18 and 32% between standard and rapid cooling (4.25 against 3.60, 2.19 against 1.66 × 10^11 cm^-2 eV^-1), more states after the slow cooling; after argon at 1300 °C the slow-cooled sample (not among the cases here) has fewer (1.18 against 1.65 × 10^12 at 0.4 eV). ### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- A cooling rate as a statement (`COOL RATE <K/min> TO <T> [BOUND upper|lower]`), so “< 70 °C/min” is written as the paper gives it rather than as an invented end temperature and duration. The rate also needs its own furnace, so a cooling difference is not silently also a furnace difference.
- A furnace or equipment field on a RUN (`FURNACE resistive | rf_induction`), and the residual oxygen it is known for (under 1 ppm for one, not reported for the other). Here the two cooling rates are two furnaces.
- A transfer through air as a RUN-like step with its own gas and time (`TRANSFER AMBIENT air DURATION …`): the Transfer here is an EVENT, so any exposure of the interface to room air between furnaces cannot reach a program.
- OFFCUT read by programs (4° off-axis here, on-axis in Song 2004), and the measurement method on an observable (1 MHz high-low counts fewer states than a 100 MHz reference, the paper's Figure 4).
- For the predictor: a small tolerance on a state's declared bounds, or a hard clamp, so that a long low-temperature hold does not refuse a history at exactly 1.00.
