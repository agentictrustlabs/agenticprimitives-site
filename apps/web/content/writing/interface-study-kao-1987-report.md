## The study and why we depend on it

**D.-B. Kao, J. P. McVittie, W. D. Nix and K. C. Saraswat, “Two-Dimensional Thermal Oxidation of Silicon—I. Experiments”, Stanford Integrated Circuits Laboratory report, DTIC ADA181721 (DARPA MDA903-79-C-0257); published as IEEE Trans. Electron Devices ED-34, 1008–1017 (1987).**

The measurements behind Kao 1988 (the challenge's curved-silicon reference). The wet cylinders and holes are scored on the kao-1988 page. This page scores what is left: the ten flat oxides of Table 1 (steam at 800 to 1200 °C on (100) and on (110) sidewalls), which test the planar wet program below its 920 °C range; the dry-oxygen cylinders and holes (Figure 10), for which no curved program exists; and the phosphorus-doped cylinders at 900 °C (Figure 11), whose difference from undoped ones Kao puts in the oxide's viscosity.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Low.** The report describes the fabrication in detail (mask, etch chemistry and rate, RCA clean with times, polysilicon cap, lapping, BHF and gold for the SEM) and prints the wet times and flat oxides (Table 1). It does not state the dry oxidation times, the not-deglazed doping time, the steam source, the furnace cycle, or the etched radii. Every curved-surface number is in a figure, normalized to a flat oxide given only as “nominally” 3500 or 5000 Å, with points not labelled by temperature.

| | |
|---|---|
| Sample described | 0 of 4 properties we need are stated and encoded the same |
| History described | 2 of 7 steps are stated and encoded the same; 1 are stated by the paper and absent from our history; 2 are ours because the paper is silent |
| Timing | 1 of 3 simulated steps have a stated duration; 1 of 2 other steps have none; 6 timing gaps listed below |
| How the results are given | Table 1 printed (wet times, flat (100) oxide, (110)/(100) ratio); Figures 7, 8, 10, 11 (normalized oxide against 1/r), read from the scan; the least-squares lines are read where points cannot be told apart |
| Repeatability stated by the authors | Error bars on convex points (about ±0.03 to ±0.06 of the flat oxide); none on concave points or on Table 1 |
| Internal consistency | Moderate. Table 1 and section 6.4 agree (900 °C, 300 min = 5 h). The normalization is said to be to flat (100) but the curves tend to 1.0 although flat (110) is 1.03 to 1.29 times flat (100); the etch gas is printed two ways |
| Is our model independent of this paper's data? | Yes. The planar wet program uses Deal and Grove's 1965 constants and an orientation factor (110)/(100) = 1.45 that matches Kao's own fitted value (Eq. 2), but nothing was fitted to these numbers. No curved-surface program was fitted to Kao |
| Outcome | 3 of 18 numbers within tolerance |

The grade is ours and is about the paper as a source for simulation, not about the quality of the experiment: **high** means the sample, every step and its timing are stated well enough that a simulation needs no choices of ours; **moderate** that some choices are ours and are listed; **low** that the result depends on choices the paper does not constrain.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- Dry oxidation times: “long enough to grow nominally 3500 A”; not stated. The assumed times are what the planar dry program needs (65 h at 900 °C to 2.9 h at 1200 °C).
- Not-deglazed doped group: oxidation time not stated.
- Furnace cycle for every oxidation: load temperature, push and pull, gas during heat-up and cool-down.
- Steam source and pressure.
- Time from the HF dip to the furnace; the POCl3 deposition's own push and pull.
- The 620 °C, 90 min polysilicon cap after every oxidation is stated and recorded, not simulated.

### What the second reading changed

- The project held Table 1 in the history bundle's EXTRACTED_DATA.json (marked “transcribed and visually checked”, not checked by us); it agrees with the second reading digit for digit. No dataset in data/derived uses it, and the figures had not been digitized.
- The planar wet program's constants are Deal and Grove's at 640 Torr of water from 920 to 1200 °C. Kao's 800 and 900 °C runs are outside that range, and the steam source is not stated: pure steam at 1 atm is 19% faster than the bubbler the constants were measured with.
- The radial program's rate is proportional to the water pressure. A dry-oxygen history runs and grows nothing, so the dry cylinders are “predicted” at about zero oxide. The planar dry program exists (Deal and Grove dry, and the thin-oxide program) but has no curved geometry.
- The dry flat oxide is “nominally 3500 A”; no measured flat value is printed for any dry run, so the absolute curved oxide carries the uncertainty of “nominally”.
- No program reads DOPING or a surface glass. Kao's explanation, that phosphorus raises the growth rate and lowers the oxide's viscosity, needs both in a stress-form program.

## Their terms and ours

| The paper says | We write | Kind in our vocabulary | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| wet planar oxidation, steam, H2O | `AMBIENT steam (or mix:H2O=0.842,O2=0.158 for a 95 °C bubbler)` | ambient by partial pressure | Steam source not stated. |
| t_ox(110)/t_ox(100) | `(none)` | a ratio of two samples' thicknesses | Scored as the (110) thickness = ratio × (100) value. |
| k_s(110) = 1.45 × k_s(100) (Eq. 2) | `r_or(110)/r_or(100) = 1.45` | PARAMETER of the planar program (sourced from the dry-oxidation orientation table) | The same number from a different source. |
| dry O2 | `AMBIENT dry_O2` | ambient | The radial program grows nothing in it. |
| heavily doped, POCl3, deglazed / not deglazed | `DOPING; PSG_NM` | sample fields no program reads | Kao: doping raises the growth rate and lowers the viscosity. |
| phosphosilicate glass retained during oxidation | `(none)` | a starting layer of another material | STARTING_OXIDE_NM is for SiO2; a doped glass has no field. |
| normalized oxide thickness | `(none)` | a ratio | Scored as the absolute oxide: normalized × the nominal flat oxide. |
| radius of curvature after oxidation | `RADIUS_AFTER_NM` | sample field (recorded) |  |

# ISL MODEL: DRY THERMAL OXIDATION OF SILICON (growth sector)
#
# Linear-parabolic (Deal-Grove) growth written as one ISL operator. Nothing is
# fitted to the test data: every parameter keeps the value declared here.
#
# Provenance of the constants: the rate constants are an Arrhenius fit to Table II of
# Deal and Grove (1965), which is for (111) silicon; see scripts/derive_deal_grove_constants.py.
# The orientation factor for other faces is not in that paper and remains a hypothesis.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_vitkavage_growth.py.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE {sample_id} {{
    FAMILY: Si_Planar
    ORIENTATION: Si{orientation}
}}
# Oxide thickness. The initial value is Deal and Grove's effective starting thickness
# for dry oxygen, which stands in for the fast initial regime their model does not describe.
STATE x_ox {{
    UNIT: cm
    INITIAL: x_i
    MIN: 0
    MAX: 1.0e-3
}}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_O2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
# Parabolic rate constant B = B0 * exp(-E_B / kT).
PARAMETER B0 {{ VALUE: 3.6053e-09; UNIT: cm^2 s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
PARAMETER E_B {{ VALUE: 1.2860; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
# Linear rate constant B/A on (111) = BA0 * exp(-E_BA / kT).
PARAMETER BA0 {{ VALUE: 2.4265e-01; UNIT: cm s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
PARAMETER E_BA {{ VALUE: 2.0303; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
# Orientation factor on the linear rate, relative to (111).
PARAMETER r_or {{ VALUE: {r_or}; UNIT: 1; STATUS: hypothesis }}
# Effective initial thickness for dry oxygen.
PARAMETER x_i {{ VALUE: 2.3e-6; UNIT: cm; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), stated intercept 230 +/- 30 angstrom for dry oxygen, agent-read }}
# Both rate constants are proportional to oxidant pressure; the constants above are for 1 atm.
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY {trajectory_id} {{
    RUN Oxidation DURATION {duration_s} TEMP {T_C}->{T_C} AMBIENT dry_O2
}}
# dx/dt = B / (2x + A), with A = B / (B/A).
OPERATOR F_LinearParabolicGrowth : OxideGrowth {{
    EQUATION: d(x_ox)/dt = (p_O2 / p_ref) * B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (2 * x_ox + B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (r_or * BA0 * exp(-E_BA / (k_B * T))))
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Oxide_Thickness {{
    TARGET: Thickness
    UNIT: cm
    EQUATION: Thickness = x_ox
}}

OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Consumed_Silicon {{
    TARGET: Consumed_substrate
    UNIT: cm
    EQUATION: Consumed_substrate = 0.44 * (x_ox - x_i)
}}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT {contract_id} {{
    FROZEN: true

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **A dry channel in the curved-silicon program.** Kao's dry cylinders (Figure 10) cannot be predicted at all: the radial program's rate is proportional to the water pressure. The planar dry programs exist; the cylindrical law needs a dry B and B/A with an oxygen-pressure term, and RUN_FLAT_OXIDE_NM should give the dry rate as it gives the wet one.
- **The gas of a step as an unknown.** Push and pull in nitrogen are invisible to every program; in steam or oxygen they would not be. The language can VARY a duration or a temperature but not the ambient; `VARY ambient inert_N2|dry_O2|steam` would let an unstated push gas be searched.
- **A steam source on the ambient.** “Steam” in Kao, Deal and Grove and Müller is three different water pressures (bubbler 640 Torr, pyrogenic with leftover oxygen, unstated). An ambient word like `steam_bubbler(95C)` or `steam_pyrogenic(H2:O2)` that resolves to a partial pressure would make the choice explicit; here it moves the planar score from 3 to 5 of 18. And a program whose rate comes from RUN_FLAT_OXIDE_NM should not also scale by p_H2O, or a stated mixture counts the water twice (the bubbler variant drops the doped cylinders by 12%).
- **Doping and a glass on the surface as program inputs.** DOPING and PSG_NM are recorded on the sample and read by nothing. Kao's doped groups differ by a factor of two in retardation at the same flat oxide; a stress-form program with a viscosity that depends on the phosphorus in the oxide, and a starting layer of a named material (not only STARTING_OXIDE_NM for SiO2), would let them be predicted.
- **A trench sidewall as a sample.** The (110) values come from trench sidewalls; the language writes them as a flat (110) wafer. A SHAPE trench with a sidewall orientation would say what was measured.
