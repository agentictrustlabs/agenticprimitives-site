## The study and why we depend on it

**T. Akinwande, C. Ho and J. Plummer, “Si/SiO2 Interface Charges”, section 7 of J. D. Plummer et al., Computer-Aided Design of Integrated Circuit Fabrication Processes for VLSI Devices, final report for 15 February 1981 to 31 March 1982, Stanford Integrated Circuits Laboratory TR-DXG501-82, July 1982, DTIC AD-A121414, printed pages 230 to 241 (PDF pages 238 to 249).**

This is the earlier report the 1983 Stanford report (reproduced as stanford-1982) summarizes in its Figures 1 to 3. It holds the experiments that first showed the argon-annealed charge is not flat in temperature and cycles reversibly, which the fixed-charge program's second equilibrium (in a gas without oxygen) is built on. New to the project here: the (100) half of the 900/1100 °C cycling (the 1983 redrawing keeps only (111)), all trap densities (Figures 7.5 and 7.8), the 1200 °C argon values as the authors explain them, and the proposed rate law Nf − Nfe = K1 (dx/dt)^n exp(EA/kT). The as-grown and argon-annealed charges against temperature were already in the project as the 1983 redrawings; here they are run as histories through the predictor for the first time.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Low.** The section states the orientation and doping type (n-type (111) and (100)), the anneal gas (argon), the anneal and growth temperatures (as figure abscissas), and that the as-grown wafers were “fast pulled”. It states nothing else: no clean, no oxidation gas, time or thickness, no anneal time, no pull time, no electrode, no measurement method. The 1983 report, which redraws three of these figures, adds the oxidation of the cycling wafers (1000 °C, 75 min, dry O2), the argon's water (< 2 ppm) and that the anneal times were chosen to reach equilibrium.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- The oxidation of every wafer: gas, time, thickness (only the growth temperature of the as-grown series is given).
- The pull after oxidation: only “fast pulled”; no time, end temperature or gas.
- The argon anneal time at each temperature in Figures 7.4 and 7.5; the 1983 report says only that it was chosen to reach equilibrium.
- How the anneal wafers went into and out of the argon furnace, and whether the anneal followed the oxidation in the same tube.
- The time of each 900 °C and 1100 °C step of the cycling, and whether the wafers were measured (and so metallized) between steps.
- Electrode, metallization and any anneal after it for the fixed-charge values.

### What the second reading changed

- No stored recipe or dataset holds this report: ontology/library.json lists the source as “held … Not yet read”. Its as-grown and argon-annealed charges are in the project as the 1983 report's redrawings (data/primary_sources/stanford_suprem_report_ada130105/figures_1_2_6_7_agent_read_UNVERIFIED.csv, Figures 1 and 2), read there from a lower-resolution scan.
- The two readings agree. As grown, the 1983 redrawing's (111) values are within 5% of ours at every temperature (e.g. 3.5 against 3.4 × 10^11 at 750 °C, 1.53 against 1.55 at 1200 °C); (100) within 10% except 1100 °C (4.5 against 4.65 × 10^10). Argon-annealed: within 5% for (111); (100) 5.0 against 5.05 at 900 °C, 2.8 against 3.5 × 10^10 at 1100 °C (the two symbols there are 1.5 and 5.5, so the mean depends on the reading).
- The 1983 report's Figure 3 (cycling between 900 and 1100 °C) shows only the (111) wafers. Figure 7.6 here also has (100): 1.64 × 10^11 as grown, then 0.69, 0.76, 0.69 and 0.75 × 10^11: a 58% drop at the first 900 °C step and nothing after. That half is new to the project.
- The 1983 report attributes the cycling to “(111) Si wafers oxidized in dry O2 at 1000° C for 75 minutes … annealed in argon (< 2 ppm H2O)”. This report states neither the oxidation nor the water. We use the 1983 statement for all cycling wafers, both orientations.
- The 1983 report's caption to its Figure 2 adds “The times were adjusted at each temperature to reach an equilibrium value”; this report's Figure 7.4 says nothing about time.
- The stanford-1982 page tests the as-grown value only at 1000 °C (three wafers). Here the as-grown series runs from 750 to 1200 °C (20 conditions) and the fixed-charge program's starting line (Deal 1967) is tested over that range through the predictor.
- This report's text calls the cycling figure “Fig. 7.5” (PDF p. 241); it is Figure 7.6. The lowest decade label of Figure 7.4 is printed 10^12 and must be 10^10.

## Their terms and ours

| The paper says | We write | Kind in our vocabulary | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nf, Qf: fixed oxide charge density | `Nf` | observable (target: fixed_charge); quantity fixed-charge | Read from a C-V curve; method not stated. |
| Dit: midgap interface state (trap) density | `Dit` | observable (target: trap_density); quantity interface-state-density | No hydrogen anneal. |
| as grown (fast pulled) | `RUN Pull DURATION 2 TEMP T->400 AMBIENT dry_O2 TIMING bound` | a RUN; process factor final-anneal | The time is the 1983 report's; this report gives none. |
| Ar anneal; annealed in Argon | `RUN Ar<T> … AMBIENT inert_Ar` | a RUN; process factor inert-anneal | Time not stated. |
| thermal cycling between 900 °C and 1100 °C in Ar | `RUN Cycle900 / Cycle1100 with push and pull, repeated` | RUNs | Whether the wafers left the furnace between steps is not stated. |
| Nfe: annealed value of fixed oxide charge (equilibrium) | `Q_inert(T) = Qi_ref exp(E_q/k_B (1/T − 1/T_iref))` | QUANTITY in the program | The program's falls monotonically with T; the report's has a minimum near 1000 to 1100 °C. |
| Nf − Nfe = K1 (dx/dt)^n exp(EA/kT) | `(none)` | a rate law the program does not contain | The program's as-grown value is Deal's 1967 steady line, a function of temperature only; it has no oxidation rate. |
| Dit = Dit∞ + (Dit0 − Dit∞) exp(−t/τ) | `first-order relaxation (OPERATOR … : Relaxation)` | operator form | Proposed, no values; the same form as the program's charge relaxation. |
| SiO2 viscous above ~965 °C; stress-related charge | `(none)` | a mechanism no program holds |  |
| reaction Si + SiO2 → SiO (gas) at 1200 °C | `(none)` | a mechanism no program holds | Offered for the high 1200 °C argon values. |
| n-type | `DOPING: n_type` | sample field, read by nothing |  |

## The simulation in our language

Each sample is one history file: a `SAMPLE` and a `CONTROL_TRAJECTORY`. This is the whole input; run it with `PYTHONPATH=. python -m isl_harness.predict <file>`.

#### (111) grown at 750 °C, fast pulled

```isl
# (111) grown at 750 °C, fast pulled
SAMPLE Wafer_asgrown_111_750 {
    FAMILY: Si_Planar
    ORIENTATION: Si111
    CLEAN: not_stated
    DOPING: n_type
}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY History {
    EVENT Load KIND load TIMING assumed                          # Clean and load into the oxidation furnace
    RUN Oxidation DURATION 4500 TEMP 750->750 AMBIENT dry_O2 TIMING bound VARY duration 600..36000
    RUN Pull DURATION 2 TEMP 750->400 AMBIENT dry_O2 TIMING bound VARY duration 1..10 VARY T_end 200..700
    EVENT Metal KIND metallization TIMING assumed                # Gate electrode and C-V capacitor
    EVENT CV KIND measurement TIMING assumed                     # C-V: fixed charge and midgap trap density
}
```

The model itself, `experiments/deal_qss/two_equilibria.isl.template`, as it is written. Every equation the simulation uses is in this text; the runtime holds no physics.

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: FIXED CHARGE WITH AN EQUILIBRIUM IN EACH GAS
#
# In oxygen the charge moves toward a steady value that falls with temperature (the
# line of Deal et al. 1967, unchanged, with the oxygen kinetics fitted there).
# In a gas without oxygen it moves toward a lower equilibrium that also falls with
# temperature, Qi_ref * exp(E_q / k_B * (1/T - 1/T_iref)), at a rate of its own.
#
# The inert clock and the (100) inert equilibrium are fitted to the argon time series
# of Akinwande, Ho and Plummer (1982). The (111) inert equilibrium is fitted to their
# cycling experiment, with the clock carried over from (100). The model is then
# tested, unchanged, on the Fairchild wafers of 1967 and 1980.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_fixed_charge_two_lines.py.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE Wafer_{tag} {{
    FAMILY: Si_Planar
    ORIENTATION: Si{orientation}
}}
# The wafer starts at the steady value for its oxidation temperature.
STATE Q_f {{ UNIT: cm^-2; INITIAL: Q_hot + slope * (T_hot - T_ox); MIN: 0; MAX: 1.0e14 }}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_O2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER Q_hot {{ VALUE: {Q_hot}; UNIT: cm^-2; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal et al. 1967 Table II, steady value after dry oxidation at 1200 C }}
PARAMETER slope {{ VALUE: {slope}; UNIT: cm^-2 K^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal et al. 1967 Table II, line through the 1200 C and 920 C steady values }}
PARAMETER T_hot {{ VALUE: 1473.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER T_ox {{ VALUE: {T_ox_K}; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
# ---- fitted to the pull-rate series ------------------------------------------------
PARAMETER k0 {{ VALUE: 1.0e3; UNIT: s^-1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_a {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
# ---- fitted to the argon anneals of Akinwande, Ho and Plummer (1982) -----------------
PARAMETER k_i0 {{ VALUE: 1.0e1; UNIT: s^-1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_i {{ VALUE: 0.9; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER Qi_ref {{ VALUE: 2.5e10; UNIT: cm^-2; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_q {{ VALUE: 0.4; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER T_iref {{ VALUE: 1273.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
# ---- where the exchange with oxygen stops as the wafer cools (hypothesis, added 10 November 2026) ----------------------
# The oxygen rate above was fitted with an activation energy at its lower bound (0.05 eV), so on its own it keeps moving
# charge at room temperature. Every history it was fitted and tested on ended its pull at 400 C, a cut-off that stood in for
# this. Reproducing Stanford 1982, Deal 1967 and Deal and Razouk 1980 with a wafer cooling to room temperature made the charge
# 3 to 5 times too high. The cut-off is now stated: the exchange is switched off over about 20 K around T_freeze. At 400 C it
# is 99% on, so every fitted and tested history is unchanged; how high the true freezing point is, is an open question
# (the Deal and Razouk alignment prefers pulls ending near 530 C).
PARAMETER T_freeze {{ VALUE: 623.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: hypothesis }}
PARAMETER w_freeze {{ VALUE: 10.0; UNIT: K; STATUS: hypothesis }}
QUANTITY rate {{ UNIT: s^-1; EQUATION: rate = k0 * exp(-E_a / (k_B * T)) / (1 + exp(-(T - T_freeze) / w_freeze)) }}
QUANTITY rate_inert {{ UNIT: s^-1; EQUATION: rate_inert = k_i0 * exp(-E_i / (k_B * T)) }}
QUANTITY Q_inert {{ UNIT: cm^-2; EQUATION: Q_inert = Qi_ref * exp((E_q / k_B) * (1 / T - 1 / T_iref)) }}
QUANTITY oxidizing {{ UNIT: 1; EQUATION: oxidizing = p_O2 / p_ref }}
QUANTITY Q_steady {{ UNIT: cm^-2; EQUATION: Q_steady = Q_hot + slope * (T_hot - T) }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY History_{tag} {{
{runs}
}}
# In oxygen the charge follows the temperature-dependent steady value.
OPERATOR F_OxidizingEquilibration : Relaxation {{
    EQUATION: d(Q_f)/dt = -rate * oxidizing * (Q_f - Q_steady)
}}
# Without oxygen it relaxes, at its own rate, toward an equilibrium of its own that falls as temperature rises.
# Akinwande, Ho and Plummer (1982) show the annealed charge cycling reversibly with temperature in argon.
OPERATOR F_InertRelaxation : Relaxation {{
    EQUATION: d(Q_f)/dt = -rate_inert * (1 - oxidizing) * (Q_f - Q_inert)
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Fixed_Charge {{ TARGET: Qss; UNIT: cm^-2; EQUATION: Qss = Q_f }}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT Contract_{tag} {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/deal_1967_qss.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{dataset_sha256}
    SPLIT: {split}
    TARGET: Qss = {target} +/- {tolerance} abs
}}
```

Read this section knowing what was fitted where. The fixed-charge program was fitted to the 1983 report's argon time series at 800 and 1000 °C ((100)) and its (111) cycling between 800 and 1100 °C; nothing on this page was used. The unknowns of the history were chosen on six numbers at the temperatures of that fit (the argon anneals at 800 and 1000 °C, both orientations, and the as-grown wafers at 1000 °C) and tested on the other 57: the as-grown series from 750 to 1200 °C, the argon anneals at 700, 900, 1100 and 1200 °C, the 900/1100 °C cycling and every trap density. The anneal times at 700, 900, 1100 and 1200 °C and the cycling steps carry no chosen sample, so they kept the values written here (18 unknowns not searched).

As reproduced: 4 of 68 within tolerance, median error 59.4%. Aligned (history only): 5 of 68, 58.7%. On the 57 held-out numbers: 3 of 57 before and after, median error 64.0% and 60.6%.

**The history does not matter for the fixed charge.** Every scan of a pull, a push or an anneal time is flat on the fixed charges. The argon anneals are long enough at every temperature that the program sits at its argon equilibrium; the program starts the as-grown wafers at Deal's 1967 steady value for the growth temperature, which no history step before the pull can change. The one scan that moves is the oxidation time (from 2 numbers within at 10 to 20 min to 1 at 75 min and more), through the trap-density program, which reads it; the combined search settled on 39 min. With one or two numbers deciding, that is not evidence about Stanford's oxidation time.

**As grown, 750 to 1200 °C: Deal's line is too high and too flat for Stanford.** Fast-pulled (2 s to 400 °C in oxygen), the program gives 1.25 to 2.0 times the report on (111) and 1.4 to 2.65 times on (100), the ratio growing with temperature on (100): the report's (100) charge falls elevenfold from 750 to 1200 °C (1.63 to 0.14 × 10^11 cm⁻²), the program's sevenfold (2.68 to 0.38). This is the authors' own point that (100) falls faster than (111). No break near 950 °C appears, since the program's starting value is a straight line in temperature.

**Cooling after the pull tests the freeze-out hypothesis, and it fails here.** With every pull written as a cool to 25 °C in the gas the wafer left (PROFILE exponential, TAU 30 s, 5 min), the as-grown charge becomes almost independent of growth temperature: (111) 8.1 × 10^11 at 750 °C and 6.8 at 1200 °C, (100) 3.4 and 2.75, against 3.4 to 1.55 and 1.63 to 0.14 measured. The program's oxygen rate (activation energy 0.05 eV) relaxes the charge toward Deal's line extrapolated down to the 350 °C freeze point during the 10 to 20 s the wafer spends hot, so the growth temperature is forgotten. The same happens with the owner's cooling scenarios after a straight 2 s pull to 400 °C (section below): (100) grown at 1000 °C goes from 1.40 to 2.07 × 10^11 (fast cooling) and 3.64 (slow). Run outside this page with T_freeze raised to 450 °C, those scenarios move the as-grown values by under 2%; with it at 400 °C, by 3 to 36% (fast cooling) and up to 2.7 times (slow cooling, (100) grown at 1200 °C). So this report's as-grown series asks for a freeze-out at or above about 450 °C, consistent with the Deal and Razouk alignment's 530 °C.

**Cooling in nitrogen** (the 1983 report's flushed furnace mouth; no oxygen exchange after the wafer leaves the hot zone) is the best variant: 7 of 68, median error 54.8%, because each as-grown wafer then keeps Deal's value for its growth temperature; (111) at 1150 and 1200 °C and (100) at 1200 °C come within tolerance. Below 1100 °C Deal's line is still 1.3 to 2.0 times high. Neither report states the cooling gas.

**Argon equilibrium against temperature (Figure 7.4).** The program's argon equilibrium falls with temperature with nearly the same energy on both orientations (0.41 eV (111), 0.38 eV (100)). The report's (111) values fall more gently (about 0.3 eV from 700 to 1000 °C: 3.05 to 1.30 × 10^11 against 5.1 to 1.6 simulated) and its (100) values more steeply (about 0.6 eV: 1.9 to 0.36 against 0.83 to 0.28). Above 1000 °C the report is flat on (111) and (100) and rises again at 1200 °C (3.05 × 10^11 (111), 0.48 (100)), where the program keeps falling (1.04, 0.19). What alignment cannot fix. The program's as-grown value is Deal's 1967 line for each orientation, a laboratory property, and Stanford's wafers sit 1.25 to 2.65 times lower and fall more steeply on (100). The authors' own proposal, Nf − Nfe = K1 (dx/dt)^n exp(EA/kT), ties the as-grown charge to the oxidation rate, which the program does not see; on (100) the faster fall with temperature is what such a term would have to supply.

The argon equilibrium has the wrong temperature dependence per orientation (too steep on (111), too shallow on (100)) and no minimum: above 1000 °C the report's values are flat and rise at 1200 °C, which the authors attribute to SiO formation or cooling transients. Neither mechanism is in any program; the argon pull here moves the charge by under 1%, because the program's argon clock (1.8 eV) is slow on cooling.

The (100) cycling between 900 and 1100 °C does not alternate in the report, while the program's equilibrium does by a factor 1.7. A reversible equilibrium per temperature cannot give a value that stays put after the first anneal; the authors read (100) as within noise. That Stanford's 1983 (100) wafers alternated by a factor 5 between 800 and 1100 °C makes this batch to batch, not a model test that can be passed.

The trap-density program cannot be tested on unannealed (111): it has no fitted second population for it. This report's Figures 7.5 and 7.8 are the measurement the program lacks.

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **A freezing point that is stated and testable.** The as-grown series is the cleanest test of where the oxygen exchange stops: ten growth temperatures, one pull. With the stated hypothesis at 350 °C a realistic cool erases the growth temperature; at 450 °C it does not. The ontology could carry the freeze-out temperature of each operator as a declared hypothesis with its own test set (this series and Deal and Razouk's pulls), so that raising it is a recorded decision, not a template edit.
- **The gas a wafer cools in.** A fast pull leaves the oxidation gas for a flushed mouth and then room air. The best variant here (cooling in nitrogen) is a Python edit; a PROFILE or WITHDRAW statement that names the gas after withdrawal would make it a declared unknown (`VARY ambient`).
- **A starting charge from the laboratory.** The program's as-grown value is Deal's Fairchild line. `INITIAL Q_f FROM <laboratory line>` would let Stanford's own as-grown line (this report's Figure 7.7) be the start for Stanford's wafers, and make the Fairchild line one stated choice.
- **A target observed on a fixed time grid, not a history.** The argon anneal times were “adjusted at each temperature to reach an equilibrium value” (1983 report). The language can only write a guessed duration. `RUN … UNTIL steady` (an anneal that lasts until the state stops moving) would state what the authors did and make the anneal time a program output instead of an assumption.
- **One wafer measured between anneals.** The cycling is one sequence with a C-V after each step; it is written as five histories. A MEASURE event inside one history whose readings are the scored values would say it once, and would show that metal must come off between steps.
- **Oxidation rate as a sample-history quantity.** Eq. 7.1 makes the as-grown charge a function of dx/dt at the end of growth. The growth programs compute it; the charge program cannot read another program's output. A cross-program QUANTITY (the oxidation rate at the pull) would let a rate-dependent charge law be written and tested on Figure 7.7.
