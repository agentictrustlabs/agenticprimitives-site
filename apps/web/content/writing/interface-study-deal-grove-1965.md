## The study and why we depend on it

**B. E. Deal and A. S. Grove, “General relationship for the thermal oxidation of silicon”, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965).**

The planar growth program, `experiments/vitkavage_dit/oxide_growth.isl.template`, takes its constants from this paper: B and B/A are a least-squares Arrhenius fit to Table II (`scripts/derive_deal_grove_constants.py`, reading our transcription `data/primary_sources/deal_grove_1965_jap_36_3770/tables_agent_read_UNVERIFIED.csv`), and every oxide starts at the paper's 230 Å intercept. This page checks three things: that our transcription of Table II agrees with a second reading, how far the Arrhenius fit departs from the table at each temperature, and how the program does against the paper's own thickness points, dry at six temperatures and wet at two.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Moderate.** The gas, the temperature and the substrate are stated, and the rate constants are printed. The history around the oxidation is not: no times are tabulated, nothing is said about insertion, time zero, withdrawal or cooling, and the paper does not say whether each point is a fresh slice. The 230 Å intercept for dry oxygen is a model choice the authors make to cover a fast first regime they do not describe, and they give it four ways (200, 210, 230, 250 Å).

| History described | 2 of 6 steps are stated and encoded the same; 2 are stated by the paper and absent from our history; 2 are ours because the paper is silent |
| Timing | 0 of 2 simulated steps have a stated duration; 4 of 4 other steps have none; 6 timing gaps listed below |
| How the results are given | Rate constants in printed Tables I and II; thickness only on log-log and linear figures (Figs. 1, 2, 5, 6), read by an agent, about ±5–10% on the log axes |
| Repeatability stated by the authors | Thickness accuracy ±25 Å below 0.20 μm and ±40 Å above; B ±2%, A ±2% to ±12% (wet), dry “of the same order” |
| Internal consistency | Good. The intercept is given as 200, 210, 230 ± 30 and 250 Å in different places; the abstract claims 700–1300 °C and 0.1–1 atm, which come from other workers' data |
| Is our model independent of this paper's data? | No. The program's constants are an Arrhenius fit to Table II, and Table II is the authors' fit to these same thicknesses. Agreement here checks our transcription, our derivation and the law's form, not a prediction |
| Outcome | 35 of 44 numbers within tolerance |

The grade is ours and is about the paper as a source for simulation, not about the quality of the experiment: **high** means the sample, every step and its timing are stated well enough that a simulation needs no choices of ours; **moderate** that some choices are ours and are listed; **low** that the result depends on choices the paper does not constrain.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- No time is tabulated: every oxidation time on this page is read from a figure.
- Whether each plotted point is a separate slice oxidized once, or one slice taken out, measured and put back. Close pairs of points at one time suggest duplicate slices.
- Insertion: push rate, warm-up of the slice, and whether time zero is insertion into the hot tube or the switch to oxygen.
- The gas during loading, warm-up and withdrawal, and how the slices were cooled.
- The delay between cleaning and oxidation, and between oxidation and measurement.
- Durations and temperatures of the argon-carrier and flow-rate experiments.

### What the second reading changed

- Our transcription of Table II (`tables_agent_read_UNVERIFIED.csv`) and the second reading agree in all 24 cells: none differs.
- The program does not carry the paper's activation energies. It carries a least-squares Arrhenius line through the six Table II rows: E_B = 1.286 eV against the paper's 28.5 kcal/mole (1.236 eV), and E_BA = 2.030 eV against 46.0 kcal/mole (1.995 eV). At the table's own temperatures the line is off the table by up to 15% (B at 1200 °C, B/A at 800 °C); see the table below.
- The 230 Å intercept is used for every oxide, also for wet oxygen, where the paper takes xi = 0. Our language cannot state a starting oxide on the sample, so the wet histories start at 23 nm.
- The paper says B is proportional to the oxidant pressure and A is not. Our operator multiplies the whole rate by p_O2/p_ref, which also scales B/A. For the 1 atm dry oxidations here this changes nothing; for any mixture it does.
- `SOURCE.md` in the source folder still says “The programs have not yet been changed to the sourced values”. The template now carries the derived values with STATUS sourced; the note is out of date.
- The dry oxygen had “water content less than 5ppm”. Our histories write dry_O2, which the language reads as pure oxygen.

## Their terms and ours

| The paper says | We write | Kind in our vocabulary | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| x0: oxide thickness | `Thickness` | observable (target: layer_thickness) |  |
| B: parabolic rate constant; B/A: linear rate constant | `B0, E_B, BA0, E_BA` | PARAMETER, STATUS sourced | Arrhenius fits to Table II, not the paper's own activation energies (28.5 and 46.0 kcal/mole from Figs. 9 and 11). |
| A = 2Deff(1/k + 1/h) | `B / (B/A)` | derived inside the OPERATOR |  |
| xi: initial thickness (230 Å, dry) | `x_i` | PARAMETER, STATUS sourced; INITIAL of the state x_ox | A model choice for the fast first regime. The language has no sample field for a starting oxide. |
| τ = (xi² + A xi)/B | `(implicit)` | follows from x_i | Starting the state at x_i is the same as the paper's time shift τ. |
| purified dry oxygen (water < 5 ppm), 760 Torr | `dry_O2` | ambient | Could be written mix:O2=0.999995,H2O=5e-6; no program reads the water. |
| wet oxygen (O2 bubbled through water at 95 °C), p(H2O) ≈ 640 Torr | `mix:H2O=0.8421,O2=0.1579` | ambient (mixture) | The named ambient wet_O2 has no partial pressures in the language. As a mixture the gas class is steam; the 1965 program reads its 0.158 atm of oxygen and nothing else. |
| B proportional to oxidant partial pressure, A independent of it | `(p_O2 / p_ref) on the rate` | OPERATOR | Our operator scales the whole rate by p_O2, so A is not kept independent of pressure. |
| multiple-beam interferometry | `Thickness` | observable |  |
| C*: solubility of the oxidant in the oxide; N1: oxidant molecules per unit volume of oxide | `(none)` | domain terms, not in the program |  |

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: DRY THERMAL OXIDATION OF SILICON (growth sector)
#
# Linear-parabolic (Deal-Grove) growth written as one ISL operator. Nothing is
# fitted to the test data: every parameter keeps the value declared here.
#
# Provenance of the constants: the rate constants are an Arrhenius fit to Table II of
# Deal and Grove (1965), which is for (111) silicon; see scripts/derive_deal_grove_constants.py.
# The orientation factor for other faces is not in that paper and remains a hypothesis.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_vitkavage_growth.py.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE {sample_id} {{
    FAMILY: Si_Planar
    ORIENTATION: Si{orientation}
}}
# Oxide thickness. The initial value is Deal and Grove's effective starting thickness
# for dry oxygen, which stands in for the fast initial regime their model does not describe.
STATE x_ox {{
    UNIT: cm
    INITIAL: x_i
    MIN: 0
    MAX: 1.0e-3
}}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_O2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
# Parabolic rate constant B = B0 * exp(-E_B / kT).
PARAMETER B0 {{ VALUE: 3.6053e-09; UNIT: cm^2 s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
PARAMETER E_B {{ VALUE: 1.2860; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
# Linear rate constant B/A on (111) = BA0 * exp(-E_BA / kT).
PARAMETER BA0 {{ VALUE: 2.4265e-01; UNIT: cm s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
PARAMETER E_BA {{ VALUE: 2.0303; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
# Orientation factor on the linear rate, relative to (111).
PARAMETER r_or {{ VALUE: {r_or}; UNIT: 1; STATUS: hypothesis }}
# Effective initial thickness for dry oxygen.
PARAMETER x_i {{ VALUE: 2.3e-6; UNIT: cm; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), stated intercept 230 +/- 30 angstrom for dry oxygen, agent-read }}
# Both rate constants are proportional to oxidant pressure; the constants above are for 1 atm.
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY {trajectory_id} {{
    RUN Oxidation DURATION {duration_s} TEMP {T_C}->{T_C} AMBIENT dry_O2
}}
# dx/dt = B / (2x + A), with A = B / (B/A).
OPERATOR F_LinearParabolicGrowth : OxideGrowth {{
    EQUATION: d(x_ox)/dt = (p_O2 / p_ref) * B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (2 * x_ox + B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (r_or * BA0 * exp(-E_BA / (k_B * T))))
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Oxide_Thickness {{
    TARGET: Thickness
    UNIT: cm
    EQUATION: Thickness = x_ox
}}
# Silicon consumed by the oxide grown in this recipe: 0.44 of its thickness, by the ratio of molar volumes.
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Consumed_Silicon {{
    TARGET: Consumed_substrate
    UNIT: cm
    EQUATION: Consumed_substrate = 0.44 * (x_ox - x_i)
}}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT {contract_id} {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/vitkavage_1990_thickness.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{dataset_sha256}
    SPLIT: test
    TARGET: Thickness = {target} +/- {tolerance} rel
}}
```

What alignment cannot fix. The dry misses are the program's constants, not the history: the Arrhenius line through Table II is off the table by up to 15%, and the 1200 °C short-time point and the 800 °C long-time points follow it. Using the table's own B and B/A at each temperature, or the paper's activation energies, is a change to sourced constants, not a history unknown, and was not made here. The 700 °C early points need a starting regime that grows from below 230 Å. Wet oxygen needs a program with water as the oxidant (Table I); none exists.

How to read the scores: the constants come from Table II, which the authors fitted to these thicknesses, so even the held-out cases are not an independent test. They test our transcription (confirmed cell by cell by the second reading), our derivation and the graph reading.

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **A starting oxide on the sample.** The 230 Å is a PARAMETER of the program and applies to every history, wet included, where the paper's own start is zero. A sample field such as `STARTING_OXIDE: 0 nm` (read by the growth programs, with the program's intercept as its default) would let wet and dry slices of one preparation differ, and would let the alignment search it.
- **Water as an oxidant.** `wet_O2` has no partial pressures; written as a mixture the water reaches no program, and the 1965 program grows the 16% oxygen share. A wet growth program (Table I constants, B with 16.3 kcal/mole) reading p_H2O is needed before any wet history means anything.
- **Pressure on B only.** The paper's law has B proportional to the oxidant pressure and A independent of it. Our operator scales the whole rate. A program that reads p_O2 only in B would be the paper's law for mixtures, which Study B (Krzeminski's 10% oxygen ramp) needs.
- **One slice measured several times.** The language cannot say that a curve's points are the same slice taken out, measured and put back (an EVENT unload, measure, reload between RUNs of one sample) or separate slices. The paper does not say which; the language should at least be able to write both.
- **A support label per gas.** The planar growth program's label claims wet and steam steps grow nothing, but a mixture makes it grow the oxygen share. The label should be computed from what the program reads (p_O2) rather than from the gas class.

## What we would like Emily to check

1. Deal and Grove give the dry intercept as 200, 210, 230 ± 30 and 250 Å in different places. Is it right for a growth program to start every dry oxide at 230 Å, or should that number belong to the sample (its starting surface) rather than to the law?
2. Our constants are a least-squares line through Table II, not the authors' own activation energies from Figs. 9 and 11 (which also use other data). Which should a program use?
3. Would the points of Figs. 1 and 2 be separate slices, or one slice re-measured? Does it matter for oxides of this thickness?
4. The paper says A does not depend on pressure. Our program scales the whole rate with oxygen pressure. For mixtures of a few percent oxygen in nitrogen, which is right?
