## The study and why we depend on it

**S. Kar and R. L. Narasimhan, “Characteristics of the Si-SiO2 interface states in thin (70-230 Å) oxide structures”, J. Appl. Phys. 61, 5353 (1987).**

The challenge asks for Dit(E), the interface state density across the band gap. This paper measures it on unannealed (100) oxides 79 to 227 Å thick, from both band edges (n- and p-type), and finds two peaks over a U-shaped background, one 0.23 to 0.44 eV above the valence band and one 0.85 to 0.93 eV above it, whose height first falls and then rises with thickness (minimum near 110 Å, maximum near 155 Å). The project read it and set it aside as “not comparable with midgap values” (SOURCE.md). Figure 3 does reach midgap for two devices, and Figure 1 is a thickness-against-time series at 900 °C that the project has not used. This page runs both through the predictor and sets the measured distributions beside what the predictor's spectrum rows can and cannot say.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Moderate.** The process is short and mostly stated: a sacrificial 1100 °C oxide stripped in HF, dry oxidation at 900 °C and 1 atm, a 6 s pull, cooling in the tube in oxygen, no anneal of any kind. What a simulation must supply: the push and warm-up, the cooling time, and which oxidation time made which measured device (only Figure 1 links time to thickness). The measured quantity is the whole distribution over the gap, which no program in the predictor computes without a hydrogen anneal.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- The push into the 900 °C furnace and the wafer's warm-up: time and gas.
- The cooling in the silica tube after the 6 s pull: how long and how fast.
- Which oxidation time made which measured device: only Figure 1 links time to thickness.
- The time between the HF strip and the oxidation, and between oxidation and metallization (the native oxide and the storage).
- The wafer temperature during evaporation from a tungsten filament.

### What the second reading changed

- The project held no recipe and no digitized value for this paper. SOURCE.md says pages 2 and 5 were read and that the paper reports only the peak density near the band edges, “not comparable with midgap quasi-static values”. Figure 3 (PDF p. 3) does reach 0.50 to 0.58 eV for the 93 and 125 Å n-type devices (about 3.1 and 2.7×10^11 cm⁻² eV⁻¹ at 0.55 eV), so two midgap values exist, by another technique (admittance, not quasi-static).
- SOURCE.md does not mention Figure 1: a thickness-against-time series at 900 °C, 79 to 211 Å over 20 to 140 min, on the same wafers. It is a second laboratory's thin dry oxides at the temperature the thin-oxide program holds out (900 °C).
- The library's conditions record the clean as not_stated. The paper states degreasing, ultrasonic cleaning, acid etching and a sacrificial 1100 °C oxide stripped in HF.
- The library records the pull correctly (6 s, cooled in dry oxygen in the tube). It does not record that there was no anneal of any kind, which decides whether the predictor writes a spectrum.

## Their terms and ours

| The paper says | We write | Kind in our vocabulary | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nis: interface state density (cm⁻² V⁻¹) | `interface state density at midgap; interface state density at 0.30 eV above the valence band …` | observable (target: trap_density) | Per volt on the axes; equal to per eV here. |
| Nis(E) profile, E − Ev | `the spectrum rows (0.20, 0.30, 0.40, 0.55, 0.70, 0.80 eV)` | observable: the challenge's Dit(E) | The predictor writes them only after a hydrogen anneal, as midgap times one measured shape. |
| peaked distribution near Ev / near Ec, overlying a concave background | `(none)` | no observable | No program has a peak, its energy or its height. |
| N_is^peak, E^peak | `(none)` | no observable | The paper's central result is these against thickness. |
| capture cross section σe, σh | `(none)` | no observable | Measured by conductance; nothing in the ontology. |
| optical MOS admittance technique (illuminated C and G) | `EVENT Measure KIND measurement` | event | The language does not record which technique produced an observation. |
| preoxidation, then HF strip | `EVENT PreOxidation, EVENT Strip` | events | Would need a RUN followed by an etch that removes oxide. |
| pulled out in 6 s, cooled in the silica tube in dry O2 | `RUN Pull DURATION 6 … RUN TubeCool … PROFILE settling` | RUNs; process factor final-anneal |  |
| logarithmic then linear phase of oxidation | `(none)` | a claim about growth | The thin-oxide program has one power law. |

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: DRY THERMAL OXIDATION OF SILICON (growth sector)
#
# Linear-parabolic (Deal-Grove) growth written as one ISL operator. Nothing is
# fitted to the test data: every parameter keeps the value declared here.
#
# Provenance of the constants: the rate constants are an Arrhenius fit to Table II of
# Deal and Grove (1965), which is for (111) silicon; see scripts/derive_deal_grove_constants.py.
# The orientation factor for other faces is not in that paper and remains a hypothesis.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_vitkavage_growth.py.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE {sample_id} {{
    FAMILY: Si_Planar
    ORIENTATION: Si{orientation}
}}
# Oxide thickness. The initial value is Deal and Grove's effective starting thickness
# for dry oxygen, which stands in for the fast initial regime their model does not describe.
STATE x_ox {{
    UNIT: cm
    INITIAL: x_i
    MIN: 0
    MAX: 1.0e-3
}}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_O2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
# Parabolic rate constant B = B0 * exp(-E_B / kT).
PARAMETER B0 {{ VALUE: 3.6053e-09; UNIT: cm^2 s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
PARAMETER E_B {{ VALUE: 1.2860; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
# Linear rate constant B/A on (111) = BA0 * exp(-E_BA / kT).
PARAMETER BA0 {{ VALUE: 2.4265e-01; UNIT: cm s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
PARAMETER E_BA {{ VALUE: 2.0303; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
# Orientation factor on the linear rate, relative to (111).
PARAMETER r_or {{ VALUE: {r_or}; UNIT: 1; STATUS: hypothesis }}
# Effective initial thickness for dry oxygen.
PARAMETER x_i {{ VALUE: 2.3e-6; UNIT: cm; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), stated intercept 230 +/- 30 angstrom for dry oxygen, agent-read }}
# Both rate constants are proportional to oxidant pressure; the constants above are for 1 atm.
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY {trajectory_id} {{
    RUN Oxidation DURATION {duration_s} TEMP {T_C}->{T_C} AMBIENT dry_O2
}}
# dx/dt = B / (2x + A), with A = B / (B/A).
OPERATOR F_LinearParabolicGrowth : OxideGrowth {{
    EQUATION: d(x_ox)/dt = (p_O2 / p_ref) * B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (2 * x_ox + B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (r_or * BA0 * exp(-E_BA / (k_B * T))))
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Oxide_Thickness {{
    TARGET: Thickness
    UNIT: cm
    EQUATION: Thickness = x_ox
}}
# Silicon consumed by the oxide grown in this recipe: 0.44 of its thickness, by the ratio of molar volumes.
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Consumed_Silicon {{
    TARGET: Consumed_substrate
    UNIT: cm
    EQUATION: Consumed_substrate = 0.44 * (x_ox - x_i)
}}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT {contract_id} {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/vitkavage_1990_thickness.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{dataset_sha256}
    SPLIT: test
    TARGET: Thickness = {target} +/- {tolerance} rel
}}
```

**The data cannot see any of the three unknowns.** Every scan is flat to 0.1 point of median error. The push is written in nitrogen, and the thin-oxide program grows nothing without oxygen; the trap-density program relaxes by under 3% in 15 min at 900 °C. The pull and the cooling happen in oxygen below 900 °C, where neither program moves a number within the ranges. The aligned values printed above (pull to 650 °C, 120 s of cooling) are ties broken by hundredths of a percent; they are not findings.

**Variants.** A push in oxygen adds 0.5 nm to every oxide (12.5 against 9.3 nm for the 93 Å device, worse) and makes the trap-density program refuse: it takes the oxidation temperature from the first oxygen step's start, 25 °C, and its second population overflows. The 1965 law puts every oxide at 26 to 43 nm (median +104%); it starts from 23 nm. A furnace 20% slower than Massoud's, written as 80% oxygen because the program's rate is proportional to the oxygen pressure, brings 11 of 46 within tolerance: the Figure 1 oxides at 20 to 90 min (+4% to +9%) and five of the six device thicknesses. It is a stand-in, not the paper's gas, and is not used for the aligned numbers.

**With heating and cooling transitions (section above).** None of the four assumed scenarios moves any number by more than 2%: a hot push or a cold-furnace ramp in nitrogen grows nothing in the thin-oxide program, and the cooling knots after the paper's own pull are in oxygen below 800 °C, where nothing here changes.

What alignment cannot fix. **Thickness:** the thin-oxide program is 15 to 25% high through 90 min and 36 to 50% high at 110 and 140 min. Kar's oxide grows 0.22 nm/min between 20 and 50 min and 0.075 nm/min between 50 and 140 min; the program grows 0.24 and 0.17 nm/min. **Midgap density:** 39% and 59% high (4.28 and 4.24×10^11 against 3.08 and 2.67×10^11). The program's value falls by 1% between 30 and 40 min at 900 °C; Kar's falls by 13%. Kar's values are from admittance under illumination, the program's training values from quasi-static C-V, and the program was fitted on oxidations of one hour or more.

**Dit(E):** not modelled. A program for it would need (1) the dangling-bond population the midgap program already carries, spread over two levels, a donor peak near 0.3 eV and an acceptor peak near 0.85 eV above the valence band, with positions and widths as parameters; (2) a separate U-shaped background of band tails, rising toward both edges, which hydrogen does not remove (the annealed Vitkavage curve and Fukuda's Figure 1 are this background); (3) peak heights and energies that depend on the oxide's growth: Kar's peak density falls then rises with thickness (minimum near 110 Å, maximum near 155 Å) and its energy moves between 0.26 and 0.43 eV, so a state tied to thickness or growth rate, which no trap program here has; (4) an observation model that knows which half of the gap a technique on n- or p-type silicon can reach. The data we hold to fit it: Kar's six unannealed curves, Vitkavage's unannealed and annealed (111) curves, Fukuda's three unannealed rapid thermal oxides.

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **Which step is the oxidation.** Writing the push in oxygen, which may be what was done, makes the trap-density program read the oxidation temperature as 25 °C and refuse. A `ROLE oxidation|load|pull|anneal` on each RUN would let a push be written as the heating it is.
- **An etch that removes oxide.** The 1100 °C sacrificial oxide is grown and stripped in HF before the gate oxide; it had to be an event. `ETCH REMOVE ALL` (or `TO <nm>`) after a RUN would let the preoxidation be simulated, so that its heat reaches the programs and its oxide does not.
- **Dit(E) as an observable.** The predictor's energy rows are fixed target names written only after hydrogen. An observation `Dit AT_ENERGY <eV> ABOVE Ev BY admittance|quasistatic` with the substrate type would let a paper's distribution be stated as what was measured, and let a program say it cannot answer at that energy rather than be silent.
- **Peaks and cross sections.** The paper's central results are a peak's height and energy and a capture cross section against thickness. The ontology has no observable for any of them.
- **A device tied to a calibration series.** Each measured device is known by its thickness; its oxidation time comes only from Figure 1. A sample field `OXIDE_NM` read as a measured property, and a way to say a RUN's duration is whatever makes that thickness (an inverse VARY), would replace the times inferred by hand here.
- **Doping and gate metal as inputs.** DOPING is recorded and read by no program; here it decides which half of the gap was measured. The gate metal (Au on n-type, Al on p-type) is not recordable; an aluminium gate can release hydrogen at the interface even without an anneal.
- **A clean word.** The vocabulary has no `sacrificial_oxide_HF` for “preoxidation was part of the surface cleaning”; CLEAN had to be `not_stated` although the paper states it.
