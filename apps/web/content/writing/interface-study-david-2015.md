## The study and why we depend on it

**T. David, A. Benkouider, J.-N. Aqua, M. Cabie, L. Favre, T. Neisius, M. Abbarchi, M. Naffouti, A. Ronda, K. Liu and I. Berbezier, “Kinetics and Energetics of Ge Condensation in SiGe Oxidation”, J. Phys. Chem. C 119, 24606 (2015); HAL hal-01473115 (accepted manuscript, the copy read).**

The dry-oxidation test of the interface enrichment relation (germanium under the oxide settles at a floor plus a share of the starting fraction), which was fitted on Müller 2023's wet oxidations. David's statement that the germanium-rich layer holds 50% at every temperature up to 900 °C is the relation's main held-out evidence. The 34 nm film on thin SOI adds what no planar program of ours describes: an alloy that is used up.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Moderate.** Two stacks, one gas, a stated temperature and duration for every sample. A simulation must still supply the RTO ramps, the gas during them, whether the 750 °C series is one sample re-oxidized in 30 min steps, and the starting surface. The numbers that matter are sentences (50% germanium up to 900 °C; 5 nm and 10 nm of germanium-rich layer; 70% at 14 h, pure germanium at 15 h); thicknesses at 800 to 950 °C exist only as a graph of thickness squared over time.

|---|---|
| Sample described | 0 of 5 properties we need are stated and encoded the same |
| History described | 3 of 8 steps are stated and encoded the same; 2 are stated by the paper and absent from our history; 2 are ours because the paper is silent |

| Outcome | 7 of 21 numbers within tolerance |

The grade is ours and is about the paper as a source for simulation, not about the quality of the experiment: **high** means the sample, every step and its timing are stated well enough that a simulation needs no choices of ours; **moderate** that some choices are ours and are listed; **low** that the result depends on choices the paper does not constrain.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- RTO ramp up and ramp down rates, and the gas during the ramps.
- Whether time counts from reaching the set point.
- Whether the 750 °C series is separate samples or one sample re-oxidized “by steps of 30 min in accordance with the RTO technical specifications”.
- Storage and any native oxide before oxidation.
- 700 and 750 °C are missing from Figure 4 without a reason; 11 h in the Figure 5a legend is 12 h in Figure 5c; a 13 h state is described with no profile.

### What the second reading changed

- The dataset holds two rows of this paper (50% at 800 °C, 90 min; 50% at 750 °C, 6 h). The text supports the same 50% at 750, 850 and 900 °C too, but Figure 3b peaks near 45% at 900 °C, so the 900 °C row is the paper's sentence against its own figure.
- The stored recipe of the 6 h row is one 6 h hold. The paper's sample is a 34 nm film on 12 nm of silicon on oxide; by 6 h all the alloy is consumed. No stored field says so.
- Ramps, the gas during them and the 30 min stepping are absent from the stored recipes; they are VARY ranges and a variant here.
- The paper says germanium is not oxidized (PDF p. 9) and that under 10% was lost as GeO2 (PDF p. 12); from its own thicknesses the loss is about 26%. Our inventory program conserves germanium exactly.
- The predictor runs the Rabie wet-oxygen program (800 °C, 14% constants) for these dry oxidations and labels the thickness “direction only”, not outside its scope.

The model itself, `experiments/sige_interface/interface_enrichment.isl.template`, as it is written. Every equation the simulation uses is in this text; the runtime holds no physics.

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: GERMANIUM AT THE OXIDE INTERFACE OF A SiGe ALLOY
#
# While the alloy is being oxidized, the germanium fraction just under the oxide moves to a
# value set by the starting alloy: a floor plus a share of the starting fraction. The two
# numbers are fitted to one laboratory's two planar measurements (Mueller et al. 2023, 20% and
# 50% alloys) and the program is then scored on other laboratories.
#
# This is a relation written as a relaxation, not a transport model: it says where the
# interface composition settles, not why. How fast it settles is not measured by any source we
# hold (the shortest oxidation is two minutes), so the settling time is a hypothesis.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_sige_interface.py.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE {sample_id} {{
    FAMILY: SiGe_oxide
    COMPOSITION: {composition}
}}
STATE ge_i {{ UNIT: 1; INITIAL: ge_start; MIN: 0; MAX: 1 }}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_N2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
CONTROL p_Ar {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER ge_start {{ VALUE: {ge_start}; UNIT: 1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: the sample's stated composition }}
PARAMETER floor_ge {{ VALUE: 0.3; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER share_ge {{ VALUE: 0.8; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
# No source measures the interface earlier than two minutes into an oxidation.
PARAMETER settle_time {{ VALUE: 30.0; UNIT: s; STATUS: hypothesis }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
# The interface moves only while the alloy is being oxidized: not during a hold in nitrogen or argon.
QUANTITY oxidizing {{ UNIT: 1; EQUATION: oxidizing = 1 - (p_N2 + p_Ar) / p_ref }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY {trajectory_id} {{
{runs}
}}
OPERATOR F_InterfaceEnrichment : Relaxation {{
    EQUATION: d(ge_i)/dt = -oxidizing * (ge_i - (floor_ge + share_ge * ge_start)) / settle_time
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Ge_At_Interface {{ TARGET: Ge_at_interface; UNIT: 1; EQUATION: Ge_at_interface = ge_i }}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT {contract_id} {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/sige_interface_germanium.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{dataset_sha256}
    SPLIT: {split}
    TARGET: Ge_at_interface = {target} +/- {tolerance} abs
}}
```

## Aligning the simulation: what the unstated things would have to be

Every step whose timing or temperature the paper leaves open carries a range in the history above (`VARY`). `scripts/align_study.py` searched those ranges against the paper's numbers through the same predictor, first one unknown at a time, then all together. Two timing unknowns were searched, the RTO ramp up (10 s to 10 min) and ramp down (20 s to 15 min), chosen on the five relaxed (structure 1) samples; the thin film on SOI (structure 2) is the test. Two variants were tried: the ramps in nitrogen instead of oxygen, and the 750 °C series as one sample re-oxidized in 30 min steps, each with its own ramps (36 RUNs for 6 h, 90 for 15 h).

**None of them changes a verdict.** On structure 1, 4 of 13 numbers are within tolerance at every ramp length; the median error moves between 21.5% and 30% and is smallest near one minute up and two minutes down, the values the reproduction assumed. The combined search settles on 77 s up and 38 s down, with the same 4 of 13. On the test, 3 of 8 within tolerance (median error 41%) before and after. Ramps in nitrogen change the germanium-rich layer by a few hundredths of a nanometre; 30 min re-oxidation steps add 3% to the oxide (268 to 276 nm at 6 h) and under 2% to the layer.

**What the data do pin down is not timing.** The germanium at the interface is 48.7% for every sample, every ramp and every variant: the enrichment relation has no temperature, no time beyond a 30 s settling, and no layer thickness. That is within 3 points of David's 50% at 750 to 900 °C and after 6 and 13 h, on data the relation never saw, and 6 points high at 950 °C, where the paper reports the germanium spreading out.

What alignment cannot fix. Three things, each a model or language gap. (1) The oxide thickness on these dry oxidations comes from the Rabie program, which carries wet-oxygen constants chosen for 800 °C and does not read the gas: it gives 77.5 to 79.5 nm from 800 to 950 °C, almost no temperature dependence, against 37 to 150 nm (activation energy 2.1 eV in the paper). On the 34 nm film it gives 268 nm of oxide after 6 h and 569 nm after 15 h, more than the whole film and silicon layer could make (about 91 nm in the paper's Figure 5b). (2) The germanium-rich layer thickness comes from the inventory program, whose dry growth is silicon's 1965 law: it consumes 2.7 nm of alloy in 90 min at 800 °C, so the layer is 1.9 nm against 11 nm, 70 to 83% low at every temperature. The two programs that run on one history disagree with each other about how much oxide grew by a factor of about 16 (77.5 against about 5 nm). (3) After 13 h the alloy on SOI is used up and the layer condenses to 70% and then pure germanium; no program of ours has a finite alloy, so the 14 h and 15 h values are 30 and 51% low. The 15 h layer thickness (5.4 against 5 nm) is within tolerance by coincidence.

The inventory's layer rows are labelled outside the tested scope because the ramps start at 25 °C, below the 750 to 900 °C its scope names: the predictor reads every step's temperature for scope, so a ramp written as stated makes a tested oxidation look untested.

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **A layered sample.** “34 nm Si0.8Ge0.2 on 12 nm Si on 10 nm BOX” is recorded as a FILM string that nothing reads. The sample needs layers with thickness and composition (`LAYER SiGe { COMPOSITION: Si0.8Ge0.2; THICKNESS: 34 nm; STRAIN: compressive }`) and programs that stop consuming a layer when it is gone. This paper's whole second half (6 h, 13 h, 14 h, 15 h) depends on it.
- **Constants tied to a gas and a condition, with a refusal outside them.** The predictor answers a dry 950 °C oxidation with wet 800 °C constants and labels it “direction only”. Parameters should carry the conditions they were chosen for, and a history outside them should be labelled or refused.
- **One oxide per history.** Two programs report on one SiGe history and grow oxide 16 times apart (Rabie 77.5 nm, inventory about 5 nm at 800 °C, 90 min). The predictor should take the oxide from one program and feed it to the others, or report the disagreement.
- **Scope read from the oxidation, not from every step.** A ramp from room temperature puts every number “outside the tested scope”. Scope should be judged on the steps that do the work (the hold), with ramps reported separately.
- **A furnace cycle as a unit.** “By steps of 30 min in accordance with the RTO technical specifications” is a repeated cycle (ramp, 30 min, ramp). A `REPEAT n { … }` block, or a tool with its own ramp law, would write it in one line instead of 90 RUNs.
- **VARY on a time constant.** A cooling written as `PROFILE exponential TAU` cannot have its TAU searched; here the cooling had to be written as a straight line to make it an unknown.
- **Germanium lost as GeO2.** The paper reports under 10% lost by oxidation (about 26% from its own thicknesses); the inventory conserves germanium exactly. A loss flux, or at least a declared bound on it, belongs in the inventory program.
- **Strain as a sample property.** Relaxed against fully strained is the paper's subject (Eqs. 1 and 2); the language has no field for it that a program could read.
