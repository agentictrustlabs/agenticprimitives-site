## The study and why we depend on it

**C. Krzeminski, G. Larrieu, J. Penaud, E. Lampin and E. Dubois, “Silicon dry oxidation kinetics at low temperature in the nanometric range: modeling and experiment”, J. Appl. Phys. 101, 064908 (2007); arXiv:1106.3160.**

The thin-oxide program the predictor runs on planar silicon in dry oxygen (`planar_si_thin_growth` in `isl_harness/predict.py`) is a structure found by `scripts/search_growth.py` on `data/derived/silicon_dry_kinetics.csv`: 115 points of Massoud's data as redrawn in this paper's Fig. 4, and 12 of the paper's own at 725 and 750 °C. It was fitted to Massoud at 800 to 1000 °C with 900 °C held out (18 of 25), and the 725 and 750 °C points are its other-laboratory test (6 of 10, 13% low at 725 °C). That test started the law at the measured thickness at time zero and ran only the isothermal stage. The second reading found the whole cycle: loading 50 °C below, a 30 min ramp in nitrogen with 9% oxygen that grows 1.53 or 1.96 nm before time zero, the stage in oxygen, then 10 min down and 30 min of reflow in nitrogen. This page writes that cycle and asks whether it explains the low result. The program printed under “The simulation in our language” is the 1965 program, which also runs on these histories but is not scored here; the thin-oxide program, which the rows compare, exists only as text generated by the search and is printed in full at the end of the page.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Moderate.** The paper's own furnace cycle is described step by step, with times, gas flows and the oxide grown before time zero. What a simulation must still supply: the temperatures of loading and reflow (on a figure only), the loading time and gas, whether the 0 min wafers went through the ramp-down and reflow, and the delay in air before ellipsometry. Massoud's data are second-hand, redrawn, with a 1.0 nm starting oxide stated and no history beyond temperature and pressure.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- Time zero is the start of the main stage: every thickness at “0 min” already holds 30 min of ramp growth. The language counts time from the first RUN, so the datasets' recipe (one isothermal RUN) and the paper's cycle disagree about what t = 0 means.
- How long the wafers sat at the loading temperature, and in what gas, before the ramp.
- The ramp's shape: 50 °C in 30 min, read from Fig. 9's end points; linear is our assumption.
- Whether the 0 min wafers went through the ramp-down and reflow (Fig. 9 labels the main stage [10–90] min).
- The reflow temperature: drawn in Fig. 9, not in the text.
- Unloading and cooling after the reflow: not in the manuscript.
- The delay in air before ex-situ ellipsometry; an uncaptioned figure shows 0.25–0.37 nm of growth in 90 min of exposure, which is as large as the growth of the first 10 min of the main stage.
- For Massoud's data: ramp, time zero and how the 1.0 nm starting oxide was made are not given in this paper.

### What the second reading changed

- The stored recipe for the paper's own wafers is one RUN of 90 min at 725 or 750 °C in dry oxygen. The paper's cycle is a load at 675/700 °C, a 30 min ramp in 9% oxygen, the main stage in oxygen, 10 min of ramp-down and 30 min of reflow in nitrogen. The ramp is the one that matters: it grows 1.53 or 1.96 nm, all of the oxide at “0 min”.
- The other-laboratory score in `evaluations/growth_search_report.json` (6 of 10, 13% low at 725 °C) started the law at the measured 1.53 or 1.96 nm. The predictor cannot do that: it starts every history at the fitted 1.71 nm. Run through the predictor with the stored recipe, the same points come out between 1% high and 14% low at 725 °C and 8 to 11% low at 750 °C, and the 0 min points (excluded from the search's score) 12% high and 13% low. The two scores of one program on one dataset differ because the starting oxide lives in different places.
- Table II prints 2.71 nm by ellipsometry for the 30 min wafer at 725 °C (TEM 2.7 nm); the dataset, from the figure, has 2.59 nm at 30 min and 2.71 nm at 45 min. One of the two is mislabelled; the dataset follows the figure.
- The main stage is written as pure oxygen. The paper says only “an enhanced oxygen flux of (2 L/min)”; whether the 2 L/min of nitrogen continues is not stated. If it does, the stage is at 0.5 atm of oxygen.
- The source folder's `SOURCE.md` says “Held, not yet extracted”; the folder holds `kinetics_vector_extracted.csv` and the dataset is built from it. The note is out of date.
- Massoud's wafers are written as (100). This paper does not state their orientation; the model geometry it uses is (100).

## Their terms and ours

| The paper says | We write | Kind in our vocabulary | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| oxide thickness (ellipsometry, in situ for Massoud, ex situ for the paper's own) | `Thickness` | observable (target: layer_thickness) | In situ and ex situ are one observable in the vocabulary. |
| preoxidation ramp, N2 2 L/min + O2 0.2 L/min | `RUN Ramp DURATION 1800 TEMP 675->725 AMBIENT mix:N2=0.909,O2=0.0909` | a RUN with a mixture | The flow ratio becomes partial pressures; no program reads them. |
| main oxidation stage, enhanced oxygen flux 2 L/min | `RUN Oxidation … AMBIENT dry_O2` | a RUN | Whether nitrogen continues is not stated. |
| ramp down in inert nitrogen; thermal reflow | `RUN RampDown … inert_N2; RUN Reflow … inert_N2` | two RUNs; process factor inert-anneal | The paper expects no growth; the thin-oxide program grows in them. |
| sample introduction 50 °C below | `EVENT Load KIND load` | event | No time or gas given. |
| time zero of the kinetics (start of the main stage) | `(none)` | no term | The datasets' t = 0 is the end of the ramp; the language counts time from the first RUN. |
| initial oxide (1.0 nm, Massoud; 1.6–2 nm native, Chao) | `STARTING_OXIDE (recorded)` | specimen field read by no program |  |
| reaction rate approach: n_O2, n_Si, K, D | `(none)` | a model of the paper's, not encoded | The paper's own model; its fitted D and K (Table I) are not in our programs. |
| Deal–Grove and Massoud's correction C2·exp(−X/L) | `experiments/vitkavage_dit/oxide_growth.isl.template (Deal–Grove only)` | OPERATOR | Massoud's correction term is not in any program; the search chose a power law instead. |

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: DRY THERMAL OXIDATION OF SILICON (growth sector)
#
# Linear-parabolic (Deal-Grove) growth written as one ISL operator. Nothing is
# fitted to the test data: every parameter keeps the value declared here.
#
# Provenance of the constants: the rate constants are an Arrhenius fit to Table II of
# Deal and Grove (1965), which is for (111) silicon; see scripts/derive_deal_grove_constants.py.
# The orientation factor for other faces is not in that paper and remains a hypothesis.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_vitkavage_growth.py.
# ==============================================================================
SPECIMEN {specimen_id} {{
    FAMILY: Si_Planar
    ORIENTATION: Si{orientation}
}}
# Oxide thickness. The initial value is Deal and Grove's effective starting thickness
# for dry oxygen, which stands in for the fast initial regime their model does not describe.
STATE x_ox {{
    UNIT: cm
    INITIAL: x_i
    MIN: 0
    MAX: 1.0e-3
}}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_O2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
# Parabolic rate constant B = B0 * exp(-E_B / kT).
PARAMETER B0 {{ VALUE: 3.6053e-09; UNIT: cm^2 s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
PARAMETER E_B {{ VALUE: 1.2860; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
# Linear rate constant B/A on (111) = BA0 * exp(-E_BA / kT).
PARAMETER BA0 {{ VALUE: 2.4265e-01; UNIT: cm s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
PARAMETER E_BA {{ VALUE: 2.0303; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), Table II, dry oxygen on (111), Arrhenius least-squares fit, table agent-read }}
# Orientation factor on the linear rate, relative to (111).
PARAMETER r_or {{ VALUE: {r_or}; UNIT: 1; STATUS: hypothesis }}
# Effective initial thickness for dry oxygen.
PARAMETER x_i {{ VALUE: 2.3e-6; UNIT: cm; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965), stated intercept 230 +/- 30 angstrom for dry oxygen, agent-read }}
# Both rate constants are proportional to oxidant pressure; the constants above are for 1 atm.
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY {trajectory_id} {{
    RUN Oxidation DURATION {duration_s} TEMP {T_C}->{T_C} AMBIENT dry_O2
}}
# dx/dt = B / (2x + A), with A = B / (B/A).
OPERATOR F_LinearParabolicGrowth : OxideGrowth {{
    EQUATION: d(x_ox)/dt = (p_O2 / p_ref) * B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (2 * x_ox + B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (r_or * BA0 * exp(-E_BA / (k_B * T))))
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Oxide_Thickness {{
    TARGET: Thickness
    UNIT: cm
    EQUATION: Thickness = x_ox
}}
# Silicon consumed by the oxide grown in this recipe: 0.44 of its thickness, by the ratio of molar volumes.
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Consumed_Silicon {{
    TARGET: Consumed_substrate
    UNIT: cm
    EQUATION: Consumed_substrate = 0.44 * (x_ox - x_i)
}}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT {contract_id} {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/vitkavage_1990_thickness.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{dataset_sha256}
    SPLIT: test
    TARGET: Thickness = {target} +/- {tolerance} rel
}}
```

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **A starting oxide a program reads.** The single most useful addition. The thin-oxide program starts every history at its fitted 1.71 nm, so Massoud's 1.0 nm wafers, Krzeminski's HF-dipped wafers and a 1.53 nm post-ramp oxide all start the same. With `STARTING_OXIDE` read by the growth programs (default: the program's fitted value), the search's other-laboratory test (start at the measured 1.53 nm) and the predictor would give one answer instead of two.
- **Growth programs that read the oxygen pressure.** The thin-oxide program has no p_O2 in its rate. It grows in nitrogen (0.2 nm in a 30 min reflow at 675 °C) and grows a 9% oxygen ramp as pure oxygen. Multiplying B (or the power law's constant) by p_O2/p_ref, as the 1965 program does, would make the stated cycle meaningful; the alignment's ramp of 580 s is a symptom.
- **Time zero as a statement.** The datasets count time from the start of the main stage; the language counts from the first RUN. A marker such as `EVENT TimeZero KIND other` that results can refer to, or a `t0` on the dataset rows, would let the stored recipe hold the whole cycle without moving every time.
- **A curve of separate wafers.** Each of the paper's points is a wafer that went through the whole cycle; Massoud's are one wafer watched in situ. The study format can sample in situ (curves within a RUN) but needs one case per wafer for the ex-situ points. The language should say which kind of series a dataset is.
- **A fast first regime.** Neither the 1965 law nor the searched power law grows the first 1.5 nm of a bare surface; both fold it into a starting thickness. An operator for it (or a second state for the first monolayers) is what Krzeminski's ramp asks for, and what its own reaction-rate model provides.
- **Exposure to air before an ex-situ measurement.** An uncaptioned figure shows 0.25–0.37 nm of growth in 90 min of air. A storage EVENT with a duration and an ambient (room air) that a program can read would carry it.

## What the reproduction shows

**Does writing the ramp explain the low result?** No. The 13% shortfall at 725 °C is in the growth after time zero, not in where the curve starts. Run from the measured 1.53 nm at the start of the main stage (a side calculation with the fitted law, integrated outside the predictor, which reproduces the search's numbers), the law adds 0.20 nm in the first 10 min and 1.51 nm in 90 min; the paper's wafers added 0.35 and 2.17 nm. At 750 °C it adds 2.31 against 2.61 nm. The ramp happened before time zero and is already in the 1.53 nm; whatever the ramp does, the main-stage increments are 30% short at 725 °C and 12% short at 750 °C. What would explain it is something that adds oxide after time zero to every wafer but the 0 min one, or a law that is too slow below the 800 °C it was fitted at. The second is what the paper itself says of Deal–Grove and Massoud's correction.

**What the ramp would take.** From an HF-dipped surface (0.3 to 0.5 nm) the fitted law grows 0.37 to 0.56 nm in the ramp at the stated 9% oxygen (if the rate scales with oxygen pressure), and 0.90 to 1.03 nm even in pure oxygen at 725 °C; the paper measured 1.53 nm. The law has no fast first regime: on Massoud's data that regime is folded into its fitted 1.71 nm start. The ramp's oxide is that regime, grown in a stated history, and no program we hold can grow it.
