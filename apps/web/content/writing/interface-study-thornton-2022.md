## The study and why we depend on it

**C. S. Thornton, B. Tuttle, E. Turner, M. E. Law, S. Pantelides, G. T. Wang and K. S. Jones, “The Diffusion Mechanism of Ge During Oxidation of Si/SiGe Nanofins”, ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces (2022); OSTI 1877137, accepted manuscript SAND2022-8627J (the copy read).**

The challenge's SiGe system includes nanostructures, and this is the one paper we hold that measures how germanium moves on an oxidizing SiGe nanostructure as a function of time and temperature. It tests whether our SiGe programs can be pointed at a fin at all: the predictor runs every SiGe sample as a planar alloy, and the nanowire program's flat-sidewall handling is written for silicon in steam.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Low.** The stack (four periods of 15 nm Si / 15 nm Si0.7Ge0.3, strained, on a 110 nm Si fin base), the gas and the four temperatures are stated. Which fin width was measured, the sidewall plane, the oxidation times (only 5, 10, 20 and 50 min at 900 °C are printed; the rest sit on a √t axis), the furnace push and pull, the oxide grown on each sample, and the conditions of the forming-gas control are not. The paper's quantity, a length along the sidewall to a 3% germanium contour, is measured on a two-dimensional cross-section that no program of ours represents.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- Oxidation times other than 5, 10, 20 (Figure 4) and 50 min (Figure 2) at 900 °C: read from the √t axis of Figure 5.
- Push and pull of the tube furnace: load temperature, rate, gas.
- Time between the Bosch etch and oxidation; any clean or polymer removal.
- The forming-gas control: temperature and times are in the supplement (Figure S5), not in this PDF.
- Oxide thickness of each sample: only “approximately 16 to 34 nm” for the sets and two sentences at 900 °C, 10 min.

### What the second reading changed

- SOURCE.md and ontology/library.json say “at 900 C”. The paper oxidizes at 750, 800, 850 and 900 °C and its result is an Arrhenius plot over the four.
- The challenge brief and library.json call these SiGe fins; the germanium the paper measures moves down the silicon base of a Si/SiGe superlattice fin. The oxidizing interface where the length is measured is Si/SiO2, fed from SiGe layers above.
- Figure 4 (5, 10, 20 min at 900 °C) does not match Figure 5's 900 °C abscissae (about 10, 15, 20, 25 min).
- The low-oxygen control is a vacuum-sealed ampule in the text (PDF p. 9) and “an anneal under forming gas” in the Figure 7 caption.
- No dataset holds this paper; it is listed as skimmed, unused.

The model itself, `experiments/sige_interface/interface_enrichment.isl.template`, as it is written. Every equation the simulation uses is in this text; the runtime holds no physics.

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: GERMANIUM AT THE OXIDE INTERFACE OF A SiGe ALLOY
#
# While the alloy is being oxidized, the germanium fraction just under the oxide moves to a
# value set by the starting alloy: a floor plus a share of the starting fraction. The two
# numbers are fitted to one laboratory's two planar measurements (Mueller et al. 2023, 20% and
# 50% alloys) and the program is then scored on other laboratories.
#
# This is a relation written as a relaxation, not a transport model: it says where the
# interface composition settles, not why. How fast it settles is not measured by any source we
# hold (the shortest oxidation is two minutes), so the settling time is a hypothesis.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_sige_interface.py.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE {sample_id} {{
    FAMILY: SiGe_oxide
    COMPOSITION: {composition}
}}
STATE ge_i {{ UNIT: 1; INITIAL: ge_start; MIN: 0; MAX: 1 }}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_N2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
CONTROL p_Ar {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER ge_start {{ VALUE: {ge_start}; UNIT: 1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: the sample's stated composition }}
PARAMETER floor_ge {{ VALUE: 0.3; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER share_ge {{ VALUE: 0.8; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
# No source measures the interface earlier than two minutes into an oxidation.
PARAMETER settle_time {{ VALUE: 30.0; UNIT: s; STATUS: hypothesis }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
# The interface moves only while the alloy is being oxidized: not during a hold in nitrogen or argon.
QUANTITY oxidizing {{ UNIT: 1; EQUATION: oxidizing = 1 - (p_N2 + p_Ar) / p_ref }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY {trajectory_id} {{
{runs}
}}
```

What alignment cannot fix: the paper's quantity has no program. The length down the sidewall (27 to 38 nm at 900 °C, √t, 1.1 eV) and the lateral width (1.5 to 2.5 nm, 1.6 eV) are 14 of the 19 numbers, and the predictor reports neither. This is case (c): no program in the predictor models the quantity. A fin program would need, in order of importance:

(1) **Germanium as an interfacial species.** A surface concentration of germanium living on the Si/SiO2 interface, with a diffusivity along it (Thornton's effective D, about 5e-15 cm²/s at 900 °C, 1.1 eV) and a source that exists only while SiGe is being oxidized: the germanium the inventory program releases (x N_ox X per unit area of oxidized alloy) feeds it instead of all going straight down into the alloy. The sealed-ampule control (little motion with 5 nm of oxide) is then a test of the source term, not a separate assumption.

(2) **A reinsertion sink.** Exchange of interfacial germanium into substitutional sites of the silicon below the interface, with its own activation energy (1.6 eV measured, 1.3 to 1.8 eV by DFT), giving the lateral width.

(3) **A coordinate along the sidewall.** The present runtime has one depth grid that travels with a planar front. A fin needs at least a second coordinate (height along the sidewall) for the interfacial species, and the boundary where the SiGe layers meet the sidewall; the full problem is a 2D cross-section with the oxide front moving on two faces and the top.

(4) **Dry oxidation on a silicon sidewall.** The nanowire program's flat-sidewall handling grows oxide only in steam (Mueller's 850 °C rate); in dry oxygen it grows nothing. The planar programs grow either 25 nm (from Deal and Grove's 23 nm offset) or 7 to 8.5 nm (thin-oxide program) where about 20 nm is reported. The sidewall plane is not stated.

(5) **A layered sample and strain.** Four 15 nm Si0.7Ge0.3 layers between silicon, pseudomorphic, are a FILM string; the programs see an infinite alloy.

(6) **An observation model for a contour.** The paper measures where the germanium falls to about 3% (EDS) along the sidewall. That is a level-set length, not a value at a depth.

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **Interface-resident species and operators that act along an interface.** A `FIELD` on an interface (surface concentration of mobile germanium) with `Transport` along it, a source tied to the oxidation flux, and an exchange into the crystal below. Without it, the paper's mechanism and every one of its 14 measured lengths and widths cannot be written.
- **A second spatial coordinate, or a sidewall coordinate.** `GRID` is one-dimensional and travels with a planar front; a fin needs height along the sidewall and lateral depth.
- **SHAPE read by every program, with a refusal.** The SiGe programs check only nanosheet and nanowire; a SHAPE: fin sample is run as a planar alloy without a label. Every program should state which shapes it accepts and label the rest outside its scope.
- **Layered samples.** `LAYER` blocks (composition, thickness, strain) for a superlattice on a silicon base, so that a program knows each SiGe layer holds 15 nm of alloy.
- **A contour observable.** `OBSERVATION_MODEL ... EQUATION: L = extent(x_Ge > 0.03 along sidewall)`: an observable defined by a level of a field.
- **Residual gas of a sealed ampule.** The ambient was written as air at 35 mtorr; a statement for “sealed, pumped to P, gas unknown” with the oxygen partial pressure as an unknown (`VARY p_O2`) would let alignment search it.
- **Sidewall orientation.** ORIENTATION is a wafer property; a fin's sidewall plane is a separate property that the growth programs should read.
