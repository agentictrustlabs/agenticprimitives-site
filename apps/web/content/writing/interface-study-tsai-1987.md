# Reproducing Tsai et al. 1987: trap density after nitridation in ammonia

*One of the [studies we reproduced](/writing/interface-studies-reproduced) for [Questions we need Emily to answer](/writing/questions-for-emily-testing-a-theory-of-the-interface), the companion to [the essay](/writing/testing-a-theory-of-the-interface-with-an-ontology). Every value from the paper was read by an AI agent and has not been checked by a person; the sample and history were re-read from the PDF alone by a second agent that had not seen our earlier transcription.*

## The study and why we depend on it

**H.-H. Tsai, L.-C. Wu, C.-Y. Wu and C. Hu, “Effects of thermal nitridation conditions on the reliability of thin nitrided oxide films”, IEEE Electron Device Lett. EDL-8, 143-145 (1987).**

A study of thermally nitrided gate oxides: 250 Å of dry oxide grown at 920 °C, nitrided in ammonia at 900, 1000, 1100 and 1150 °C for 0.5 and 3 h, then put through a full polysilicon-gate process ending in forming gas at 430 °C. It is in the project's library because it measures trap density after nitridation. It does not measure trap density against oxidation history: what it reports is the trap density generated by electron injection, the flat-band voltage and the charge to breakdown. Nitridation is the first process in the challenge's system 1 that our language cannot write: there is no ammonia (or N2O) among its gases and no program for nitrogen at the interface. This page writes the history with nitrogen standing in for the heat of the nitridation, shows what the programs then predict, and records what is missing.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Low.** The gate oxidation temperature, the 250 Å thickness, the 30 min nitrogen anneal, the nitridation temperatures and times, the load temperature, the reflow and the forming-gas temperature are stated. Not stated: the oxidation time, the ramp rates and ramp gas around the nitridation, the drive-in time, the reflow gas, the forming-gas time and H2 fraction, and every deposition temperature. The central step cannot be written: the language has no ammonia, and no program incorporates nitrogen. The trap densities are only plotted, against injected fluence, and the pre-stress values are not printed.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- Gate oxidation time at 920 °C: only the 250 Å thickness is stated.
- Pull from the oxidation furnace and transfer to the nitridation furnace: rate, gas and waiting time.
- Ramp from the 900 °C load to the nitridation temperature and back down: rate, gas (ammonia or inert) and unload temperature.
- Whether the stated 0.5 and 3 h include the ramps.
- Drive-in at 1000 °C: time and gas.
- Reflow at 1038 °C: gas and ramps (time stated, 30 min).
- Forming gas at 430 °C: time and H2 fraction.
- LPCVD polysilicon and CVD oxide: temperatures and times (both expose the interface to hydrogen-bearing gases).

### What the second reading changed

- The project holds no recipe or dataset for this paper (`ontology/library.json`: status held, used_by empty; SOURCE.md: “Not read by us”). Everything here is from the second reading.
- SOURCE.md says the paper reports “trap density after nitridation at 900 to 1150 C”. It reports the trap density generated during electron injection after nitridation (Figure 1). The pre-stress trap density was measured (“first measured for the interface state density”) but is not printed; the curves of Figure 1 start below about 1×10^11 cm⁻² eV⁻¹.
- The 1000 °C, 3 h condition is listed in the text (“at 900, 1000, 1100, and 1150 C for 0, 0.5, and 3 h”) but has no entry in either table.
- The pure oxide was stressed at 1 mA/cm², the nitrided oxides at 10 mA/cm² (Table I and figure captions), so the paper's comparison of pure and nitrided oxide in Figure 1 mixes two stress currents.

## Their terms and ours

| The paper says | We write | Kind in our vocabulary | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| thermally nitrided in NH3 gas ambient | `RUN Nitridation … AMBIENT inert_N2 (stand-in)` | no ambient: NH3 is refused by the runtime | Needs a gas (p_NH3) and a program for nitrogen at the interface. |
| degree of nitridation | `(none)` | no state or observable | The paper's explanatory variable; it is not measured in the paper. |
| nitrided oxide, oxynitride | `(none)` | no material in the sample fields | Every program treats the film as SiO2. |
| interface trap density Dit | `Dit` | observable (target: trap_density) | The paper's technique and energy are not stated. |
| interface trap generation under constant current stress | `(none)` | no stress step, no generation observable | The paper's main result. |
| flat-band voltage Vfb (unstressed) | `(none)` | no observable | Could be computed from fixed charge, trapped charge and the gate work function if the gate were a sample property a program reads. |
| total charge to breakdown Qbd | `(none)` | no observable | Reliability, outside the interface properties the predictor covers. |
| electron traps in the bulk of the oxynitride | `(none)` | no state | The paper attributes the flat-band shift under stress to them. |
| in-situ annealed … in N2 | `RUN NitrogenAnneal … AMBIENT inert_N2` | a RUN; process factor inert-anneal |  |
| forming gas annealing at 430 C | `RUN FormingGas … AMBIENT forming_gas_H2` | a RUN; process factor hydrogen-anneal | Time and H2 fraction ours. |
| reflowed at 1038 C | `RUN Reflow … AMBIENT inert_N2` | a RUN | Gas ours. |

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **Ammonia and nitrous oxide as gases.** `AMBIENT mix:NH3=1.0` and `mix:N2O=1.0` are refused (the gases are Ar, H2, H2O, N2, O2). Adding p_NH3 and p_N2O as controls would let a nitridation be written as done; today it can only be written as nitrogen, which carries its heat and nothing else. Every row here would then at least be run on the paper's history.
- **A program for nitrogen at the interface.** With the gas written, a program has to say what it does: incorporate nitrogen at the Si/SiO2 interface (rate rising with temperature, saturating with time), release hydrogen into the film, and shift fixed charge and trap generation. The paper's Table II (more negative flat-band voltage at lower nitridation temperature, positive after 3 h at 1100 and 1150 °C) is the first check such a program would have.
- **An electrical stress step.** Figure 1 and 2 are measured during constant-current injection; the language has no statement for it, so the paper's main result is outside any history. A `STRESS` statement with current density, polarity and fluence, and observables for generated trap density and flat-band shift, would make it a target.
- **The gate as a sample property a program reads.** A flat-band voltage needs the gate work function (As-doped polysilicon here). `GATE` is recorded on the sample and read by nothing; a program computing Vfb from fixed charge, trapped charge and the gate would let Table II be scored.
- **The forming-gas time and H2 fraction (history).** Since 11 November the capping rate has Brower's 1.66 eV (see the Reed 1989 page), so a forming-gas ramp from room temperature counts for little (the cold-furnace ramps lower the density 37%, where they gave ten times before). What still sets the predicted density is what the paper does not state: the forming-gas time (10 to 60 min) and the H2 fraction (5% gives 3.7 times the density of 10%).
- **Process steps that bring hydrogen.** LPCVD polysilicon (silane), CVD oxide and the ammonia itself are hydrogen sources the history records only as events. A hydrogen source on an EVENT or a deposition RUN with its gas would let them reach the trap-density program.

## With Quantum ESPRESSO switched on

This page was simulated with parameters from atomic calculations switched on (`ISL_USE_COMPUTED=1`). They come from Quantum ESPRESSO (the discovered-materials dm_qe build), run locally on CPU and on an H100 on RunPod, recorded in `evaluations/atomistic/` with the code version, inputs, pseudopotential checksums and convergence, and carried into ISL as `STATUS: computed`. They never replace a fitted or sourced number: the rows scored above are the same with the switch off. Each computed row sits beside the measurement it bears on.

What is computed so far is one reaction: Si• + ½H₂ → Si–H for a silicon dangling bond with three silicon back-bonds (the local structure of a Pb centre), on a 14-atom cluster. With the free energy of hydrogen gas it bounds the share of dangling bonds that a hydrogen step could leave uncapped if it reached equilibrium, and so the lowest interface state density that step could reach. A measurement far above the bound means what remains after the hydrogen step is not limited by the strength of the Si–H bond: the anneal is far from equilibrium, or the traps that remain are not dangling bonds hydrogen can cap.
