## The study and why we depend on it

**D. Goto and Y. Hijikata, “Unified theory of silicon carbide oxidation based on the Si and C emission model”, J. Phys. D: Appl. Phys. 49, 225103 (2016).**

Two things. A third laboratory's dry-oxygen growth on the Si and C faces (eight temperatures across the two faces), run through the SiC growth program with Song 2004's constants. And a candidate structure for that program: the Si and C emission model, whose equations and parameters are recorded below with whether our language can carry them.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Low.** A modelling paper whose data are in-situ growth rates against thickness. The oxidation itself is stated (dry oxygen, 1 atm, a lamp heater, argon before oxygen), but not its duration, the clean, or the heat-up, and the model's material parameters are mostly not printed. Thickness against time has to be rebuilt by integrating the rates.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- Clean and loading: not stated.
- Heat-up rate of the lamp heater and time in argon at temperature: not stated.
- Time from the oxygen admission to the first thickness the ellipsometry resolved: not stated; Figure 3 starts at 3 to 13 nm.
- Total oxidation time of each run: not stated. Times here are integrals of the read rates.
- Cooling: not stated (it does not enter in-situ data).

### What the second reading changed

- The project held this paper as “abstract read … nothing digitized”, with no stored recipe and no dataset. The rates and parameters here are the second reader's readings of Figures 3 to 5.
- Text p. 6: activation energies of k0 “0.68, 1.37 and 2.02” for the “Si-, a- and C-faces”; Figure 4(a) labels 2.02 eV on the Si-face line and 0.68 eV on the C-face line, and the paper's own argument (three Si back-bonds on the Si face, one on the C face) requires the figure's order.
- L_S: 1.2 nm at 1100 °C for both faces in the text (for Figure 2), about 2.3 nm at 1100 °C on Figure 5(b) (for Figure 3).
- The Si face here is 8° off axis; Song's, whose constants the program uses, was on axis. The C face is 0.5° off.

## Their terms and ours

| The paper says | We write | Kind in our vocabulary | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| oxide growth rate dX/dt | `(none: d(x_ox)/dt is internal to the program)` | not an observable | A rate against thickness cannot be scored; it was integrated to thickness against time. |
| k0: initial interfacial oxidation rate | `B/A of the linear-parabolic law is its limit k_min C_O(1−ν_Si)/N0` | PARAMETER | Fitted in the paper; graph only (Figure 4a). |
| ν_Si, ν_C: emission ratios | `(none)` | not in vocabulary | The share of Si and C atoms leaving the interface as interstitials instead of oxidizing. |
| C_Si^I, C_C^I: interstitial concentrations at the interface | `would be FIELDs on a grid in the oxide` | FIELD | Our grids have fixed length; the oxide's grows. |
| R_S, L_S: enhanced surface oxide growth | `an added term R_S exp(−X/L_S) in d(x_ox)/dt` | OPERATOR kind OxideGrowth | The steady-state limit of the model is the Massoud relation. |
| Si and C emission model | `(candidate structure)` | operator structure | See the alignment section: what the language can and cannot carry. |
| in-situ spectroscopic ellipsometry, three-layer model | `Thickness` | observable | An interface layer is part of the optical model and is not reported. |
| oxygen partial pressure 0.02–1 atm | `mix:O2=p,Ar=1−p (diluent not stated)` | ambient by partial pressure | Not run here. |

The model itself, `experiments/song_sic/oxide_growth_sic.isl.template`, as it is written. Every equation the simulation uses is in this text; the runtime holds no physics.

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: DRY THERMAL OXIDATION OF 4H-SiC (growth sector on a second material)
#
# The OPERATOR and OBSERVATION_MODEL blocks below are copied character for character
# from experiments/vitkavage_dit/oxide_growth.isl.template, the silicon program; a test
# checks that they are identical. Only the declared parameters differ.
#
# Song et al. (2004) fit their SiC data with the same linear-parabolic law, reading its
# two constants as including out-diffusion of the carbon product. The constants here are
# Arrhenius fits to that paper's Tables I and II, per crystal face
# (scripts/build_song_dataset.py). Nothing is fitted by us.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_song_sic.py.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE {sample_id} {{
    FAMILY: SiC_SiO2
    ORIENTATION: 4H_SiC_{face}_face
}}
STATE x_ox {{
    UNIT: cm
    INITIAL: x_i
    MIN: 0
    MAX: 1.0e-3
}}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_O2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER B0 {{ VALUE: {B0}; UNIT: cm^2 s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
PARAMETER E_B {{ VALUE: {E_B}; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
PARAMETER BA0 {{ VALUE: {BA0}; UNIT: cm s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
PARAMETER E_BA {{ VALUE: {E_BA}; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
# The constants above are already specific to the crystal face, so no further factor is applied.
PARAMETER r_or {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: 1; STATUS: constant }}
# The paper sets the time offset to zero and reports a native oxide of about 2 angstrom.
PARAMETER x_i {{ VALUE: 2.0e-8; UNIT: cm; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Song et al., J. Appl. Phys. 95, 4953 (2004), native oxide about 2 angstrom, agent-read }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY {trajectory_id} {{
    RUN Oxidation DURATION {duration_s} TEMP {T_C}->{T_C} AMBIENT dry_O2
}}
# dx/dt = B / (2x + A), with A = B / (B/A).
OPERATOR F_LinearParabolicGrowth : OxideGrowth {{
    EQUATION: d(x_ox)/dt = (p_O2 / p_ref) * B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (2 * x_ox + B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (r_or * BA0 * exp(-E_BA / (k_B * T))))
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Oxide_Thickness {{
    TARGET: Thickness
    UNIT: cm
    EQUATION: Thickness = x_ox
}}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT {contract_id} {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/song_2004_sic_thickness.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{dataset_sha256}
    SPLIT: {split}
{targets}
}}
```

**How the comparison is made.** Figure 3 gives growth rate against thickness at 1 atm. The second reader read 7 to 12 points per curve; the time between points is the integral of 1/rate over thickness (trapezoid rule), which turns each curve into thickness against time measured from its first point. The history anchors that first point where the program's own constants put it (the time the program needs to grow the first read thickness, 3 to 13 nm), so the scored points test the growth from there on, not the start. Points just after the anchor agree almost by construction; the last point of each curve is the real test. Every number is labelled outside the tested scope: 1200 °C and 900 °C are outside Song's 950 to 1150 °C, and the argon heat-up trips the program's scope check (see the gaps below).

**As reproduced: 29 of 73 within 10%, median error 13.5%. The two faces err in opposite directions.** Si face: the program is too slow, and more so as the oxide thickens; last points −36% (1200 °C, 98 nm), −33% (1150 °C, 56 nm), −17% (1100 °C, 29 nm), −17% (1050 °C, 14 nm). At 1150 °C and 43 nm Goto and Hijikata measure 11.0 nm/h; the program's rate there is B/(2X + A) = 4.3 nm/h. C face: the program is too fast at 15 to 100 nm, by up to +74% at 1200 °C and +73% at 1100 °C; at 1100 °C and 32 nm they measure 26 nm/h, the program gives 65 nm/h. The gap closes as the oxide thickens (+20% at 330 nm, where the rates are 10.8 and 13.1 nm/h) and at low temperature (900 °C: −13% at the last point).

**What was searched.** One unknown: the anchor, a factor from 0.25 to 4 on the time to the first read point, shared by every curve; chosen on the 1100 °C curves (20 points), tested on the other six (53 points). It is not flat: 8 of 20 at the program's own anchor, 5 at 0.63, 4 at 1.59, 0 at 0.25. The search keeps the program's own anchor. Held-out: 21 of 53 before and after. No time offset rescues a curve whose slope is wrong in the middle, and on these two faces the slope is wrong in opposite directions.

**What it says about Song's constants.** On the C face, Song's B and B/A transfer to Gupta and Akhtar's furnace (their page) and not to Goto and Hijikata's in-situ data. The C-face difference is largest at 20 to 100 nm, the region Goto and Hijikata call the interface-oxidation stage with deceleration; Song's linear-parabolic fit has no deceleration and Gupta and Akhtar's first point is already 21 to 78 nm. On the Si face, both other laboratories grow faster than Song's constants allow (Gupta and Akhtar by about 1.4 times in thickness, Goto and Hijikata's 8° off-axis wafers by up to 2.5 times in rate at 40 nm). The Si-face constants are the weak part of the program.

**Heating and cooling transitions** (above): no number moves. The argon heat-up and the assumed nitrogen transitions grow nothing in this program, and in-situ data have no cool-down.

**Can the project's growth law carry it?** Partly. (1) The steady-state limit (the paper says it is Massoud's relation) can be written today: one more term R_S exp(−X/L_S) in d(x_ox)/dt beside B/(2x + A). With Song's Si-face constants and Goto and Hijikata's R_S = 32 nm/h, L_S = 2.3 nm at 1100 °C, the rate rises from 10.3 to 13.9 nm/h at 5 nm (measured 12.5) and is unchanged above 10 nm, where the Si-face deficit is (3.6 against 5.3 nm/h at 20 nm). So the term fixes the first nanometres, not the Si face. (2) The substrate side (eqs. 9 to 12) can be written in the language as it is: a grid in SiC that travels with the front, a boundary flux ν^b N_s d(x_ox)/dt at the interface, a vacancy field and a recombination source. (3) The oxide side cannot: its fields live on a layer whose thickness is the growing state x_ox, and the language's grids have fixed length (“a layer whose own thickness changes, such as the growing oxide itself, has no grid of varying length yet”). The interface concentrations would be `left(C_Si)`, which exists; the absorption integral ∫ κ C_Si C_O dx needs the integral of a product of two fields, which does not.

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- A grid whose length is a state (`GRID oxide { FROM: 0; TO: x_ox; CELLS: 50 }`), or a stretched coordinate with position-dependent velocity, so interstitial and oxidant profiles can live in the growing oxide. This is the one change that would let the Si and C emission model be written as a candidate structure beside the linear-parabolic law.
- `integral(expr)` over a product of fields (the paper's ∫ κ C_Si C_O dx), not only `integral(F)` of one field.
- A growth rate against thickness as an observable (`OBSERVE rate OF x_ox AT Thickness`), so in-situ rate data can be scored directly instead of integrated into times that carry every reading error.
- A time origin at gas admission and an observation that starts later (`MEASURE … FROM first_resolved_thickness`): the paper's clock starts when argon is replaced by oxygen, its data at 3 to 13 nm; we had to anchor the clock with the program's own constants.
- OFFCUT read by the growth program: this Si face is 8° off axis, Song's on axis, and Song's Si-face constants are up to 36% slow here. The C face is 0.5° off.
- The sic_growth scope check marks a history outside its tested scope when any step is not dry oxygen, including an argon heat-up that grows nothing. It should check only the steps where oxide grows (and the temperatures of those).

## With heating and cooling transitions

No paper we hold states how its wafers heated up or cooled down. The project owner specified scenarios for one Vitkavage wafer (`isl_harness/vitkavage_transitions.py`); `isl_harness/thermal_transitions.py` applies the same numbers to every sample here: a heat-up in nitrogen before the first furnace step (a hot push of 1 to 5 min, or a cold-furnace ramp of 60 to 120 min), the owner's radiative cooling knots after the last furnace step or after the paper's own pull (fast, or every time multiplied by 5), and ramps around an anneal at 300 to 500 °C. The paper's own steps are kept as written. All transitions are assumptions, written in the history with `TIMING assumed`.
