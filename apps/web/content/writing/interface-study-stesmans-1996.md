# Reproducing Stesmans and Afanas'ev 1996: Pb defects released from hydrogen and created by vacuum anneals

*One of the [studies we reproduced](/writing/interface-studies-reproduced) for [Questions we need Emily to answer](/writing/questions-for-emily-testing-a-theory-of-the-interface), the companion to [the essay](/writing/testing-a-theory-of-the-interface-with-an-ontology). Generated by `scripts/reproduce_studies.py` from `reproductions/stesmans_1996.py`. Every value from the paper was read by an AI agent and has not been checked by a person; the sample and history were re-read from the PDF alone by a second agent that had not seen our earlier transcription.*

## The study and why we depend on it

**A. Stesmans and V. V. Afanas'ev, “Creation of Pb interface defects in thermal Si/SiO2 through annealing”, J. Phys.: Condens. Matter 8, L505-L509 (1996).**

The paper the Quantum ESPRESSO calculation bears on most directly. It measures the Pb centre itself (the dangling bond with three silicon back-bonds that the 14-atom cluster models) after a full hydrogen passivation and after anneals that drive the hydrogen off, and it quotes the measured barriers for both directions: passivation in H2 with 1.66 eV and dissociation of PbH with 2.56 eV (Brower). It is also the source of the project's finding that vacuum anneals above about 640 °C create dangling bonds (ontology/findings.json, bonds-created). Nothing was scored against it before this page.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Moderate.** The anneal series is stated precisely: a fresh, fully hydrogen-passivated sample for each temperature, 62 min in a diffusion-pumped vacuum (≤4×10⁻⁷ Torr), temperature to 0.3%. The oxidation is stated by temperature, pressure and thickness only (about 970 °C, 1.1 atm O2, about 42 nm), the passivation as about 40 min at 405 °C in 1.1 atm H2. Not stated: oxidation time, cooling, the clean, and any heat-up or cool-down of the anneals. The results exist as one figure; three values are printed in the text (N0 = 4.7×10¹², 12.3×10¹² after 968 °C, Nc = 7.6×10¹² at 960 °C) and the fitted kinetic parameters are given.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- Oxidation time: not stated (about 42 nm at about 970 °C).
- Cooling after oxidation and after the passivation: gas, rate, end temperature not stated.
- Passivation: “∼40 min”; heat-up and cool-down not stated; for the cycled sample, the repassivation time is not stated at all.
- Vacuum anneals: 62 min hold stated; heat-up and cool-down of the slices not stated (in a diffusion-pumped vacuum a small slice heats by radiation).
- Time between passivation, anneal and ESR not stated.

### What the second reading changed

- The project had no stored recipe and no dataset for this paper; SOURCE.md summarises the text and says Figure 1 was not digitized. The values in SOURCE.md agree with this reading.
- SOURCE.md says “Up to about 640 C the density recovers to the as-oxidized pool, 4.7e12”. The 4.7×10¹² is the authors' fit (N0) under the HPb-dissociation model, and the paper calls it “the natural Pb bath … found in the as-oxidized state” (p. L508); no as-oxidized measurement before passivation is shown.
- evaluations/atomistic/si_dangling_bond_hydrogen.json names this paper as giving the measured passivation and dissociation barriers. It quotes them (1.66 and 2.56 eV) from Brower 1988-1990, which the project does not hold; the paper's own fits are 2.63 eV with a 0.105 eV spread (fresh oxide) and 2.71 eV (cycled sample), both with Brower's prefactor 1.2×10¹² s⁻¹ adopted, not fitted.
- ontology/findings.json (bonds-created) scopes the creation to “(111), vacuum, 62 min above about 640 C”. The paper puts the onset of creation at about 640-660 °C and says the same happens in N2 and Ar.

| Pb (Si≡Si3), ESR-active interfacial dangling bond | `N_free (equilibrium program); N_Pb (trap-density program)` | STATE; quantity density-of-dangling-bonds | Neither program reports a spin density in cm⁻². |
| HPb (hydrogen-passivated Pb) | `N_capped; N_SiH` | STATE |  |
| Pb/N0, eq. (3) | `share of dangling bonds left uncapped (Normalized_Pb)` | observable | The paper's N0 is fitted (4.7×10¹²) or the plateau (12.3×10¹²). |
| passivation Pb + H2 → HPb + H, Ea ≈ 1.66 eV | `F_HydrogenCapping (equilibrium program, rate k_eq·√p_H2, k_eq hypothesis); F_HydrogenPassivation (trap program, rate 10^log_k_H·p_H2 fitted at 400 °C, times exp(−(Ef/k)(1/T − 1/673 K)) with Ef = 1.66 eV, Brower's, sourced)` | OPERATOR kind Passivation | The trap program carries the measured 1.66 eV about its fitted 400 °C rate; the equilibrium program has no activation energy. |
| dissociation HPb → Pb + H, Ed = 2.56 eV, kd0 = 1.2×10¹² s⁻¹; spread σEd | `uncapping at k_eq/K_eq (equilibrium program); F_HydrogenRelease, kd0·exp(−Ed/kT)·N_SiH (trap program)` | OPERATOR kind Passivation (reverse) | The equilibrium program's uncapping rate is set by detailed balance from the computed energy and the hypothesis k_eq; the trap program's is Brower's single barrier, Ed = 2.56 eV and kd0 = 1.2×10¹² s⁻¹ (sourced), with no spread σEd. |
| Pb creation, Nc, irreversible, T ⩾ 660 °C in O-free ambient | `(none)` | no operator | The trap program's relaxation moves N_Pb toward a floor of 4.8×10¹⁰; it has no creation. |
| diffusion-pumped vacuum ⩽4×10⁻⁷ Torr | `AMBIENT vacuum` | ambient | No pressure is carried. |
| 1.1 atm H2, 1.1 atm O2 | `pure_H2; dry_O2 (1 atm)` | ambient by partial pressure | The runtime refuses a partial pressure above 1 atm. |
| reoxidation (only cure for created Pb) | `(none)` | no operator |  |

```isl

# ISL MODEL: HOW FAR HYDROGEN CAN CAP SILICON DANGLING BONDS AT EQUILIBRIUM
#
# Si* + 1/2 H2 <-> Si-H. The energy of the reaction is a COMPUTED parameter: a Quantum ESPRESSO
# calculation on a small cluster (atomistic/calcs/si_dangling_bond_hydrogen.json), not a measurement
# and not a fit. With the free energy of hydrogen gas it gives the equilibrium constant, and so the
# share of dangling bonds that hydrogen at a given temperature and pressure would leave uncapped if
# the anneal were long enough to reach equilibrium.
#
# What this is for: a bound. The trap-density program has a fitted share of sites that the
# hydrogen anneal does not remove. If equilibrium already leaves that share, the residue is
# thermodynamic; if equilibrium leaves far less, the residue is kinetic or is another defect.
# What it is not: a rate. How fast equilibrium is approached is the hypothesis k_eq below.
#
# Used by isl_harness/predict.py only when computed parameters are switched on (ISL_USE_COMPUTED=1
# or --computed). Fields in {{braces}} are filled from evaluations/atomistic/<id>.json.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE {sample_id} {{
    FAMILY: Si_Planar
}}
# Fractions of the sites: uncapped (electrically active) and capped by hydrogen.
STATE N_free {{ UNIT: 1; INITIAL: 1; MIN: 0; MAX: 1.000001 }}
STATE N_capped {{ UNIT: 1; INITIAL: 0; MIN: 0; MAX: 1.000001 }}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_H2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER dE_cap {{ VALUE: {dE_cap}; UNIT: eV; STATUS: computed; SOURCE: {source} }}
# Zero-point energy of the Si-H bond (one stretch, two bends) less half that of H2. Recalled from vibrational frequencies
# (about 2100 and 630 cm^-1 for Si-H, 4400 cm^-1 for H2), not from a source in the library and not yet computed (ph.x can).
PARAMETER dZPE {{ VALUE: 0.07; UNIT: eV; STATUS: hypothesis }}
# Hydrogen gas as an ideal diatomic: standard entropy 130.7 J/mol/K at 298.15 K and 1 atm, heat capacity 3.5 k_B.
# Textbook values, recalled, not from a source in the library.
PARAMETER S_ref {{ VALUE: 1.3546e-3; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: hypothesis }}
PARAMETER c_p {{ VALUE: 3.0161e-4; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: hypothesis }}
PARAMETER T_ref {{ VALUE: 298.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
# How fast equilibrium is approached: assumed fast against any anneal. The answer is the equilibrium, not the path.
PARAMETER k_eq {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: s^-1; STATUS: hypothesis }}
# Free energy of one H2 molecule at 1 atm above its energy at rest: enthalpy 3.5 k_B T less T times the entropy.
QUANTITY mu_H2 {{ UNIT: eV; EQUATION: mu_H2 = c_p * T - T * (S_ref + c_p * log(T / T_ref)) }}
QUANTITY dG {{ UNIT: eV; EQUATION: dG = dE_cap + dZPE - 0.5 * mu_H2 }}
QUANTITY K_eq {{ UNIT: 1; EQUATION: K_eq = exp(-dG / (k_B * T)) }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY {trajectory_id} {{
    RUN PostMetalAnneal DURATION 1800 TEMP 400->400 AMBIENT forming_gas_H2
}}
# Capping at a rate proportional to the square root of the hydrogen pressure; uncapping at the rate detailed balance requires.
OPERATOR F_HydrogenCapping : Passivation {{
    EQUATION: d(N_free)/dt = -k_eq * (sqrt(p_H2 / p_ref) * N_free - N_capped / K_eq)
    EQUATION: d(N_capped)/dt = k_eq * (sqrt(p_H2 / p_ref) * N_free - N_capped / K_eq)
    CONSERVATION: N_free + N_capped = const
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Uncapped {{
    TARGET: Normalized_Pb
    UNIT: 1
    EQUATION: Normalized_Pb = N_free
}}
```

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **A kinetic hydrogen program: what it still needs.** The trap-density program now has both forward rates with Brower's numbers: passivation Pb + H₂ → HPb + H (its fitted 400 °C rate times exp(−(Ef/k)(1/T − 1/T_H)), Ef 1.66 eV) and release HPb → Pb + H (kd = kd0·exp(−Ed/kT), Ed 2.56 eV, kd0 1.2×10¹² s⁻¹). Still missing: a state for the released hydrogen (in the oxide, at the interface, or recombined to H₂) so that the reverse rates follow from detailed balance with the computed reaction energies instead of a placeholder; a Gaussian spread of Ed (σ 0.105 eV), which needs a STATE distributed over a parameter (e.g. `STATE N_HPb { DISTRIBUTED: Ed GAUSSIAN(2.63, 0.105) }`) since one exponential cannot give the measured breadth with the measured barrier; and a creation term above about 650 °C in oxygen-free gas. With the barriers written `PARAMETER Ed { STATUS: computed; SOURCE: evaluations/atomistic/<neb record> }` instead of sourced from Brower, the measured values from Brower and this paper would be the test, not the input.
- **Which barriers Quantum ESPRESSO can compute on the H100, and how.** The cluster already relaxed (Si₄H₉/Si₄H₁₀, PBEsol, 40/320 Ry, spin-polarized) is the starting point; neb.x (climbing image, 7 to 9 images) does the rest. (1) Passivation, Pb + H₂ → HPb + H, on the cluster: H₂ approaching the dangling bond, final state HPb with a free H; this is small (15 to 16 atoms) and runs in hours, and gives a first number to set beside 1.66 eV. (2) Dissociation into a bound site: on the bare cluster HPb → Pb + H has no barrier beyond its 3.54 eV endothermicity, so the hydrogen needs somewhere to go. That needs an interface model: a (111) Si slab with a thin oxide (for example 4 to 6 Si bilayers under 1 to 1.5 nm of SiO₂ built from tridymite or a relaxed amorphous cell, about 150 to 250 atoms) with one Pb pointing into an oxide void, and final states at a back-bond centre, an oxide interstitial, and HPb + H → Pb + H₂ with a second H nearby. On one H100 an SCF of that size takes minutes; a 9-image NEB of 50 to 100 iterations is one to three days per path. (3) Prefactors: ph.x at the initial state and the saddle (harmonic transition state theory, Vineyard) gives kd0 to compare with 1.2×10¹² s⁻¹, and the same with deuterium masses gives the isotope effect Johnson 1981 needs. (4) The spread: the same path at several Pb sites of an amorphous interface gives a distribution of Ed to compare with σ = 0.105 eV. Each result enters the program as `STATUS: computed` and is scored against this page's 16 rows.
- **Partial pressures above 1 atm.** The paper oxidizes and passivates in 1.1 atm; the runtime refuses any partial pressure above 1 (`mix:H2=1.1` is rejected). Allowing it would let the stated pressure through; in the equilibrium program it moves the uncapped share by a factor 1/√1.1.
- **A vacuum with a pressure.** “Diffusion-pumped vacuum (⩽4×10⁻⁷ Torr)” is written as `vacuum`. Johnson 1981's vacuum, which removes Pb where this one restores it, has no pressure at all. `AMBIENT vacuum PRESSURE 4e-7 Torr` and a residual-gas composition would let the two be told apart; no program reads either yet.
- **One sample annealed in a series.** The cycled sample is one slice taken through passivation and anneal at rising temperatures with ESR in between. Each history here is cut to its last cycle. A `REPEAT … FOR T IN …` with an observation after each pass would state the sequence and let a program with memory (created Pb) be tested on it.
- **An observable named for what it is.** The program's target is called the share left uncapped “at equilibrium”, and after a vacuum anneal it is not: it is the rate hypothesis. The predictor should report the equilibrium share (1 in vacuum, the bound in hydrogen) separately from the share at the end of the history, so a page cannot read one as the other.

## With Quantum ESPRESSO switched on

This page was simulated with the computed parameters switched on (`ISL_USE_COMPUTED=1`): the energy of Si• + ½H₂ → Si–H, −1.284 eV, and its zero-point correction, +0.079 eV, both from Quantum ESPRESSO (PBEsol) on a 14-atom cluster, Si₄H₉ with the dangling bond and Si₄H₁₀ capped (`evaluations/atomistic/si_dangling_bond_hydrogen.json`, `si_dangling_bond_hydrogen_vibrations.json`). The same calculation gives the energy to pull the hydrogen off into vacuum, D(Si–H) = 3.54 eV, and the H₂ binding energy, 4.52 eV. No barrier was computed. Every Pb share scored above comes from the program these numbers feed, `experiments/atomistic/hydrogen_passivation_equilibrium.isl.template`.

**Reaction energies against the measured barriers.** The paper quotes two measured activation energies (Brower): passivation Pb + H₂ → HPb + H, 1.66 eV, and dissociation HPb → Pb + H, 2.56 eV; it fits 2.63 eV with a 0.105 eV spread on its own fresh oxides and 2.71 eV on the cycled sample, all with Brower's prefactor 1.2×10¹² s⁻¹. A barrier cannot be below the energy the reaction must climb. From the calculation, passivation by H₂ climbs D(H₂) − D(Si–H) = 0.98 eV if the second H is left free: 1.66 eV is above that, consistent. Dissociation to a free H atom climbs 3.54 eV, a full eV above the measured 2.56-2.71 eV; dissociation to half an H₂ molecule climbs 1.28 eV (1.21 eV with the zero-point correction), below it. So the measured barrier is consistent with the cluster only if the hydrogen that leaves is caught at once, bound by at least 1 eV relative to a free atom (in the oxide or at the interface, or by a second H forming H₂), or if a real Pb holds its hydrogen about 1 eV less strongly than the cluster. Johnson 1981's SIMS, deuterium held at the interface to above 500 °C, is direct evidence for such bound sites. The two measured barriers together (1.66 + 2.56 = 4.22 eV, the cycle H₂ → 2H) are 0.3 eV short of the computed 4.52 eV, which says the same thing.

**The equilibrium bound after the hydrogen anneal.** At 405 °C in 1 atm of H₂ equilibrium leaves 2.1×10⁻⁶ of the dangling bonds uncapped (2.0×10⁻⁶ at the paper's 1.1 atm), about 10⁷ cm⁻² of the 4.7×10¹² bath. The paper says the bath was “fully passivated … as affirmed by ESR”: consistent, and the bound is far below anything ESR resolves, so the measurement cannot test it. The 40 min anneal reaching full passivation also says that at 405 °C the passivation rate is not the limit, which a 1.66 eV barrier allows.

**After the dissociating anneals the bound says nothing.** In vacuum there is no hydrogen gas, so equilibrium is all bonds uncapped, a share of 1. Up to 645 °C the measured share stays below 1 (0.17 to 0.94), which is consistent and uninformative: how far the bonds get is set by the dissociation barrier and the 62 min. Above 660 °C the measured share exceeds 1 (1.5 to 3.5): Pb is created, and a share of a fixed population cannot express that. The paper's statement that the same happens in N₂ and Ar fits the same picture: no hydrogen, equilibrium fully uncapped, kinetics decide.

**What the program reports after a vacuum anneal is not a bound.** Its capping operator also uncaps, at the rate k_eq/K_eq that detailed balance requires, with k_eq = 1 s⁻¹ a hypothesis (“assumed fast against any anneal”). In vacuum that rate is the whole answer. From 480 to 650 °C it behaves as an Arrhenius law with 1.33 eV and 1.6×10⁴ s⁻¹; the measured law is 2.6 eV and 1.2×10¹² s⁻¹. The two cross near 515 °C and stay within a factor of 3 from 480 to 560 °C; above that both are fast enough to saturate in 62 min. That is why five of six fresh-oxide points fall within 10%. Outside this window they part: at 400 °C the program's rate is 22 times Brower's, at 700 °C a thirtieth of it. The match is not evidence for the program; it shows that one isochronal series cannot separate an activation energy from a prefactor, and that a spread of barriers (the paper's σ = 0.105 eV) and a lower single barrier broaden the curve alike.

**The table above.** “Our fitted program” is the trap-density program after the full history. The hydrogen anneal takes it to 6.4×10⁶ cm⁻² eV⁻¹ (2.5×10⁻⁵ of the 2.61×10¹¹ it has without that anneal); its release, Brower's 2.56 eV and 1.2×10¹² s⁻¹, then gives back 0.21, 0.61, 0.94 and 0.99 of it after 62 min at 520, 550, 575 and 590 °C, and all of it from 620 °C. Scored as Pb shares against the paper that is 2 of 15 within tolerance (645 °C fresh and 700 °C cycled), median error 47%: the onset is right and the rise too steep. Above 750 °C its density falls (1.9×10¹¹ at 968 °C, 5.2×10¹⁰ at 1125 °C), because its relaxation pulls the population toward its fitted floor (4.8×10¹⁰ cm⁻²), where the paper creates Pb. “Computed equilibrium bound” is the predictor's product of the trap program without the hydrogen step and the share at the end of the history; after a vacuum anneal that share is the hypothesis rate above, so the column is not a bound on this page. There is no interface-state density in this paper to set beside it.

## What we would like Emily to check

1. The paper adopts Brower's prefactor 1.2×10¹² s⁻¹ and fits only Ed and a spread. On one isochronal series a prefactor and an activation energy trade against each other; is there an isothermal series (Brower 1990, 500-590 °C) we should hold to separate them?
2. Brower's dissociation energy, 2.56 eV, is about 1 eV below the computed energy to pull H off the cluster into vacuum (3.54 eV). Is the accepted picture that the released H is bound elsewhere in the oxide or interface (so the barrier is below the free-atom limit), or that the dissociation is assisted (HPb + H → Pb + H2)?
3. Is 4.7×10¹² cm⁻² the usual natural Pb density for dry (111) oxides near 970 °C? Johnson 1981 reports about 6×10¹¹ as oxidized on (111) at 1000 °C.
