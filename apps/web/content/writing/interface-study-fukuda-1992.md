## The study and why we depend on it

**H. Fukuda, M. Yasuda, T. Iwabuchi, S. Kaneko, T. Ueno and I. Ohdomari, “Process dependence of the SiO2/Si(100) interface trap density of ultrathin SiO2 films”, J. Appl. Phys. 72, 1906 (1992).**

Rapid thermal oxides of 4 to 11 nm on n-type (100) silicon, grown in seconds at 1000 to 1200 °C, are the shortest and hottest planar histories the project holds. The paper finds that midgap trap density falls exponentially during oxidation, with a time constant of 3 to 17 s, from a starting value that is the same at every temperature, that fixed charge follows it, and that 10 to 120 s anneals in deuterium, argon or vacuum first cut the density about ten times and then let it rise. `scripts/analyse_fukuda.py` used Figure 3 to show that the trap-density program's clock, fitted on furnace oxides, is about 170 times too slow here. This page runs the predictor's own programs on the paper's histories, written with the lamp ramp, the cooling and the two gate steps the stored analysis leaves out.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Low.** The oxidation temperature and time and the anneal gas, temperature and time are given for every point, which is more than most papers give. Everything around them is not: the lamp ramp, whether the stated time includes it, the gas and rate of cooling, whether the anneals were in situ, and the time of the two steps after every anneal, a 620 °C polysilicon deposition and a 900 °C phosphorus doping. Thickness is not given per oxidation time. For seconds-long steps at 1000 to 1200 °C these unstated parts are as long as the stated ones.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- The lamp heat-up: rate, gas, and whether the stated 1 to 100 s includes it. At 5 to 10 s and 1200 °C a 10 s ramp is as long as the oxidation.
- The cooling after every oxidation and anneal: rate, gas and where it ends.
- No thickness per oxidation time: Figures 2 and 3 give the same densities against thickness and against time without saying which point is which.
- Whether the deuterium and argon anneals followed the oxidation in the same chamber, and how they heated up.
- How long the oxygen took to pump out and the lamps to drop from the oxidation temperature to 900 °C before the vacuum anneal.
- The polysilicon deposition at 620 °C (350 nm; time not stated) and the phosphorus doping at 900 °C (time, source and gas not stated), after every anneal and before every measurement.

### What the second reading changed

- The project held no stored recipe for this paper: `scripts/analyse_fukuda.py` fits closed-form laws to Figure 3 with the oxidation time as the only history. This page adds the heat-up, cooling, gate deposition and doping as RUNs, all with stated or searched ranges.
- The project's Figure 3 reading (data/primary_sources/fukuda_1992_jap_72_1906/figure3_agent_read_UNVERIFIED.csv) and the second reading agree within 5% on all 14 points (for example 5.0 against 5.1×10^11 at 1000 °C, 10 s); Figures 2 and 7 agree within 5%.
- Figures 4, 5 and 6 were not digitized in the project; the second reading reads all three. Figure 5 (deuterium) and Figure 6 (fixed charge) are on this page for the first time.
- SOURCE.md calls the argon 500 °C result an inconsistency between abstract and text. The text (PDF p. 5) says Dit-m “decreases at the initial stage down to 9.5×10^9” then rises; the abstract says argon at 500 °C “did not affect the density at all”. Figure 7 agrees with the text.
- The library records the pull as “rapid thermal, lamp cooled”. The paper says nothing about cooling.
- The project's analysis paired Figures 2 and 3 implicitly (“the same samples against thickness and against time”). The second reading finds the pairing exact at 1000 °C and for three of four points at 1100 °C; at 1200 °C Figure 3 has six points and Figure 2 five, and they cannot be paired.

# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: FIXED CHARGE WITH AN EQUILIBRIUM IN EACH GAS
#
# In oxygen the charge moves toward a steady value that falls with temperature (the
# line of Deal et al. 1967, unchanged, with the oxygen kinetics fitted there).
# In a gas without oxygen it moves toward a lower equilibrium that also falls with
# temperature, Qi_ref * exp(E_q / k_B * (1/T - 1/T_iref)), at a rate of its own.
#
# The inert clock and the (100) inert equilibrium are fitted to the argon time series
# of Akinwande, Ho and Plummer (1982). The (111) inert equilibrium is fitted to their
# cycling experiment, with the clock carried over from (100). The model is then
# tested, unchanged, on the Fairchild wafers of 1967 and 1980.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_fixed_charge_two_lines.py.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE Wafer_{tag} {{
    FAMILY: Si_Planar
    ORIENTATION: Si{orientation}
}}
# The wafer starts at the steady value for its oxidation temperature.
STATE Q_f {{ UNIT: cm^-2; INITIAL: Q_hot + slope * (T_hot - T_ox); MIN: 0; MAX: 1.0e14 }}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_O2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER Q_hot {{ VALUE: {Q_hot}; UNIT: cm^-2; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal et al. 1967 Table II, steady value after dry oxidation at 1200 C }}
PARAMETER slope {{ VALUE: {slope}; UNIT: cm^-2 K^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal et al. 1967 Table II, line through the 1200 C and 920 C steady values }}
PARAMETER T_hot {{ VALUE: 1473.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER T_ox {{ VALUE: {T_ox_K}; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
# ---- fitted to the pull-rate series ------------------------------------------------
PARAMETER k0 {{ VALUE: 1.0e3; UNIT: s^-1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_a {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
# ---- fitted to the argon anneals of Akinwande, Ho and Plummer (1982) -----------------
PARAMETER k_i0 {{ VALUE: 1.0e1; UNIT: s^-1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_i {{ VALUE: 0.9; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER Qi_ref {{ VALUE: 2.5e10; UNIT: cm^-2; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_q {{ VALUE: 0.4; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER T_iref {{ VALUE: 1273.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
# ---- where the exchange with oxygen stops as the wafer cools (hypothesis, added 10 November 2026) ----------------------
# The oxygen rate above was fitted with an activation energy at its lower bound (0.05 eV), so on its own it keeps moving
# charge at room temperature. Every history it was fitted and tested on ended its pull at 400 C, a cut-off that stood in for
# this. Reproducing Stanford 1982, Deal 1967 and Deal and Razouk 1980 with a wafer cooling to room temperature made the charge
# 3 to 5 times too high. The cut-off is now stated: the exchange is switched off over about 20 K around T_freeze. At 400 C it
# is 99% on, so every fitted and tested history is unchanged; how high the true freezing point is, is an open question
# (the Deal and Razouk alignment prefers pulls ending near 530 C).
PARAMETER T_freeze {{ VALUE: 623.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: hypothesis }}
PARAMETER w_freeze {{ VALUE: 10.0; UNIT: K; STATUS: hypothesis }}
QUANTITY rate {{ UNIT: s^-1; EQUATION: rate = k0 * exp(-E_a / (k_B * T)) / (1 + exp(-(T - T_freeze) / w_freeze)) }}
QUANTITY rate_inert {{ UNIT: s^-1; EQUATION: rate_inert = k_i0 * exp(-E_i / (k_B * T)) }}
QUANTITY Q_inert {{ UNIT: cm^-2; EQUATION: Q_inert = Qi_ref * exp((E_q / k_B) * (1 / T - 1 / T_iref)) }}
QUANTITY oxidizing {{ UNIT: 1; EQUATION: oxidizing = p_O2 / p_ref }}
QUANTITY Q_steady {{ UNIT: cm^-2; EQUATION: Q_steady = Q_hot + slope * (T_hot - T) }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY History_{tag} {{
{runs}
}}
# In oxygen the charge follows the temperature-dependent steady value.
OPERATOR F_OxidizingEquilibration : Relaxation {{
    EQUATION: d(Q_f)/dt = -rate * oxidizing * (Q_f - Q_steady)
}}
# Without oxygen it relaxes, at its own rate, toward an equilibrium of its own that falls as temperature rises.
# Akinwande, Ho and Plummer (1982) show the annealed charge cycling reversibly with temperature in argon.
OPERATOR F_InertRelaxation : Relaxation {{
    EQUATION: d(Q_f)/dt = -rate_inert * (1 - oxidizing) * (Q_f - Q_inert)
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Fixed_Charge {{ TARGET: Qss; UNIT: cm^-2; EQUATION: Qss = Q_f }}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT Contract_{tag} {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/deal_1967_qss.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{dataset_sha256}
    SPLIT: {split}
    TARGET: Qss = {target} +/- {tolerance} abs
}}

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **Which step is the oxidation.** A lamp heat-up written in oxygen, as an RTP usually runs, makes the trap-density program read the oxidation temperature as 25 °C and refuse every history here; the heat-up had to be written in vacuum, which removes 0.2 to 1.6 nm of oxide the programs would otherwise grow. A `ROLE oxidation|ramp|anneal|pull` on each RUN would let programs find the step by what it is.
- **Isotopes and process gases.** Deuterium had to be written as hydrogen (`pure_H2`), silane as nitrogen, and the phosphorus source not at all: the language's gases are Ar, H2, H2O, N2 and O2. `mix:D2=1` and `mix:SiH4=…`, `POCl3` would record what was done; a program could then ignore them, or carry an isotope effect (the paper's SIMS counts deuterium).
- **A deposition and a doping as steps.** The 350 nm polysilicon at 620 °C and its 900 °C doping are written as nitrogen holds. A `DEPOSIT polysilicon 350 nm` statement that caps the oxide (no gas reaches it afterwards) would say why the gas does not matter and let a program ask whether hydrogen can leave through the cap, which is what the computed bound below depends on.
- **An observation's timing.** The trap density and charge were measured after the gate steps, not after the anneal the figure is about. The language ends the history at the measurement implicitly. An explicit `OBSERVE Dit, Nf AFTER <step>` would make the gap between the anneal and the measurement visible on the page and let a program answer at the anneal as well.
- **Thickness and time on separate figures.** Figures 2 and 3 are the same wafers against thickness and against time, never paired. A sample field `OXIDE_NM` read as a measured property beside a stated time would let each wafer carry both, and let a growth program be scored on it without a pairing of ours.
- **Temperature in the hydrogen rate.** Not a language gap but a program one the language exposes: the trap-density program's capping has no activation energy, so a long cooling in hydrogen (the transitions above) keeps capping at room temperature, and 500 and 900 °C anneals give the same answer.

## With Quantum ESPRESSO switched on

This page was simulated with parameters from atomic calculations switched on (`ISL_USE_COMPUTED=1`): the energy of Si• + ½H₂ → Si–H for a silicon dangling bond, computed with Quantum ESPRESSO on a 14-atom cluster, with the zero-point correction from its computed vibrations. With the free energy of hydrogen gas it bounds the share of dangling bonds a hydrogen step could leave uncapped at equilibrium. Deuterium is written as hydrogen; the bound has no isotope effect.

**Read the table below with care.** The bound is evaluated over the whole history, and here the deuterium anneal is followed by 40 min at 620 °C and 30 min at 900 °C without hydrogen (the gate deposition and doping). At equilibrium those steps release every capped bond, so the bound for every deuterium sample is the program's density without hydrogen, 3.17×10^11. The measured densities (1.8×10^9 to 2.9×10^10) are 10 to 170 times below it: the deuterium did not leave during the gate steps as equilibrium would require (the polysilicon cap, or a release much slower than the template's assumed 1 s⁻¹). SIMS finds 1.7×10^12 to 2.8×10^13 cm⁻² of deuterium in the oxide (PDF p. 4).

**At the end of the anneal itself** (a scratch run of the same histories cut after the anneal, `bound_fk.py`): at 500 °C the uncapped share is 3.4×10^-5, a bound of 1.1×10^7 cm⁻² eV⁻¹; the measured 1.8×10^9 to 9.8×10^9 are 160 to 900 times above it, so the 500 °C anneal is far from equilibrium. At 900 °C the share is 3.2% and the bound 1.06×10^10 (4.8×10^9 if it is scaled from the paper's own 1.5×10^11 before the anneal instead of the program's 3.3×10^11). The paper's 900 °C density starts below it (1.9×10^9 at 10 s) and rises through it (1.4×10^10 at 60 s, 2.9×10^10 at 120 s). That rise is the direction equilibrium at 900 °C requires: hydrogen leaving the dangling bonds. It is the release the authors call thermal emission of SiH and SiD (Eq. 6) and the release the fitted program lacks.
