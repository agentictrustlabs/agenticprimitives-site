## The study and why we depend on it

**N. M. Johnson, D. K. Biegelsen and M. D. Moyer, “Low-temperature annealing and hydrogenation of defects at the Si-SiO2 interface”, J. Vac. Sci. Technol. 19, 390-394 (1981).**

One of the two papers the Quantum ESPRESSO calculation of Si• + ½H₂ → Si–H bears on most directly. It measures the interface spin centre (Pb, the dangling bond the calculation models) and the interface states on the same (111) oxide, after 15 min anneals in vacuum with and without aluminium, and after atomic deuterium. Its central result is that the Pb centre goes at a lower temperature (below 300 °C with aluminium) than the characteristic interface-state peak (above 400 °C), so Pb is not the peak. The project held only five sentences of it as unverified text values; nothing was scored against it before this page.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Low.** The oxidation is stated by temperature, gas and thickness only (1000 °C, dry O2, 94 to 219 nm, no post-oxidation anneal, “rapidly cooled”); no oxidation time, no clean, no cooling rate. The anneals are stated (15 min in vacuum at each temperature; 400 °C 15 min and 450 °C 60 min for the capacitors), but not the vacuum pressure, the ramps, or whether the isochronal series is one specimen annealed in turn. The two hydrogen sources that drive the result, hydrogen released by the aluminium and atomic deuterium from a remote plasma, are described qualitatively: no flux, no pressure, no hydrogen content. The spin density is in arbitrary units except one absolute value (about 6×10¹¹ cm⁻² as oxidized).

#### Timing the paper does not give

- Oxidation time for every oxide (116, 200, 94, 219 nm at 1000 °C): not stated.
- Cooling after oxidation: “rapidly cooled to room temperature in the furnace ambient”, no rate or time.
- Vacuum anneals: heat-up, cool-down and pressure not stated; only the 15 or 60 min hold.
- Whether the isochronal anneals of Fig. 2 are on one specimen in sequence or one specimen per temperature: not stated.
- Atomic deuteration of the ESR specimens: “identical conditions” to the SIMS specimens, whose Fig. 5 legend gives 30 min; D flux, chamber pressure and plasma power not stated.
- Time between oxidation, aluminium evaporation and the anneals; how and when the aluminium was removed before ESR.

### What the second reading changed

- The project had no stored recipe for this paper: five sentences in data/primary_sources/johnson_biegelsen_moyer_1981_jvst_19_390/text_values_agent_read_UNVERIFIED.csv, no dataset, no script. Those five values agree with this reading.
- That file reads “onset of spin-centre loss with aluminium … 250 C”; Fig. 2 shows the with-Al density still at 1.0 at 250 °C and falling between 275 and 350 °C. 250 °C is where the fall begins, not where it is half done.
- The library note says the paper concerns “the dangling-bond centre removed by low-temperature anneals”. The removal without aluminium and without hydrogen above 400 °C in vacuum is in the paper (p. 2) and is unexplained there; it is opposite to Stesmans and Afanas'ev 1996, where a vacuum anneal of hydrogen-passivated (111) at 480-640 °C brings Pb back.
- The as-oxidized Pb density here, about 6×10¹¹ cm⁻² (p. 2), is eight times below the natural Pb density Stesmans and Afanas'ev give for a similar (111) dry oxide after full dissociation, 4.7×10¹² cm⁻². Johnson's wafers were not annealed to dissociate hydrogen before measurement, so part of their Pb may have been hydrogen-passivated as grown; the paper does not say.
- The evaluations/atomistic record lists this paper as giving a barrier for passivation or dissociation. It gives none: it reports temperatures at which signals disappear, in 15 min isochronal anneals, with no rate or activation energy.

## Their terms and ours

| The paper says | We write | Kind in our vocabulary | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| interface spin center; trivalent silicon defect bonded to three silicon atoms; g = 2.0015 | `N_Pb (trap-density program, internal); N_free (equilibrium program)` | STATE; quantity density-of-dangling-bonds | The trap-density program reports N_Pb only through Dit = c_mid·N_Pb; the equilibrium program reports the share N_free. Neither reports a spin density in cm⁻². |
| spin density (arbitrary units) | `share of dangling bonds left uncapped (computed)` | observable Normalized_Pb | Compared as a share of the level before the loss; the paper's absolute scale is given only for the as-oxidized wafer. |
| interface-state distribution, characteristic peak 0.3 eV above Ev | `Dit_at_0p30eV_above_Ev` | observable (target: trap_density) | The predictor gives a spectrum only after a hydrogen step (the shape of one Vitkavage curve). |
| interface states near midgap (DLTS, dashed) | `Dit` | observable (target: trap_density) | The paper warns that DLTS near midgap includes minority-carrier processes. |
| postmetallization anneal (PMA) in vacuo | `RUN VacuumAnneal … AMBIENT vacuum` | a RUN; process factor hydrogen-anneal (by the paper's mechanism) or inert-anneal (by its gas) | The ontology has no way to say a step in vacuum delivers hydrogen from a film. |
| anneal in atomic deuterium (remote plasma) | `RUN AtomicDeuterium … AMBIENT vacuum (stand-in)` | no ambient | Missing: an atomic hydrogen ambient with a flux. |
| aluminum overlayer releases hydrogen during sinter | `(none)` | no statement | Variant: written as forming gas. |
| rapidly cooled in the furnace ambient | `RUN Cool … AMBIENT dry_O2` | a RUN | Rate not stated. |
| deuterium accumulation at the interface (SIMS) | `(none)` | no observable | No program carries hydrogen in the oxide. |

```isl
Structure search for midgap trap density on planar silicon (Vitkavage 1990), with and without the hydrogen anneal.
What the measurements show before any model: the forming-gas anneal lowers the density 5 to 8 times on (100) but 10 to
29 times on (110), and on (110) the unannealed density does not fall with time at 1000 C while the annealed one does.
The grammar asks which structure accounts for that:
    one_population          sites relax with time at temperature; hydrogen caps them at one rate (the form used so far)

    two_populations         a second population set at oxidation, per orientation, that hydrogen removes and time does not
    two_populations_T       the same, with the second population depending on oxidation temperature
Every candidate is an ISL program (kinds Relaxation and Passivation). Split, fixed here: the 900 C recipes and the
8 h recipes are held out; everything else is fitted. (The earlier split held out the (511) orientation; a structure
with a number per orientation cannot be scored that way, so the split was changed and that is recorded here.)
The structure is chosen on training rows only: most passed, then fewest parameters, then smallest training error.
Output: evaluations/trap_density_search_report.json
"""
import json
import math
import os
import sys

}}
OPERATOR F_HydrogenPassivation : Passivation {{
    CONSERVATION: N_Pb + N_SiH = const
    EQUATION: d(N_Pb)/dt = -H_unit * exp(ln10 * (log_k_H + log_h)) * p_H2 * N_Pb
    EQUATION: d(N_SiH)/dt = H_unit * exp(ln10 * (log_k_H + log_h)) * p_H2 * N_Pb
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Dit_Midgap {{ TARGET: Dit; UNIT: cm^-2 eV^-1; EQUATION: Dit = c_mid * ({'N_Pb + N_B' if two else 'N_Pb'}) }}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT Contract {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/vitkavage_1990_dit_midgap.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{sha}
    SPLIT: search
    TARGET: Dit = {row['Dit']:.4g} +/- {d.CONTRACT_TOLERANCE} rel
}}
"""

def overrides(structure, x, names, orientation):
    v = dict(zip(names, [float(a) for a in x]))
    ov = {k: v[k] for k in BASE}
    ov["log_h"] = v.get(f"log_h_{orientation}", 0.0)
    if structure.startswith("two_populations"):
        ov["log_B"] = v.get(f"log_B_{orientation}", 0.0)       # (111): no second population can be fitted; it is left out (zero is 1 per cm2)
        ov["E_B"] = v.get("E_B", 0.0)
    return ov

def split_of(row):
    return "holdout" if row["T_ox_C"] == 900 or row["t_ox_hr"] == 8.0 else "train"

def evaluate(structure):
    rows, sha = d.load_rows()
    pars = parameters(structure)
    names = list(pars)
    rts = []
    for r in rows:
        rt = ISLRuntime()
        rt.parse_isl_source(program(structure, r, sha))
        rts.append(rt)
    meas = np.array([r["Dit"] for r in rows])
    train = [i for i, r in enumerate(rows) if split_of(r) == "train"]
    def predict(x, idx):
        out = []
        for i in idx:
            try:
                out.append(next(iter(rts[i].execute_isl_evaluation(parameter_overrides=overrides(structure, x, names, rows[i]["orientation"]), base_dir=ROOT)["receipt"]["results"].values()))["pred"])
            except Exception:
                out.append(float("nan"))
        return np.array(out)
    def residuals(x):
        p = predict(x, train)
        return np.where(np.isfinite(p) & (p > 0), np.log(np.maximum(p, 1.0) / meas[train]) / 0.10, 30.0)
    rng, best = np.random.default_rng(7), None
    lo, hi = [pars[n][1] for n in names], [pars[n][2] for n in names]
    for x0 in [[pars[n][0] for n in names]] + [[float(rng.uniform(a, b)) for a, b in zip(lo, hi)] for _ in range(5)]:
        f = least_squares(residuals, x0=x0, bounds=(lo, hi), diff_step=1e-2, max_nfev=60)
        if best is None or f.cost < best.cost:
            best = f
    pred = predict(best.x, range(len(rows)))
    samples = [{"orientation": r["orientation"], "anneal": r["anneal"], "T_ox_C": r["T_ox_C"], "t_ox_hr": r["t_ox_hr"], "split": split_of(r), "measured": r["Dit"], "predicted": float(p),
                "passed": bool(abs(p - r["Dit"]) <= d.CONTRACT_TOLERANCE * r["Dit"])} for r, p in zip(rows, pred)]
    out = {"structure": structure, "n_fitted": len(names), "fitted": dict(zip(names, [float(v) for v in best.x])), "samples": samples}
    for split in ("train", "holdout"):
        sel = [s for s in samples if s["split"] == split]
        err = [abs(s["predicted"] - s["measured"]) / s["measured"] for s in sel]
        out[split] = {"n": len(sel), "passed": sum(s["passed"] for s in sel), "within_25_percent": int(sum(e <= 0.25 for e in err)), "median_error": round(float(np.median(err)), 3)}
    return out

def run(workers=1):
    if workers > 1:
        import multiprocessing
        with multiprocessing.Pool(workers) as pool:
            results = pool.map(evaluate, STRUCTURES)
    else:
        results = [evaluate(s) for s in STRUCTURES]
    key = lambda r: (-r["train"]["passed"], r["n_fitted"], r["train"]["median_error"])
    results.sort(key=key)
    report = {"what": "Structure search for midgap trap density on planar silicon, Vitkavage 1990. Candidates come from a grammar.",
              "data_verification": "Agent-transcribed values; four values ruled on by the project owner. Unverified otherwise.",
              "split": "held out: the 900 C recipes and the 8 h recipes; fitted: the rest. Changed from the earlier split (which held out (511)) because some structures have a number per orientation.",
              "selection_rule": "most training targets passed, then fewest parameters, then smallest training error; held-out scores are not used to choose",
              "selected": {k: v for k, v in results[0].items() if k != "samples"}, "ranking": [{k: v for k, v in r.items() if k != "samples"} for r in results],
              "samples_of_each": {r["structure"]: r["samples"] for r in results}}
    with open(REPORT, "w") as f:
        json.dump(report, f, indent=1)
    return report

if __name__ == "__main__":
    rep = run(int(sys.argv[sys.argv.index("--workers") + 1]) if "--workers" in sys.argv else 1)
    for r in rep["ranking"]:
        print(f"  {r['structure']:26s} parameters {r['n_fitted']:2d}  train {r['train']['passed']:2d}/{r['train']['n']} (25%: {r['train']['within_25_percent']}, median {r['train']['median_error']})"
              f"  held-out {r['holdout']['passed']:2d}/{r['holdout']['n']} (25%: {r['holdout']['within_25_percent']}, median {r['holdout']['median_error']})")
```

What alignment cannot fix. **Hydrogen that does not come from the gas.** The paper's two hydrogen sources, the aluminium film during a vacuum anneal and atomic deuterium from a remote plasma, cannot be written. Every program here reads hydrogen only as p_H2 in the gas. Forming gas as a stand-in gives hydrogen everywhere at once and no temperature threshold.

**No temperature in the passivation rate.** The trap program's hydrogen rate was fitted on one temperature (400 °C, Vitkavage) and carries none; the equilibrium program's is a placeholder. A 15 min isochronal series like Fig. 2 is the measurement that would fix an activation energy, and both programs give a flat line through it.

**Interface states are not Pb here.** The paper's central result is that Pb goes 100 to 150 °C below the 0.3 eV peak. Both programs make interface states out of one population of dangling bonds (the trap program's second population is set to nothing on (111)), so they cannot separate them. The paper's DLTS values are also 20 times the trap program's as-oxidized density; the program was found on another laboratory's (111) wafers measured after forming gas, and on unannealed (111) it is outside its fitted range.

**Vacuum that removes Pb.** Without aluminium and without hydrogen in the gas the spin signal goes between 400 and 525 °C. In Stesmans 1996 a vacuum anneal of passivated Pb brings it back above 480 °C. No program here has a source of hydrogen inside the oxide, or a vacuum that is not pure, that could do what Johnson sees.

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **An ambient of atomic hydrogen.** “Annealing … in atomic deuterium at 230 °C” downstream of a plasma has no word. It needs a species (H or D atoms) with a flux or an arrival rate at the surface (cm⁻² s⁻¹) rather than a partial pressure, since the atoms recombine on the walls; e.g. `AMBIENT atomic:D FLUX 1e15`. With it, a kinetic program could cap Pb at the rate atoms arrive at the interface, which this paper shows happens at 25 to 230 °C (Fig. 5).
- **A film as a hydrogen source.** The aluminium releases hydrogen during the anneal; the same anneal without it does nothing below 400 °C. A `FILM Al { RELEASES: H }` on the sample, read by hydrogen programs as a source term during any RUN, would let a post-metallization anneal in vacuum or nitrogen be written as what it is. The ontology has process factor hydrogen-anneal for a gas, nothing for a film.
- **Isotopes.** Deuterium is written as hydrogen or not at all. `D2` in the gas list (and D in the atomic ambient) with masses carried into the zero-point energy (a computed ph.x number with deuterium masses) and into any rate prefactor would let the paper's “not regained up to 600 °C” be compared with Si–H dissociation rates on the right basis.
- **A kinetic hydrogen program** (see also Stesmans 1996): passivation from H₂ with an activation energy (1.66 eV measured, by Brower) is what this paper's with-Al series needs; dissociation with its activation energy what its deuterated series needs. Both barriers can be computed on the H100 with neb.x, from the relaxed cluster for H₂ + Pb and from a (111)/SiO₂ slab for dissociation into a bound site; the Stesmans page gives the plan.
- **Spin density as an observable, with its units.** Fig. 2 is in arbitrary units with one absolute anchor (6×10¹¹ cm⁻² as oxidized). A target `Pb_spin_density` in cm⁻² with a declared normalization (`RELATIVE TO <case>`) would let both programs be scored on Pb directly instead of on a share.
- **A series on one sample.** Whether the isochronal anneals of Fig. 2 are one specimen annealed in turn is not stated. The language cannot write either reading as one statement; `REPEAT` over temperatures with an observation after each pass would, and an alignment could then treat the reading as a variant.
- **Interface states at an energy without a hydrogen step.** The trap program reports a spectrum only after hydrogen, from the shape of one annealed curve. The as-oxidized (111) peak at 0.3 eV, 6.7×10¹² cm⁻² eV⁻¹, here and in Vitkavage, has no program at all.

## What we would like Emily to check

1. Without aluminium and without any hydrogen in the gas, the spin signal disappears in vacuum between 400 and 525 °C (Fig. 2). Is the hydrogen already in a 200 nm dry oxide (from the furnace or the rapid cool) enough to cap 6×10¹¹ cm⁻² of Pb? Or was the 1981 vacuum poor enough to supply water?
2. Stesmans and Afanas'ev see the opposite in a diffusion-pumped vacuum (≤4×10⁻⁷ Torr): Pb returns above 480 °C. Is the difference the starting state (passivated or not), the vacuum, or the oxide?
3. After atomic deuterium the spin signal stays away after 15 min at 600 °C, while Brower's dissociation rate (2.56 eV, 1.2×10¹² s⁻¹) would bring most of it back. Would Si–D dissociate that much more slowly than Si–H, or does the atomic-deuterium exposure do more than cap Pb?
