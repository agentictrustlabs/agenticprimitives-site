## The study and why we depend on it

**M. L. Reed, “Models of Si-SiO2 interface reactions”, Semicond. Sci. Technol. 4, 980-985 (1989).**

The trap-density program caps dangling bonds in any step with hydrogen at a rate k_H·p_H2, with k_H fitted on one anneal (Vitkavage 1990, 400 °C, 30 min). Nothing in the project held how that rate depends on temperature or time. This review is the project's first source for it: Brower's activation energy (1.66 eV, rate linear in p_H2), Burte and Matthies' decay curves at 450 and 570 °C, and Reed and Plummer's 340 °C curves for (100) and (111). It also bears on the Quantum ESPRESSO bound, which says how far capping could go at equilibrium.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Low.** A review. It reports no experiment of its own; it re-plots three anneal series from other papers (Brower; Burte and Matthies; Reed and Plummer) with their anneal temperature, time and, for two, ambient. None of them states the oxidation that made the traps, the oxide thickness, the metallisation, or the forming-gas composition, so every starting density here rests on an oxidation we invented. What it does give, and what the predictor can be tested on, is how the anneal depends on temperature and time: activation energies of 1.66 and 1.57 eV, a plateau, and dissociation above about 420 to 550 °C.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- The oxidation that made the traps, for all three data sets: temperature, time, gas, cooling.
- Burte and Matthies: the three metallisations (each partly anneals), the forming-gas composition, the furnace push and pull for anneals of 6 and 12 s.
- Reed and Plummer: the RTA ambient, ramp-up and cool-down; whether one capacitor was annealed repeatedly or each time is a fresh one.
- Brower: how the Pb centres were depassivated before the H2 anneal.

### What the second reading changed

- The project had no dataset, script or stored recipe from this paper (library status “skimmed”); SOURCE.md says only the first page was read. Everything here is new.
- A 6 s or 12 s furnace anneal cannot reach 450 or 570 °C in the wafer; the review writes them as anneal times without saying how the wafer was heated. Ours holds the wafer at temperature for the stated time.

The model itself, `experiments/atomistic/hydrogen_passivation_equilibrium.isl.template`, as it is written. Every equation the simulation uses is in this text; the runtime holds no physics.

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: HOW FAR HYDROGEN CAN CAP SILICON DANGLING BONDS AT EQUILIBRIUM
#
# Si* + 1/2 H2 <-> Si-H. The energy of the reaction is a COMPUTED parameter: a Quantum ESPRESSO
# calculation on a small cluster (atomistic/calcs/si_dangling_bond_hydrogen.json), not a measurement
# and not a fit. With the free energy of hydrogen gas it gives the equilibrium constant, and so the
# share of dangling bonds that hydrogen at a given temperature and pressure would leave uncapped if
# the anneal were long enough to reach equilibrium.
#
# What this is for: a bound. The trap-density program has a fitted share of sites that the
# hydrogen anneal does not remove. If equilibrium already leaves that share, the residue is
# thermodynamic; if equilibrium leaves far less, the residue is kinetic or is another defect.
# What it is not: a rate. How fast equilibrium is approached is the hypothesis k_eq below.
#
# Used by isl_harness/predict.py only when computed parameters are switched on (ISL_USE_COMPUTED=1
# or --computed). Fields in {{braces}} are filled from evaluations/atomistic/<id>.json.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE {sample_id} {{
    FAMILY: Si_Planar
}}
# Fractions of the sites: uncapped (electrically active) and capped by hydrogen.
STATE N_free {{ UNIT: 1; INITIAL: 1; MIN: 0; MAX: 1.000001 }}
STATE N_capped {{ UNIT: 1; INITIAL: 0; MIN: 0; MAX: 1.000001 }}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_H2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER dE_cap {{ VALUE: {dE_cap}; UNIT: eV; STATUS: computed; SOURCE: {source} }}
# Zero-point energy of the Si-H bond (one stretch, two bends) less half that of H2. Recalled from vibrational frequencies
# (about 2100 and 630 cm^-1 for Si-H, 4400 cm^-1 for H2), not from a source in the library and not yet computed (ph.x can).
PARAMETER dZPE {{ VALUE: 0.07; UNIT: eV; STATUS: hypothesis }}
# Hydrogen gas as an ideal diatomic: standard entropy 130.7 J/mol/K at 298.15 K and 1 atm, heat capacity 3.5 k_B.
# Textbook values, recalled, not from a source in the library.
PARAMETER S_ref {{ VALUE: 1.3546e-3; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: hypothesis }}
PARAMETER c_p {{ VALUE: 3.0161e-4; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: hypothesis }}
PARAMETER T_ref {{ VALUE: 298.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
# How fast equilibrium is approached: assumed fast against any anneal. The answer is the equilibrium, not the path.
PARAMETER k_eq {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: s^-1; STATUS: hypothesis }}
# Free energy of one H2 molecule at 1 atm above its energy at rest: enthalpy 3.5 k_B T less T times the entropy.
QUANTITY mu_H2 {{ UNIT: eV; EQUATION: mu_H2 = c_p * T - T * (S_ref + c_p * log(T / T_ref)) }}
QUANTITY dG {{ UNIT: eV; EQUATION: dG = dE_cap + dZPE - 0.5 * mu_H2 }}
QUANTITY K_eq {{ UNIT: 1; EQUATION: K_eq = exp(-dG / (k_B * T)) }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY {trajectory_id} {{
    RUN PostMetalAnneal DURATION 1800 TEMP 400->400 AMBIENT forming_gas_H2
}}
# Capping at a rate proportional to the square root of the hydrogen pressure; uncapping at the rate detailed balance requires.
OPERATOR F_HydrogenCapping : Passivation {{
    EQUATION: d(N_free)/dt = -k_eq * (sqrt(p_H2 / p_ref) * N_free - N_capped / K_eq)
    EQUATION: d(N_capped)/dt = k_eq * (sqrt(p_H2 / p_ref) * N_free - N_capped / K_eq)
    CONSERVATION: N_free + N_capped = const
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Uncapped {{
    TARGET: Normalized_Pb
    UNIT: 1
    EQUATION: Normalized_Pb = N_free
}}
```

The chosen oxidation is therefore not a finding about the experiment; it is the program asking for a lower start.

Run after 1 h at 1000 °C, forming gas for 6 s, 60 s, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h and 6.5 h gives 2.16×10^11, 1.58×10^11, 1.43×10^11, 7.51×10^10, 7.45×10^9 and 7.3×10^6, identical to three figures at 240, 340, 400, 450 and 570 °C (`fga_scan`). **At low temperature the same program is far too fast.** In pure H2 at 240 °C, Brower's Pb0 intensity falls by a factor 0.77 in 89 min and 0.34 in 389 min (a first-order rate of 2.7×10^-3 per min). The program's rate in 1 atm H2 is 0.26 per min, 95 times faster: after 89 min it has removed all but 10^-10 of the sites. One temperature-free rate fitted at 400 °C cannot be both 200 times too slow at 450 °C and 95 times too fast at 240 °C; an Arrhenius rate with an energy near Brower's 1.66 eV would be on the right side of both.

**No plateau and no reverse reaction.** Burte's density stops at about 1×10^10 at both 450 and 570 °C, and Reed reports that above 420 °C a long anneal raises it again; Brower sees passivated centres dissociate near 550 °C in vacuum. The program decays towards zero for as long as hydrogen is present and never releases a capped site.

**Reed and Plummer's anneals do nothing in the program.** Written as stated (ambient not given, nitrogen ours), the RTA carries no hydrogen, so the program holds 2.61×10^11 for (100) and 1.86×10^11 for (111) through 600 s, while the paper's density falls from 3.7×10^10 to 3.4×10^9 (100) and from 1.2×10^11 to 2.5×10^10 (111). The variant that writes the RTA as forming gas changes the score by nothing (1 of 33): even with hydrogen the program's 39 min time constant hardly moves in 600 s. Writing Burte's anneals in pure H2 instead of 10% forming gas makes the program ten times faster and lifts the score to 2 of 33 alone and 4 of 33 with the aligned oxidation, still far from the 12 s decay.

**With Quantum ESPRESSO switched on.** The computed bound on the density hydrogen could reach at equilibrium is 7.0×10^6 cm⁻² eV⁻¹ at 450 °C and 1.4×10^8 at 570 °C in 10% forming gas (and 1.4×10^3, 1.6×10^5 and 1.5×10^6 at 240, 340 and 400 °C). Burte's plateaus (1.0×10^10 and about 1.3×10^10) are 1400 and 90 times above it, and Reed's (100) density after 600 s at 340 °C (3.4×10^9) is 2×10^4 times above the 340 °C bound. So the plateau is not set by the thermodynamics of the Si–H bond computed on the cluster: either the anneal stops short of equilibrium, or the plateau is traps that hydrogen does not cap (Reed's “annealed Pb1 not detected electrically”, a second kind of site). The bound rises 10^5 times between 240 and 570 °C, the direction Thanh and Balk report for their temperature-only steady state, but it is 2 to 4 orders too low to be that steady state.

**With heating and cooling transitions.** A hot push (1 to 5 min heat-up) before the assumed oxidation, ramps around the anneals and a cool-down at the end move 9 of 33 numbers by more than 2%, lower the median error from 749% to about 640%, and lose the one number within tolerance (the 450 °C plateau after 120 min, which the program reached only by decaying past it). None of the transitions touches the hydrogen rate, which is where the error is.

What alignment cannot fix: the program's hydrogen step has no temperature dependence, no plateau and no reverse reaction, and it needs H2 in the gas. Burte's 450 and 570 °C curves need a capping rate roughly 200 times the fitted one at 450 °C, Brower's 240 °C curve one about 100 times lower, and both curves end on a plateau near 1×10^10 cm⁻² eV⁻¹. No choice of the unstated oxidation changes any of that; it only moves the starting value, and the starting value it would need (8.4×10^10) is below what any oxidation in the searched range gives.

These are the program's faults, measured on data it was not fitted on, and they matter for every page that has a forming-gas anneal: the program was fitted where its one rate happens to be right (400 °C, 30 min) and is used from 340 to 570 °C and from seconds to hours.

The review cannot be reproduced as a set of experiments: none of its three data sets states the oxidation, the gate process or the gas composition, and Figure 3's unit (cm⁻²) is matched to the program's midgap density only through the program's own hypothesis c_mid = 1 eV⁻¹.

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **A gate that supplies hydrogen.** Reed's Al-gate capacitors anneal at the same rate in any ambient because aluminium reacting with hydroxyl at the Al–SiO2 interface releases atomic hydrogen. The language can record `GATE: Al` on a sample, but no statement says the gate is a hydrogen source during every later heat; an RTA in nitrogen therefore anneals nothing. A sample layer with a declared reservoir (`LAYER Al { SOURCE: H }`) or a `SOURCE H` clause on a RUN would let the Al-gate curves be simulated at all.
- **Atomic against molecular hydrogen.** Bare oxides anneal much faster in atomic hydrogen than in H2 (PDF p. 3); the language's gases are Ar, H2, H2O, N2 and O2. An `H` species in `mix:` (and a program that reads it) is needed before corona, plasma or post-metal anneals can be written.
- **A measured starting state.** Every case here starts from an oxidation we invented because the program cannot be told “the sample has 8.4×10^10 cm⁻² eV⁻¹ of traps at t = 0”. A sample field such as `STARTING_DIT: 8.4e10` (as STARTING_OXIDE_NM does for thickness) would let an anneal series be tested on its kinetics alone, without an unknown oxidation in front of it.
- **One sample measured along an anneal.** Each point of a decay curve is written as a separate history ending at that time. Whether the paper annealed one capacitor repeatedly (with cooling and a measurement between anneals) or a fresh one each time is not stated, and the language cannot say which. A `REPEAT` of anneal-and-measure on one sample, with the cooling between, would make the difference explicit.
- **A plateau and a reverse reaction in the trap-density program** (a predictor change): an Arrhenius capping rate (Brower 1.66 eV), a dissociation of Si–H above about 500 °C, and a floor or steady state after hydrogen. The computed Si–H energy (−1.28 eV for Si• + ½H₂) already gives the equilibrium; the missing pieces are the rates.

## With Quantum ESPRESSO switched on

This page was simulated with parameters from atomic calculations switched on (`ISL_USE_COMPUTED=1`). They come from Quantum ESPRESSO (the discovered-materials dm_qe build), run locally on CPU and on an H100 on RunPod, recorded in `evaluations/atomistic/` with the code version, inputs, pseudopotential checksums and convergence, and carried into ISL as `STATUS: computed`. They never replace a fitted or sourced number: the rows scored above are the same with the switch off. Each computed row sits beside the measurement it bears on.

What is computed so far is one reaction: Si• + ½H₂ → Si–H for a silicon dangling bond with three silicon back-bonds (the local structure of a Pb centre), on a 14-atom cluster. With the free energy of hydrogen gas it bounds the share of dangling bonds that a hydrogen step could leave uncapped if it reached equilibrium, and so the lowest interface state density that step could reach. A measurement far above the bound means what remains after the hydrogen step is not limited by the strength of the Si–H bond: the anneal is far from equilibrium, or the traps that remain are not dangling bonds hydrogen can cap.

## What we would like Emily to check

1. Burte and Matthies' decay is complete within 6 to 12 s at 450 °C. Can a wafer in a furnace reach 450 °C in 6 s, or is most of that anneal happening during the push and pull?
2. Is Brower's 1.66 eV the right activation energy for the capping rate in a forming-gas anneal of a gate oxide, or only for a bare oxide in pure H2?
3. Should a trap-density model carry a plateau after hydrogen (Burte about 1×10^10, Thanh and Balk a temperature-dependent steady state), and is that plateau the same population as the program's second population?
4. Reed's Al-gate anneals do not depend on the ambient. Should a sample with an Al gate be treated as carrying its own hydrogen source in every anneal?
