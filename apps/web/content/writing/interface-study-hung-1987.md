## The study and why we depend on it

**Hung Kwok Kwong, “Electrical Characterization of Si-SiO2 Interface for Thin Oxides”, PhD thesis, University of Hong Kong (1987), chapters 4 and 5 and Appendix A.**

A thesis on oxides 8 to 43 nm thick, the thinnest planar oxides in the graph. The project holds 26 fixed charges (chapter 4's tables and the anneal series of Figure 5.9) and 34 midgap trap densities (Figures 5.4 and 5.9) from it. It is the second laboratory for the trap-density program, which was found on Vitkavage's wafers, and a laboratory the fixed-charge program never saw. Here both chapters are written out with the Appendix's furnace recipe, which the stored recipes leave out: the boat-in, the boat-out or fast pull, and the anneal after metallization.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Moderate.** Oxidation temperature, gas, time and the nitrogen anneal are printed per wafer, and the Appendix gives a recipe with boat-in, anneal and boat-out times. What a simulation still has to supply: whether the printed oxidation times include the 3 min boat-in, the furnace's ramp from 900 to 1050 °C for chapter 4's second batch, how the anneal series was withdrawn, and the wafer's cooling after every withdrawal. The trap densities of chapter 5 exist only as points on two figures.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- Whether the oxidation times of Tables 4.1 and 5.1 include the 3 min boat-in. For B1 (1.5 min in oxygen at 1050 °C) the boat-in is twice the printed time.
- Boat-in and boat-out for chapter 4's 900 °C batch: the Appendix gives them for the 1050 °C, 20% O2 recipe only.
- How the furnace went from 900 °C (oxidation) to 1050 °C (anneal) for batches K and J: time, gas, and whether the wafers stayed inside.
- Where the wafer cools after a boat-out or a fast pull, and how fast.
- How the anneal series P and Q were withdrawn after the nitrogen anneal: fast pull or 3 min boat-out, in nitrogen or oxygen; and whether the 0 min wafers had a gas switch at all.
- The forming-gas anneal: 30 min (chapter 4), 10 min (chapter 5), “10-30 min” (Appendix), with 15 min of nitrogen at 400 °C before and after in the Appendix.
- Time between steps: storage “in a dry environment throughout the course of experiments” for the capacitor wafers.

### What the second reading changed

- The stored recipes have one or two RUNs: the oxidation and, in chapter 4 and the anneal series, the nitrogen anneal. The thesis's Appendix adds a 3 min boat-in in the oxidation gas, a 3 min boat-out in nitrogen (chapter 4), a fast pull in the oxidation gas (chapter 5), and in chapter 4 a 30 min forming-gas anneal with 15 min of nitrogen before and after. None of these is in the stored recipes.
- The anneal series P (1050 °C, 20% O2) is stored with 27 min of oxidation, “inferred from the thickness”; Table 5.1b prints 20 min. The datasets were built before Table 5.1b was read.
- Batches K and J are stored as 900 °C oxidation followed at once by 30 min at 1050 °C; the ramp between them is not described in the thesis and is not in the stored recipe.
- The library records the pull as “under 3 s in the oxidation ambient” for the whole source. That is chapter 5's; chapter 4's wafers had a 30 min nitrogen anneal and a 3 min boat-out in nitrogen.
- The time_law_dit dataset has no 0 min rows for the anneal series; two_population_dit has them (5.48 and 1.42 × 10^11). The 0 min wafers of Figure 5.9 are separate wafers from those of Figure 5.4.
- Figure 5.9's fixed charge comes from the half of each wafer that had forming gas, its trap density from the other half. One stored recipe stands for both.

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: FIXED CHARGE WITH AN EQUILIBRIUM IN EACH GAS
#
# In oxygen the charge moves toward a steady value that falls with temperature (the
# line of Deal et al. 1967, unchanged, with the oxygen kinetics fitted there).
# In a gas without oxygen it moves toward a lower equilibrium that also falls with
# temperature, Qi_ref * exp(E_q / k_B * (1/T - 1/T_iref)), at a rate of its own.
#
# The inert clock and the (100) inert equilibrium are fitted to the argon time series
# of Akinwande, Ho and Plummer (1982). The (111) inert equilibrium is fitted to their
# cycling experiment, with the clock carried over from (100). The model is then
# tested, unchanged, on the Fairchild wafers of 1967 and 1980.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_fixed_charge_two_lines.py.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE Wafer_{tag} {{
    FAMILY: Si_Planar
    ORIENTATION: Si{orientation}
}}
# The wafer starts at the steady value for its oxidation temperature.
STATE Q_f {{ UNIT: cm^-2; INITIAL: Q_hot + slope * (T_hot - T_ox); MIN: 0; MAX: 1.0e14 }}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_O2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER Q_hot {{ VALUE: {Q_hot}; UNIT: cm^-2; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal et al. 1967 Table II, steady value after dry oxidation at 1200 C }}
PARAMETER slope {{ VALUE: {slope}; UNIT: cm^-2 K^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal et al. 1967 Table II, line through the 1200 C and 920 C steady values }}
PARAMETER T_hot {{ VALUE: 1473.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER T_ox {{ VALUE: {T_ox_K}; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
# ---- fitted to the pull-rate series ------------------------------------------------
PARAMETER k0 {{ VALUE: 1.0e3; UNIT: s^-1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_a {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
# ---- fitted to the argon anneals of Akinwande, Ho and Plummer (1982) -----------------
PARAMETER k_i0 {{ VALUE: 1.0e1; UNIT: s^-1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_i {{ VALUE: 0.9; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER Qi_ref {{ VALUE: 2.5e10; UNIT: cm^-2; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_q {{ VALUE: 0.4; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER T_iref {{ VALUE: 1273.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
# ---- where the exchange with oxygen stops as the wafer cools (hypothesis, added 10 November 2026) ----------------------
# The oxygen rate above was fitted with an activation energy at its lower bound (0.05 eV), so on its own it keeps moving
# charge at room temperature. Every history it was fitted and tested on ended its pull at 400 C, a cut-off that stood in for
# this. Reproducing Stanford 1982, Deal 1967 and Deal and Razouk 1980 with a wafer cooling to room temperature made the charge
# 3 to 5 times too high. The cut-off is now stated: the exchange is switched off over about 20 K around T_freeze. At 400 C it
# is 99% on, so every fitted and tested history is unchanged; how high the true freezing point is, is an open question
# (the Deal and Razouk alignment prefers pulls ending near 530 C).
PARAMETER T_freeze {{ VALUE: 623.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: hypothesis }}
PARAMETER w_freeze {{ VALUE: 10.0; UNIT: K; STATUS: hypothesis }}
QUANTITY rate {{ UNIT: s^-1; EQUATION: rate = k0 * exp(-E_a / (k_B * T)) / (1 + exp(-(T - T_freeze) / w_freeze)) }}
QUANTITY rate_inert {{ UNIT: s^-1; EQUATION: rate_inert = k_i0 * exp(-E_i / (k_B * T)) }}
QUANTITY Q_inert {{ UNIT: cm^-2; EQUATION: Q_inert = Qi_ref * exp((E_q / k_B) * (1 / T - 1 / T_iref)) }}
QUANTITY oxidizing {{ UNIT: 1; EQUATION: oxidizing = p_O2 / p_ref }}
QUANTITY Q_steady {{ UNIT: cm^-2; EQUATION: Q_steady = Q_hot + slope * (T_hot - T) }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY History_{tag} {{
{runs}
}}
# In oxygen the charge follows the temperature-dependent steady value.
OPERATOR F_OxidizingEquilibration : Relaxation {{
    EQUATION: d(Q_f)/dt = -rate * oxidizing * (Q_f - Q_steady)
}}
# Without oxygen it relaxes, at its own rate, toward an equilibrium of its own that falls as temperature rises.
# Akinwande, Ho and Plummer (1982) show the annealed charge cycling reversibly with temperature in argon.
OPERATOR F_InertRelaxation : Relaxation {{
    EQUATION: d(Q_f)/dt = -rate_inert * (1 - oxidizing) * (Q_f - Q_inert)
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Fixed_Charge {{ TARGET: Qss; UNIT: cm^-2; EQUATION: Qss = Q_f }}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT Contract_{tag} {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/deal_1967_qss.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{dataset_sha256}
    SPLIT: {split}
    TARGET: Qss = {target} +/- {tolerance} abs
}}
```

As reproduced: 5 of 76 numbers within tolerance (median error 85.3%). Aligned: 6 of 76 (89.0%). On the held-out wafers: 3 of 39 before and after; the median error there rose from 47.2% to 84.7%.

**The data do not constrain most of the unknowns.** Eight of the nine scans cannot raise the count within tolerance on the 1050 °C wafers (2 of 37 at every value, or fewer); only the chapter 5 boat-in does, from 2 to 3 at 76 s. Where scores tie, the search keeps the first grid value that reached them, so the aligned values printed for those eight (ramp 300 s, pull 1 s to 200 °C, forming gas 800 s, and so on) are not findings. The reason is the size of the model errors: fixed charge is 80 to 96% low on every wafer and no history step moves it by more than 25%.

**The forming-gas time was a tie and it made the test worse.** On the 1050 °C wafers 800 s and the stated 1800 s score the same; 800 s puts M1 instead of M2 on the printed “~5×10^10”, which is one rounded value for the whole group. On the 900 °C batch 800 s doubles the simulated value (8.7 to 10.4 × 10^10 against 5 × 10^10, from 5.2 to 6.2 before), and the four K and J trap densities that were within twice the tolerance no longer are. That is the held-out loss. Chapter 4 states 30 min; nothing here argues against it.

**The boat-in is pinned by thickness, not by charge or traps.** Scanned on its own, thickness of the pure-oxygen wafers decides it. B1 (1.5 min printed, 11.1 nm) is 8.05 nm with no boat-in, 10.8 nm with 60 s of boat-in at 1050 °C, 12.1 nm with 90 s and 15.6 nm with the full 180 s. C1 (6 min at 975 °C) is within 10% only with 30 s or less. So the full 3 min at furnace temperature added to the printed time is too much; about one minute at temperature, the end of a 3 min push, fits B1, and the printed times are best read as starting once the boat is in. The trap density moves by under 5% over the whole range, and fixed charge not at all.

**Variants.** Storing the P series with 27 min of oxidation (as the datasets do) instead of the printed 20 min adds one number within tolerance (a trap density) and makes the thickness worse (52.6 against 18 nm). Thickness from the 1965 law instead of the thin-oxide program loses 2 numbers; that law starts every oxide at 23 nm, above most of these oxides. Ramping K and J in oxygen instead of nitrogen changes nothing: the 30 min in nitrogen at 1050 °C that follows erases it in both programs.

What alignment cannot fix. **Fixed charge:** after 30 min of nitrogen at 1050 °C the program sits at its equilibrium without oxygen, 2.7 × 10^10 cm⁻², for every chapter 4 wafer. Hung measures 3.8 to 6.0 × 10^11, rising as the as-grown oxide gets thinner and flat for the etched-back oxides. The program has no thickness and no memory of the growth once the anneal is over, so the thesis's central result (as-grown against etched-back) cannot appear in it. In the anneal series the program falls in both series (Q: 1.74 to 0.39 × 10^11; P: 0.47 to 0.27 × 10^11) where Hung's 900 °C series falls by 22% and the 1050 °C series rises from 2.2 to 4.8 × 10^11 after 10 min.

**Trap density without hydrogen:** on the as-oxidized wafers the program is 28 to 86% high at 1050 °C and 10 to 34% low at 900 and 975 °C, so Hung's temperature dependence is steeper (D1 over B1: 2.5 measured, 1.3 simulated), and so is the fall with oxidation time (D1 to D4: -34% measured, -11% simulated). The program has no oxygen-pressure term, which Hung finds at 975 °C (Y against C). In nitrogen at 900 °C Hung's density falls by 71% in 40 min and the program's by 4%; at 1050 °C Hung's dips and then rises and the program's falls. Every one of these histories is outside the range the program was fitted on (Vitkavage: 750 to 1000 °C, one hour or more).

**Thickness:** the thin-oxide program does not read the oxygen pressure (8 min in 20% oxygen and in pure oxygen both give 22.4 nm) and keeps growing oxide in nitrogen, so every 20% and 50% oxide is two to three times too thick and every oxide that had a nitrogen anneal is 2 to 6 times too thick (M1: 64 against 10.1 nm). On the pure-oxygen wafers without an anneal it does well: B4 +8%, D4 -4%, D2 +12%, Q0 +10% as reproduced.

How to read the aligned numbers: the unknowns were chosen on the 1050 °C wafers only, so the rows shown (900 and 975 °C) are a test. The ramp is the one unknown the 1050 °C wafers cannot inform at all; it kept its first grid value.

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **Which step is the oxidation.** The fixed-charge and trap-density programs take the oxidation temperature from the first oxygen-bearing RUN. A boat-in written as a heating ramp (`TEMP 25->1050 PROFILE exponential`) would then be read as an oxidation at 25 °C, so the boat-in here had to be written as a hold at furnace temperature. A `ROLE oxidation|anneal|load|pull` on each RUN would let programs find the step by what it is, and let a push-in be written as the heating it is.
- **An unknown that is an offset, not a duration.** “Does the printed time include the 3 min boat-in?” is one question for all 24 wafers whose oxidation times run from 1.5 to 151 min. `VARY duration` on the boat-in stands in for it; `VARY offset -180..0` on the oxidation, shared across samples, would say it directly.
- **A choice of gas as an unknown.** The ramp from 900 to 1050 °C and the withdrawal after the anneal series have unknown gases. The language varies numbers only; the gas had to become a Python variant. `VARY ambient inert_N2|dry_O2` would make it a declared unknown.
- **A wafer split in two.** In chapter 5 each wafer was cleaved and only one half had forming gas; Figure 5.9's trap density is from one half and its fixed charge from the other. `EVENT Cleave` records it, but one history cannot carry two halves. A `SPLIT` that forks the trajectory into named halves, with each observation bound to its half, would let both values be compared on the right history.
- **An etch that removes oxide.** The etched-back oxides are Hung's control: same growth, different final thickness. `EVENT EtchBack KIND etch` takes nothing off the simulated oxide. A statement that sets or removes thickness (`ETCH REMOVE 25 nm` or `ETCH TO 14.5 nm`) is needed before any program with a thickness term can be tested against this control.
- **How a charge was extracted.** Hung's Qf uses φMS = -0.715 V from the etched-back intercept. At 10 nm, 0.1 V of φMS is 2.1 × 10^11 cm⁻² of charge; at 40 nm it is 0.5 × 10^11. An observation statement carrying the φMS, the frequency and whether the half had forming gas would let charge from different laboratories be compared on one basis.
- **Gas by pressure in every program.** Writing `mix:O2=0.2,N2=0.8` now reaches the programs, but the thin-oxide growth program ignores the oxygen pressure and grows oxide in nitrogen. That is a predictor change, not a language one, and it decides every 20% and 50% thickness on this page.
- **Rounded and typical values.** Table 4.2 gives “~5×10^10” for a group of four, and chapter 5 reports “only typical or representative results”. A result's tolerance should be able to say it is a rounding of a group value, so that a search does not fit one wafer to it, as happened with the forming-gas time here.

## With Quantum ESPRESSO switched on

This page was simulated with parameters from atomic calculations switched on (`ISL_USE_COMPUTED=1`). They come from Quantum ESPRESSO (the discovered-materials dm_qe build), run locally on CPU and on an H100 on RunPod, recorded in `evaluations/atomistic/` with the code version, inputs, pseudopotential checksums and convergence, and carried into ISL as `STATUS: computed`. They never replace a fitted or sourced number: the rows scored above are the same with the switch off. Each computed row sits beside the measurement it bears on.

What is computed so far is one reaction: Si• + ½H₂ → Si–H for a silicon dangling bond with three silicon back-bonds (the local structure of a Pb centre), on a 14-atom cluster. With the free energy of hydrogen gas it bounds the share of dangling bonds that a hydrogen step could leave uncapped if it reached equilibrium, and so the lowest interface state density that step could reach. A measurement far above the bound means what remains after the hydrogen step is not limited by the strength of the Si–H bond: the anneal is far from equilibrium, or the traps that remain are not dangling bonds hydrogen can cap.

## What we would like Emily to check

1. In a 1980s furnace recipe written “Boat-in 3 min, Oxidation variable”, would the printed oxidation time start after the boat-in? For B1 (1.5 min printed) it decides most of the oxide.
2. For batch K, oxidized at 900 °C and annealed in situ at 1050 °C: how long would such a furnace take to climb 150 °C, and would the tube be in nitrogen during the climb?
3. Fixed charge of 3.9 to 6 × 10^11 cm⁻² after 30 min of nitrogen at 1050 °C is higher than Deal's values for the same anneal (about 1.5 to 2 × 10^11). Is this the thin oxides, the φMS of -0.715 V, or the laboratory?
4. The P series (20 min in 20% O2 at 1050 °C) is about 180 Å where U2 on the same nominal recipe is 153 Å. Is a 15% run-to-run difference in thickness usual for such a furnace?
5. The trap density rises again after 10 min of nitrogen at 1050 °C but not at 900 °C. Is that a known effect (nitrogen at the interface, as the author suggests), or could the withdrawal after the anneal differ between the two series?
