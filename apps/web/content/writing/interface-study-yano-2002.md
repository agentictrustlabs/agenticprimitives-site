## The study and why we depend on it

**H. Yano, T. Hirao, T. Kimoto, H. Matsunami and H. Shiomi, “Interface properties in metal-oxide-semiconductor structures on n-type 4H-SiC(03-38)”, Appl. Phys. Lett. 81, 4772 (2002).**

A second laboratory's interface-state densities on SiC, after wet oxidation, a low-temperature argon anneal and a forming-gas anneal: three steps no SiC program we hold was built for. The page records what the predictor returns and why it should not be trusted here.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Moderate.** Every furnace step has its temperature, time and gas class: wet oxidation at 1150 °C (25 or 120 min), argon in situ at 1150 °C for 30 min, forming gas at 400 °C for 30 min. What a simulation must supply: how the steam was made, the heat-up and cooling around each step, and the switch from steam to argon. The two samples also differ in doping and oxide thickness, not only in face.

### What the second reading changed

- The project held this paper as “held, not yet transcribed”: there was no stored recipe and no dataset. The Dit values here are the second reader's digitization of Figure 3 (pixel calibration), not a project dataset.
- The project's note calls the paper “interface state density against energy on two faces … (and the effect of anneals)”. The paper has one process for both faces and no anneal comparison: one wet oxidation, one argon anneal and one forming-gas anneal for every sample.
- The two faces were not oxidized for the same time (25 against 120 min) and carry different doping (3.2×10¹⁷ against 8.7×10¹⁶ cm⁻³) and oxide thickness (67 against 45 nm); a face comparison is also a comparison of these.
- The (11-20) points on Figure 3 are from another paper (the authors' ref. 13) and are not reproduced here.

# ISL MODEL: INTERFACE STATES OF SiC/SiO2 THROUGH ARGON ANNEALS
#
# Two parts. One is stable. The other is removable: a hold in argon takes it down to a level
# set by the anneal temperature, and it does not come back at a lower temperature. The density
# at each energy below the conduction band is the stable part plus the removable part, each
# with its own weight at that energy.
#
# The form was chosen among three (decay to nothing, a reversible equilibrium, a one-way level)
# by scripts/run_sic_interface_states.py, on the samples oxidized at 1150 C. How fast the level
# is reached is not resolved by any source we hold (the anneals are "1 to 10 min"), so the rate
# is a hypothesis: fast enough to finish within a minute.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by the runner.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE {sample_id} {{
    FAMILY: SiC_SiO2
    ORIENTATION: 4H_SiC_Si_face
    CLEAN: not_stated
}}
STATE removable {{ UNIT: 1; INITIAL: 1; MIN: 0; MAX: 1 }}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_Ar {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER log_level {{ VALUE: -0.3; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_level {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_S_0p3 {{ VALUE: 11.8; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_S_0p4 {{ VALUE: 11.5; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_S_0p5 {{ VALUE: 11.2; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_R_0p3 {{ VALUE: 12.4; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_R_0p4 {{ VALUE: 12.3; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_R_0p5 {{ VALUE: 12.1; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER settle_rate {{ VALUE: 0.1; UNIT: s^-1; STATUS: hypothesis }}
PARAMETER way {{ VALUE: {way}; UNIT: 1; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER soft {{ VALUE: 1.0e-3; UNIT: 1; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER T_ref {{ VALUE: 1573.15; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER D_unit {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: cm^-2 eV^-1; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER ln10 {{ VALUE: 2.302585092994046; UNIT: 1; STATUS: constant }}

# The level the anneal temperature allows, as a fraction of the as-oxidized removable part.

**What was searched.** One unknown, steam still in the tube at the switch to argon (1 s to 10 min). It cannot reach the Dit rows, and on thickness it moves the numbers from 7.6 to 8.1 nm and from 2.4 to 3.1 nm. The search counts stayed at 0 of 6 everywhere; the combined search still moved it to 10 min on median error alone (89.7% to 88.7%). That move is not evidence.

The 2.3 to 4 times lower density after this wet process than after Kobayashi's dry oxidation is consistent with the known benefit of wet oxidation on SiC, but this page cannot separate the wet oxide, the forming gas and the laboratory.

**Quantum ESPRESSO switched on.** The computed record (`evaluations/atomistic/si_dangling_bond_hydrogen.json`) is for a silicon dangling bond with three silicon back-bonds, the Pb centre of Si/SiO2. What remains after the hydrogen step here is not limited by the Si–H bond.

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- A SiC face vocabulary beyond Si, C and a: (03-38) is written here as `4H_SiC_03-38_face`, a new word no program or ontology entry knows. Faces should be Miller indices (with polytype and off-cut), so a program can say which it has constants for and the runtime can refuse a misspelling.
- The interface-state program's energies should be the sample's to choose (`OBSERVE Dit AT Ec-0.15 eV`), or the program should report the whole curve it can support; today only 0.3, 0.4 and 0.5 eV exist, and this paper's result lies at 0.1 to 0.2 eV.
- The sic_interface_states program returns nothing (not even a refusal with a reason) when a history has no dry-oxygen step. A wet history should be refused with a stated reason, so the page can say why, rather than appear as “no program”.
- In situ steps: a statement that two RUNs are in the same tube with no unload between them (`IN_SITU`), against Kobayashi's anneal in another furnace (a transfer through air). Here the argon anneal is in situ, and the gas switch in between is an unknown we had to write as an extra step.
- The measurement temperature and method on an observable (`MEASURE Dit METHOD conductance AT 300 K`; C-V at 100 K): the same sample gives different counts of shallow states by method and temperature, which is how this paper argues.
