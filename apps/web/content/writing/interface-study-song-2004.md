## The study and why we depend on it

**Y. Song, S. Dhar, L. C. Feldman, G. Chung and J. R. Williams, “Modified Deal Grove model for the thermal oxidation of silicon carbide”, J. Appl. Phys. 95, 4953 (2004).**

The challenge's reference for SiC. The graph holds 43 thicknesses read from its Figures 3 to 5, and the SiC growth program is the silicon growth operator unchanged with this paper's tabulated rate constants. The 1050 °C curves are held out: their constants are interpolated from the neighbouring temperatures, not read from the paper.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**High for growth.** One step, fully stated: dry oxygen at one atmosphere at a stated temperature. The weakness is in the data, not the history: thickness appears only on figures, and the paper does not say whether the points of a curve are separate samples.

|---|---|
| Specimen described | 3 of 5 properties we need are stated and encoded the same |
| History described | 2 of 5 steps are stated and encoded the same; 2 are stated by the paper and absent from our history; 1 are ours because the paper is silent |
| Timing | 0 of 1 simulated steps have a stated duration; 4 of 4 other steps have none; 5 timing gaps listed below |
| How the results are given | Thickness only in Figures 3 to 5 (read from the page by an agent, about ±2 nm); rate constants in printed tables |
| Repeatability stated by the authors | Not stated; RBS and ellipsometry “agreed well within the limits of experimental errors” |
| Internal consistency | Good. One ratio in the text (1.60) has no temperature and differs from the table's |
| Is our model independent of this paper's data? | No, except at 1050 °C. The rate constants are the paper's own fits to these curves, so agreement at the tabulated temperatures shows we read the paper correctly; only the 1050 °C curves, whose constants we interpolate, test anything |
| Outcome | 21 of 24 numbers within tolerance |

The grade is ours and is about the paper as a source for simulation, not about the quality of the experiment: **high** means the specimen, every step and its timing are stated well enough that a simulation needs no choices of ours; **moderate** that some choices are ours and are listed; **low** that the result depends on choices the paper does not constrain.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- Whether each point is a separate sample or one sample oxidized again and again. If cumulative, every point includes the earlier unloads and reloads.
- The moment time zero is counted from: loading, reaching temperature, or admitting oxygen. For the shortest times (15 and 30 min) this matters.
- Loading temperature, push rate and ramp.
- Cooling and unloading: nothing is said.
- The oxidation times themselves are only on the figure axes.

### What the second reading changed

- Our stored recipe (one hold in dry oxygen at the stated temperature) is what the paper states. The second reading found nothing our encoding contradicts.
- The paper never says whether the points on a curve are separate samples or one sample re-oxidized. Our simulation treats each time as an uninterrupted oxidation from a clean surface.
- The Si-face activation energies rest on three temperatures: Figure 5 plots 950 and 1000 °C points but the tables have no constants there. We kept only the three tabulated temperatures.
- Figures 3 to 5 do not say whether the plotted thickness is from RBS or ellipsometry; in Figure 2 the two differ visibly at 6 h on the C face.

## Their terms and ours

| The paper says | We write | Kind in our vocabulary | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| X: oxide thickness | `Thickness` | observable (target: layer_thickness); quantity oxide-thickness |  |
| B: parabolic rate constant; B/A: linear rate constant | `B, B_over_A` | PARAMETER, STATUS sourced (Tables I and II) | One pair per face and temperature, as the paper tabulates them. |
| τ (time offset) | `(zero)` | INITIAL of the state x_ox | The paper sets τ to zero. |
| C face, Si face, a face | `ORIENTATION: 4H_SiC_C_face …` | specimen field; process factor orientation |  |
| dry oxidation at atmospheric pressure, 1 l/min | `dry_O2` | ambient | Flow is not expressible and no program reads it. |
| CO out-diffusion | `FLUX of carbon out of the oxide` | operator kind Transport | The paper's modification of Deal-Grove enters through A and B; the growth law keeps its form. |
| RBS / spectroscopic ellipsometry | `Thickness` | observable | Two techniques, one observable; the paper says they agree within error. |

The model itself, `experiments/song_sic/oxide_growth_sic.isl.template`, as it is written. Every equation the simulation uses is in this text; the runtime holds no physics.

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: DRY THERMAL OXIDATION OF 4H-SiC (growth sector on a second material)
#
# The OPERATOR and OBSERVATION_MODEL blocks below are copied character for character
# from experiments/vitkavage_dit/oxide_growth.isl.template, the silicon program; a test
# checks that they are identical. Only the declared parameters differ.
#
# Song et al. (2004) fit their SiC data with the same linear-parabolic law, reading its
# two constants as including out-diffusion of the carbon product. The constants here are
# Arrhenius fits to that paper's Tables I and II, per crystal face
# (scripts/build_song_dataset.py). Nothing is fitted by us.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_song_sic.py.
# ==============================================================================
SPECIMEN {specimen_id} {{
    FAMILY: SiC_SiO2
    ORIENTATION: 4H_SiC_{face}_face
}}
STATE x_ox {{
    UNIT: cm
    INITIAL: x_i
    MIN: 0
    MAX: 1.0e-3
}}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_O2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER B0 {{ VALUE: {B0}; UNIT: cm^2 s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
PARAMETER E_B {{ VALUE: {E_B}; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
PARAMETER BA0 {{ VALUE: {BA0}; UNIT: cm s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
PARAMETER E_BA {{ VALUE: {E_BA}; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
# The constants above are already specific to the crystal face, so no further factor is applied.
PARAMETER r_or {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: 1; STATUS: constant }}
# The paper sets the time offset to zero and reports a native oxide of about 2 angstrom.
PARAMETER x_i {{ VALUE: 2.0e-8; UNIT: cm; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Song et al., J. Appl. Phys. 95, 4953 (2004), native oxide about 2 angstrom, agent-read }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY {trajectory_id} {{
    RUN Oxidation DURATION {duration_s} TEMP {T_C}->{T_C} AMBIENT dry_O2
}}
# dx/dt = B / (2x + A), with A = B / (B/A).
OPERATOR F_LinearParabolicGrowth : OxideGrowth {{
    EQUATION: d(x_ox)/dt = (p_O2 / p_ref) * B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (2 * x_ox + B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (r_or * BA0 * exp(-E_BA / (k_B * T))))
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Oxide_Thickness {{
    TARGET: Thickness
    UNIT: cm
    EQUATION: Thickness = x_ox
}}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT {contract_id} {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/song_2004_sic_thickness.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{dataset_sha256}
    SPLIT: {split}
{targets}
}}
```

### The paper's own conclusions

| The paper states | What we find | |
|---|---|---|
| Growth is fastest on the C face and slowest on the Si face, with the a face between | Reproduced: the three faces order the same way at every temperature | direction right |
| Activation energy of B/A: 1.29, 1.20, 1.34 eV for the C, a and Si faces; of B: 1.99, 2.94, 3.12 eV | Not derived by us: the program takes the tabulated constants at each temperature | not tested |
| Thickness against time follows X² + AX = Bt on all three faces | A power law in thickness does as well on these points; a 24 h oxidation would separate them | not singled out |
