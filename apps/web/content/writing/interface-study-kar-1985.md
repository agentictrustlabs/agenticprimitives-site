# Reproducing Kar, Shanker and Chari 1985: interface states against oxide thickness in unannealed ultrathin oxides

*One of the [studies we reproduced](/writing/interface-studies-reproduced) for [Questions we need Emily to answer](/writing/questions-for-emily-testing-a-theory-of-the-interface), the companion to [the essay](/writing/testing-a-theory-of-the-interface-with-an-ontology). Generated by `scripts/reproduce_studies.py` from `reproductions/kar_1985.py`. Every value from the paper was read by an AI agent and has not been checked by a person; the sample and history were re-read from the PDF alone by a second agent that had not seen our earlier transcription.*

## The study and why we depend on it

**S. Kar, D. Shanker and K. S. Chari, “Dependence of Si-SiO2 interface state density on oxide thickness in structures with ultrathin (79-227 Å) oxides”, Appl. Phys. Lett. 47 (11), 1203-1205 (1985).**

One of the few sources of interface state density on oxides thinner than 25 nm measured with no anneal at all, so that every dangling bond the oxidation left is still there: the case the trap-density program's unannealed branch is meant for. Its central result is that the density does not fall monotonically with thickness: it dips near 110-125 Å and rises to a maximum near 150 Å before falling again, in two n-type sets and a p-type set. The project held it unread; the bundle note said “two thicknesses at 900 C”. There are six per set, eighteen in all.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Low.** Temperature, gas and pressure of the final oxidation are stated (900 °C, dry O2, 1 atm), and that no anneal followed. No oxidation time is printed for any sample, nor the load, pull or cooling, nor the time between the HF strip of the 1100 °C preoxide and loading. Thickness is given for one set (the Figure 1 legend) and otherwise only as positions on an axis. Every density is a graph reading on a log axis, and the reported quantity is the peak of a band-edge distribution, not the midgap value a program predicts.

|---|---|
| Sample described | 1 of 5 properties we need are stated and encoded the same |
| History described | 3 of 10 steps are stated and encoded the same; 4 are stated by the paper and absent from our history; 2 are ours because the paper is silent |
| Timing | 0 of 2 simulated steps have a stated duration; 5 of 7 other steps have none; 6 timing gaps listed below |
| How the results are given | Figures 1 and 2 only, log axes, read by eye from a scan; no table. Midgap-region values only for the six profiles of Figure 1, and only as the lowest point of each profile near 0.7 eV |
| Repeatability stated by the authors | Not stated as a number. A second n-type set made later to check reproducibility: at like thicknesses the two sets differ by 7 to 17% in the peak, but at about 125 Å by a factor of 1.8 (0.40 against 0.71 × 10¹² at 103 Å) |
| Internal consistency | Fair. Abstract 79-227 Å, Figure 1 caption 91-208 Å (n-type only); the extremes come from the p-type set. Both n-type sets and the p-type set show the same valley near 110-125 Å and peak near 140-150 Å |
| Is our model independent of this paper's data? | Yes. Nothing in the predictor was fitted to this paper. The trap-density program was fitted on Vitkavage 1990 (dry oxidations of one hour or more, 750 to 1000 °C); every history here is shorter than 83 min, so outside its tested scope |
| Outcome | 0 of 24 numbers within tolerance |

The grade is ours and is about the paper as a source for simulation, not about the quality of the experiment: **high** means the sample, every step and its timing are stated well enough that a simulation needs no choices of ours; **moderate** that some choices are ours and are listed; **low** that the result depends on choices the paper does not constrain.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- Oxidation time at 900 °C: not printed for any sample.
- Load temperature, push, pull, cooling rate and cooling gas after the 900 °C oxidation: not stated (no post-oxidation anneal is stated).
- Time between the HF strip of the preoxide and loading for the final oxidation (native regrowth): not stated.
- Ramps and cooling of the 1100 °C preoxidation: not stated.
- Time between oxidation and metal evaporation, and between evaporation and measurement: not stated.
- The interval between the first and second n-type sets: “a period of time”.

### What the second reading changed

- The source note (data/primary_sources/kar_shanker_chari_1985_apl_47_1203/SOURCE.md) says “two thicknesses at 900 C”. The paper has six thicknesses in each of three sets (two n-type, one p-type), 79 to 227 Å.
- The library lists this source as held and used by nothing, quantity trap_density. Its numbers are peak densities near a band edge (Figure 2), the same kind of quantity the library notes as not comparable with midgap values for Kar and Narasimhan 1987; only Figure 1 gives values near midgap, and only by eye.
- No stored recipe or dataset exists for this paper; every history here is new. The oxidation times are ours, chosen to reproduce the thicknesses.
- The sacrificial 1100 °C preoxidation is not simulated. Written as a RUN it would be taken by the trap program as the oxidation (variant below), and the language cannot strip it.

## Their terms and ours

| The paper says | We write | Kind in our vocabulary | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| interface state density Nis (cm⁻² V⁻¹) | `Dit (target: interface state density at midgap)` | observable (target: trap_density) | Per volt equals per eV for unit charge. The program gives the midgap value only. |
| Nis peak near Ev / near Ec | `(none)` | no program | The band-edge peak of a distribution; the predictor gives a distribution only after a hydrogen anneal. |
| concave (U-shaped) background | `(none)` | no program |  |
| unannealed; no post-oxidation or post-metallization annealing | `no RUN after the pull; EVENT NoAnneal` | absence of a step |  |
| preoxidation ... part of the surface cleaning | `EVENT Preoxidation, EVENT Strip` | events | The language has no statement that removes oxide. |
| dry O2 at 900 °C at 1 atm | `RUN Oxidation ... TEMP 900->900 AMBIENT dry_O2` | a RUN; process factor oxidation | Time ours. |
| oxide thickness tox | `oxide thickness (thin-oxide program); OXIDE_NM_MEASURED` | observable (target: layer_thickness) and a recorded sample field |  |
| field-aided to field-free diffusion of oxidant (Deal-Grove initial regime, Cabrera-Mott) | `(none)` | no program | The authors' explanation of the peak near 150 Å. |
| Si3≡Si bonding defects (Poindexter) | `N_Pb in the trap program` | STATE | The program's dangling-bond population; no energy distribution. |
| illuminated capacitance and conductance | `(none)` | observation method not carried |  |

```isl
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
Structure search for midgap trap density on planar silicon (Vitkavage 1990), with and without the hydrogen anneal.
What the measurements show before any model: the forming-gas anneal lowers the density 5 to 8 times on (100) but 10 to
29 times on (110), and on (110) the unannealed density does not fall with time at 1000 C while the annealed one does.
The grammar asks which structure accounts for that:
    one_population          sites relax with time at temperature; hydrogen caps them at one rate (the form used so far)
    orientation_passivation the same, with the hydrogen rate fitted for each orientation
    two_populations         a second population set at oxidation, per orientation, that hydrogen removes and time does not
    two_populations_T       the same, with the second population depending on oxidation temperature
Every candidate is an ISL program (kinds Relaxation and Passivation). Split, fixed here: the 900 C recipes and the
8 h recipes are held out; everything else is fitted. (The earlier split held out the (511) orientation; a structure
with a number per orientation cannot be scored that way, so the split was changed and that is recorded here.)
The structure is chosen on training rows only: most passed, then fewest parameters, then smallest training error.
Output: evaluations/trap_density_search_report.json
"""
import json
import math
import os
import sys
import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import least_squares
from isl_harness.isl_runtime import ISLRuntime
from scripts import run_vitkavage_dit as d
ROOT = os.path.abspath(os.path.join(os.path.dirname(__file__), ".."))
REPORT = os.path.join(ROOT, "evaluations", "trap_density_search_report.json")
K_B, T_REF = 8.617333262e-5, 1173.15
STRUCTURES = ("one_population", "orientation_passivation", "two_populations", "two_populations_T")
ORIENTATIONS = ("100", "110", "111", "511")
# name: (start, low, high)
BASE = {"log_N_born": (12.0, 10.5, 13.5), "log_N_floor": (11.3, 9.5, 12.5), "log_k_ref": (-4.0, -7.0, -1.0), "E_r": (2.0, 0.0, 4.0), "log_k_H": (-2.0, -4.0, 0.0)}

def parameters(structure):
    p = dict(BASE)
    if structure == "orientation_passivation":
        p.update({f"log_h_{o}": (0.0, -1.5, 1.5) for o in ORIENTATIONS[1:]})
    if structure.startswith("two_populations"):
        p.update({f"log_B_{o}": (11.0, 9.0, 12.5) for o in ("100", "110", "511")})      # (111) has no unannealed measurement, so its second population cannot be fitted
        if structure.endswith("_T"):
            p["E_B"] = (0.5, 0.0, 2.0)
    return p

def program(structure, row, sha):
    o, two = row["orientation"], structure.startswith("two_populations")
    runs = [f"    RUN Oxidation DURATION {row['t_ox_hr'] * 3600.0:.1f} TEMP {row['T_ox_C']:.1f}->{row['T_ox_C']:.1f} AMBIENT dry_O2"]
    if row["anneal"] == "PMA":
        runs.append("    RUN PostMetalAnneal DURATION 1800.0 TEMP 400.0->400.0 AMBIENT forming_gas_H2")
    second = f"""
STATE N_B {{ UNIT: cm^-2; INITIAL: B_unit * exp(ln10 * log_B) * exp((E_B / k_B) * (1 / T_ox - 1 / T_ref)); MIN: 0; MAX: 1.0e15 }}
STATE N_BH {{ UNIT: cm^-2; INITIAL: 0; MIN: 0; MAX: 1.0e15 }}
PARAMETER log_B {{ VALUE: 11.0; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_B {{ VALUE: 0.0; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER T_ox {{ VALUE: {row['T_ox_C'] + 273.15:.2f}; UNIT: K; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: the temperature of the oxidation step in this recipe }}
PARAMETER B_unit {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: cm^-2; STATUS: constant }}
# No source resolves how fast hydrogen removes this population; it is gone after the 30 min anneal in every sample.
PARAMETER k_HB {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm^-1 s^-1; STATUS: hypothesis }}
OPERATOR F_SecondPopulationPassivation : Passivation {{
    CONSERVATION: N_B + N_BH = const
    EQUATION: d(N_B)/dt = -k_HB * p_H2 * N_B
    EQUATION: d(N_BH)/dt = k_HB * p_H2 * N_B
}}""" if two else ""
    return f"""# GENERATED by scripts/search_trap_density.py. Structure: {structure}. A candidate from the grammar; every rate and level is fitted.
SAMPLE Wafer_{o}_{row['anneal']}_{row['T_ox_C']:.0f}C_{str(row['t_ox_hr']).replace('.', 'p')}h {{ FAMILY: Si_Planar; ORIENTATION: Si{o} }}
STATE N_Pb {{ UNIT: cm^-2; INITIAL: g_or * N_unit * exp(ln10 * log_N_born); MIN: 0; MAX: 1.0e15 }}
STATE N_SiH {{ UNIT: cm^-2; INITIAL: 0; MIN: 0; MAX: 1.0e15 }}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_H2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER log_N_born {{ VALUE: 12.0; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_N_floor {{ VALUE: 11.0; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_k_ref {{ VALUE: -4.0; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER E_r {{ VALUE: 2.0; UNIT: eV; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_k_H {{ VALUE: -2.0; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER log_h {{ VALUE: 0.0; UNIT: 1; STATUS: fitted }}
PARAMETER g_or {{ VALUE: {d.G_OR[o]}; UNIT: 1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Vitkavage, Irene and Massoud, ONR TR 34 (1990), Table VIII, ratio of silicon surface atom density to (100) }}
PARAMETER c_mid {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: eV^-1; STATUS: hypothesis }}
PARAMETER N_unit {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: cm^-2; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER rate_unit {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: s^-1; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER H_unit {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm^-1 s^-1; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER T_ref {{ VALUE: {T_REF}; UNIT: K; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER ln10 {{ VALUE: 2.302585092994046; UNIT: 1; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}{second}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY History {{
{chr(10).join(runs)}
}}
OPERATOR F_Relaxation : Relaxation {{
    EQUATION: d(N_Pb)/dt = -rate_unit * exp(ln10 * log_k_ref) * exp(-(E_r / k_B) * (1 / T - 1 / T_ref)) * (N_Pb - g_or * N_unit * exp(ln10 * log_N_floor))
}}
OPERATOR F_HydrogenPassivation : Passivation {{
    CONSERVATION: N_Pb + N_SiH = const
    EQUATION: d(N_Pb)/dt = -H_unit * exp(ln10 * (log_k_H + log_h)) * p_H2 * N_Pb
    EQUATION: d(N_SiH)/dt = H_unit * exp(ln10 * (log_k_H + log_h)) * p_H2 * N_Pb
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Dit_Midgap {{ TARGET: Dit; UNIT: cm^-2 eV^-1; EQUATION: Dit = c_mid * ({'N_Pb + N_B' if two else 'N_Pb'}) }}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT Contract {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/vitkavage_1990_dit_midgap.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{sha}
    SPLIT: search
    TARGET: Dit = {row['Dit']:.4g} +/- {d.CONTRACT_TOLERANCE} rel
}}
"""

def overrides(structure, x, names, orientation):
    v = dict(zip(names, [float(a) for a in x]))
    ov = {k: v[k] for k in BASE}
    ov["log_h"] = v.get(f"log_h_{orientation}", 0.0)
    if structure.startswith("two_populations"):
        ov["log_B"] = v.get(f"log_B_{orientation}", 0.0)       # (111): no second population can be fitted; it is left out (zero is 1 per cm2)
        ov["E_B"] = v.get("E_B", 0.0)
    return ov

def split_of(row):
    return "holdout" if row["T_ox_C"] == 900 or row["t_ox_hr"] == 8.0 else "train"

def evaluate(structure):
    rows, sha = d.load_rows()
    pars = parameters(structure)
    names = list(pars)
    rts = []
    for r in rows:
        rt = ISLRuntime()
        rt.parse_isl_source(program(structure, r, sha))
        rts.append(rt)
    meas = np.array([r["Dit"] for r in rows])
    train = [i for i, r in enumerate(rows) if split_of(r) == "train"]
    def predict(x, idx):
        out = []
        for i in idx:
            try:
                out.append(next(iter(rts[i].execute_isl_evaluation(parameter_overrides=overrides(structure, x, names, rows[i]["orientation"]), base_dir=ROOT)["receipt"]["results"].values()))["pred"])
            except Exception:
                out.append(float("nan"))
        return np.array(out)
    def residuals(x):
        p = predict(x, train)
        return np.where(np.isfinite(p) & (p > 0), np.log(np.maximum(p, 1.0) / meas[train]) / 0.10, 30.0)
    rng, best = np.random.default_rng(7), None
    lo, hi = [pars[n][1] for n in names], [pars[n][2] for n in names]
    for x0 in [[pars[n][0] for n in names]] + [[float(rng.uniform(a, b)) for a, b in zip(lo, hi)] for _ in range(5)]:
        f = least_squares(residuals, x0=x0, bounds=(lo, hi), diff_step=1e-2, max_nfev=60)
        if best is None or f.cost < best.cost:
            best = f
    pred = predict(best.x, range(len(rows)))
    samples = [{"orientation": r["orientation"], "anneal": r["anneal"], "T_ox_C": r["T_ox_C"], "t_ox_hr": r["t_ox_hr"], "split": split_of(r), "measured": r["Dit"], "predicted": float(p),
                "passed": bool(abs(p - r["Dit"]) <= d.CONTRACT_TOLERANCE * r["Dit"])} for r, p in zip(rows, pred)]
    out = {"structure": structure, "n_fitted": len(names), "fitted": dict(zip(names, [float(v) for v in best.x])), "samples": samples}
    for split in ("train", "holdout"):
        sel = [s for s in samples if s["split"] == split]
        err = [abs(s["predicted"] - s["measured"]) / s["measured"] for s in sel]
        out[split] = {"n": len(sel), "passed": sum(s["passed"] for s in sel), "within_25_percent": int(sum(e <= 0.25 for e in err)), "median_error": round(float(np.median(err)), 3)}
    return out

def run(workers=1):
    if workers > 1:
        import multiprocessing
        with multiprocessing.Pool(workers) as pool:
            results = pool.map(evaluate, STRUCTURES)
    else:
        results = [evaluate(s) for s in STRUCTURES]
    key = lambda r: (-r["train"]["passed"], r["n_fitted"], r["train"]["median_error"])
    results.sort(key=key)
    report = {"what": "Structure search for midgap trap density on planar silicon, Vitkavage 1990. Candidates come from a grammar.",
              "data_verification": "Agent-transcribed values; four values ruled on by the project owner. Unverified otherwise.",
              "split": "held out: the 900 C recipes and the 8 h recipes; fitted: the rest. Changed from the earlier split (which held out (511)) because some structures have a number per orientation.",
              "selection_rule": "most training targets passed, then fewest parameters, then smallest training error; held-out scores are not used to choose",
              "selected": {k: v for k, v in results[0].items() if k != "samples"}, "ranking": [{k: v for k, v in r.items() if k != "samples"} for r in results],
              "samples_of_each": {r["structure"]: r["samples"] for r in results}}
    with open(REPORT, "w") as f:
        json.dump(report, f, indent=1)
    return report

if __name__ == "__main__":
    rep = run(int(sys.argv[sys.argv.index("--workers") + 1]) if "--workers" in sys.argv else 1)
    for r in rep["ranking"]:
        print(f"  {r['structure']:26s} parameters {r['n_fitted']:2d}  train {r['train']['passed']:2d}/{r['train']['n']} (25%: {r['train']['within_25_percent']}, median {r['train']['median_error']})"
              f"  held-out {r['holdout']['passed']:2d}/{r['holdout']['n']} (25%: {r['holdout']['within_25_percent']}, median {r['holdout']['median_error']})")
```

**Nothing the history leaves open brings the program down to the data.** The program gives 4.1 to 4.3 × 10¹¹ cm⁻² eV⁻¹ for every oxide; the profiles' lowest points are 1.65 to 2.95 × 10¹¹. Each unknown, scanned alone, leaves the count at 0 of 6. The search drives every one to the end of its range that lowers the program's value most (oxidation time doubled, a 600 s pull ending at 800 °C), and that buys 5 to 7%: 125 Å goes from 4.29 to 4.13 × 10¹¹, 208 Å from 4.12 to 3.83 × 10¹¹, against 1.65 and 2.0 measured.

**The aligned oxidation time is ruled out by the thickness the paper states.** Doubling the time makes the 93 Å oxide 15.2 nm and the 208 Å oxide 33 nm in the thin-oxide program. Thickness is not scored on this page because the stated times are ours and were chosen from it, so the search cannot see that; read with the printed thicknesses, the oxidation-time factor is pinned at 1 and the aligned improvement is not available.

**The pull's gas does not matter.** Cooling in nitrogen instead of oxygen changes no value by more than 1% (variant: 0 of 24 alone and with the aligned history).

**Writing the sacrificial oxidation as a RUN lands closer for the wrong reason.** With 30 min at 1100 °C in dry oxygen as the first RUN (it is stripped in HF in the paper; the language cannot strip it), the trap program takes 1100 °C as the oxidation temperature and its second population, which falls as the oxidation temperature rises, drops: 1.55 to 1.60 × 10¹¹ for all six oxides, 1 of 6 within tolerance (125 Å, 1.59 against 1.65 × 10¹¹; median error 26%). The simulated oxides are then 82 to 86 nm thick, four to nine times the paper's. This variant shows what the language does to a stripped preoxide, not a better history.

**Heating and cooling transitions** (section below): a 1 to 5 min hot push or a 60 to 120 min cold-furnace ramp in nitrogen, with fast or five-times-slower radiative cooling after the pull, move none of the 24 numbers by more than 2% (median error 105.8% as stated, 104.8 to 105.6% with transitions). In this program the short-time heat around a 900 °C oxidation is invisible; the value is set by the oxidation temperature.

**Quantum ESPRESSO bound.** The page has no section for it: no step in this paper carries hydrogen (no anneal of any kind, stated), so the computed equilibrium bound on the density after a hydrogen step does not apply.

What alignment cannot fix. **The paper's result is a shape the program cannot make.** Kar's peak density near Ev dips near 103-126 Å and peaks near 138-149 Å in both n-type sets (0.40 to 2.55 × 10¹² in set 2) and near Ec in the p-type set (0.71 at 90 Å to 4.0 × 10¹² at 148 Å). No program in the predictor gives a band-edge peak, and the predictor gives no distribution over energy without a hydrogen anneal (reason c). The one quantity it does give, midgap density, falls monotonically by 5% from 93 to 208 Å; the profiles' lowest points follow the peak's shape weakly (1.65 × 10¹¹ at 125 Å, 2.95 at 148 Å) and the program has no way to move up and then down with thickness.

**The level is 1.4 to 2.6 times high.** The program's unannealed value is set by the oxidation temperature (the second population) and by relaxation over time at temperature; it was fitted on Vitkavage's oxidations of one hour or more, and every history here is 15 to 82 min. Part of the gap may be the comparison itself: the measured value is the lowest point of each profile at 0.69 to 0.72 eV, not 0.56 eV, and four of the six profiles have no points between about 0.5 and 0.7 eV.

**The authors' mechanism has no counterpart.** They link the peak near 150 Å to the end of the rapid, field-aided initial oxidation regime. The thin-oxide growth program has an initial regime in its rate, but no program connects how the last layer grew to the defects it leaves (reason c).

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **An etch that removes oxide.** The 1100 °C preoxide is grown and stripped before the oxidation that matters. The language has no statement that removes oxide, so the step had to become an EVENT and its 30 min at 1100 °C left out of the simulated heat; written as a RUN it stays as about 70 nm of oxide under the real one. `ETCH REMOVE ALL` (or `ETCH TO 0 nm`) would let the strip be simulated and the heat kept.
- **Which RUN is the oxidation.** The trap-density program takes the oxidation temperature from the first dry-oxygen RUN. A sacrificial oxidation in front of the real one changes the predicted density by a factor of 2.7 (4.3 to 1.6 × 10¹¹) through that choice alone. A `ROLE sacrificial|oxidation|anneal|pull` on each RUN would let programs find the oxidation by what it is (the same gap Hung 1987 hit with a boat-in).
- **A measured thickness as a constraint on an unstated time.** The paper states thicknesses, not times. Here the time was set by hand from the thickness and thickness was then dropped from the score, so the search could double the time unchecked. A sample field the alignment reads as a constraint (`OXIDE_NM_MEASURED` checked against the growth program while other quantities are searched) would pin the time and say so.
- **A band-edge peak as an observable.** Every number in Figure 2 is the height of a peak near Ev or Ec of an unannealed distribution. The observable vocabulary has midgap density and, after hydrogen only, a fixed set of energies. An observable `Dit_peak_near_Ev` / `Dit_peak_near_Ec` with its energy, and a program that predicts a distribution without hydrogen, are needed before any of the 18 peak values can be compared.
- **The clean.** “Degreasing in hot solvents, ultrasonic cleaning, and etching in acids” followed by a sacrificial oxide has no CLEAN word in the vocabulary; it is written `not_stated`. A `CLEAN: sacrificial_oxide_HF` would record the most consequential part of it.
- **How the measurement was made.** The densities are from illuminated admittance (capacitance and conductance, 30 Hz to 100 kHz) on 100 Å semitransparent Au or Al dots. Nothing in a history or result says which technique gave a density, and techniques disagree near the band edges; an observation statement with the method would let results from different methods be kept apart.
