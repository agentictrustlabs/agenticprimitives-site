## The study and why we depend on it

**Y. Hijikata, S. Yagi, H. Yaguchi and S. Yoshida, “Thermal Oxidation Mechanism of Silicon Carbide”, chapter 7 in Physics and Technology of Silicon Carbide Devices (InTech, 2013), doi:10.5772/50748.**

The second SiC growth source for challenge system 3, and the only one that measures the thin-oxide regime. Song 2004, whose constants the SiC growth program uses, measured thickness ex situ at 0.25 to 6 h; Hijikata's group measured the growth rate continuously by in-situ ellipsometry and found it higher below about 20 nm than a Deal-Grove law fitted to the thick part. This page runs the SiC growth program (Song's constants, dry, 950 to 1150 °C) on each in-situ run and compares thickness against time, rebuilt from the measured rates, and the rate itself at each thickness read.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Moderate.** A review chapter: the process is one sentence (dry O2, 1 atm, a temperature, a face) and every experimental detail is deferred to the authors' earlier papers. What it gives that no other source in our library does is the growth rate measured in situ against thickness, from about 1 nm, on both faces at 4 to 8 temperatures. No times are given: thickness against time has to be rebuilt by integrating the rates, from an unknown time zero.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- No time axis anywhere: every run's duration and time zero are unknown; the durations here are reconstructed by integrating the measured rates.
- Heat-up gas and time before oxygen is admitted (in refs [4, 5, 8-10]).
- The C-face 1 atm runs begin at 5 to 12 nm because the early growth was too fast to follow (p. 16): the time to reach the first point is unknown.
- Cooling and unload: not described (the measurement is in situ, so they do not affect the data).

### What the second reading changed

- There is no stored recipe or dataset for this chapter (ontology/library.json: status skimmed, used by nothing). SOURCE.md lists Figures 3, 5, 7 and 9 as the ones with data; Figure 1 (C face at 8 temperatures, linear axes) is the most complete and is used here.
- Figure 3's Si face at 1100 °C and Figure 5's 1100 °C curve agree point for point; they are taken to be the same run and only Figure 5 is used.
- Figure 5's y axis labels only 10⁻⁶ µm/s; the decade was taken from the minor-tick spacing and checked against Figure 3 at 1100 °C (both give 4.0 nm/h at 10 to 40 nm).
- The temperature of Figures 7 to 9 (partial pressures) is not printed in their captions; they are not used.

## Their terms and ours

| The paper says | We write | Kind in our vocabulary | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| oxide growth rate dX/dt against oxide thickness (in-situ ellipsometry) | `(none): Thickness at reconstructed times` | observable (target: layer_thickness) | The language has no rate observable; the rate is compared by integrating it to thickness. |
| Deal-Grove B, B/A | `B0, E_B, BA0, E_BA` | PARAMETER, STATUS sourced (Song 2004) | Hijikata's own D-G fits (Figure 1, solid lines) are not printed. |
| Massoud term C exp(-X/L) | `(none)` | no operator | The silicon thin-oxide program has a fitted term of this kind; the SiC program does not. |
| Si and C emission ratios ν_Si, ν_C | `(none)` | no operator in the SiC program | Emission into the oxide is a boundary flux; a FIELD with a FLUX can write it (Eq. 9 to 10). |
| interstitial Si and C in SiO2, C_Si, C_C, with D_Si, D_C, κ, η | `(none)` | FIELD + Transport + Exchange: writable, not written | Table 1 gives D_C, κ′, C_C⁰. |
| interfacial reaction k = k0 (1 − C_Si/C_Si⁰)(1 − C_C/C_C⁰) | `(none)` | OxideGrowth whose rate reads left(F) |  |
| oxygen partial pressure 0.02 to 1 atm | `mix:O2=p,N2=1-p` | ambient | The program scales its whole rate with p_O2; Hijikata finds the initial rate R_r nearly independent of p (Figure 8). |

The model itself, `experiments/song_sic/oxide_growth_sic.isl.template`, as it is written. Every equation the simulation uses is in this text; the runtime holds no physics.

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: DRY THERMAL OXIDATION OF 4H-SiC (growth sector on a second material)
#
# The OPERATOR and OBSERVATION_MODEL blocks below are copied character for character
# from experiments/vitkavage_dit/oxide_growth.isl.template, the silicon program; a test
# checks that they are identical. Only the declared parameters differ.
#
# Song et al. (2004) fit their SiC data with the same linear-parabolic law, reading its
# two constants as including out-diffusion of the carbon product. The constants here are
# Arrhenius fits to that paper's Tables I and II, per crystal face
# (scripts/build_song_dataset.py). Nothing is fitted by us.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_song_sic.py.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE {sample_id} {{
    FAMILY: SiC_SiO2
    ORIENTATION: 4H_SiC_{face}_face
}}
STATE x_ox {{
    UNIT: cm
    INITIAL: x_i
    MIN: 0
    MAX: 1.0e-3
}}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_O2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER B0 {{ VALUE: {B0}; UNIT: cm^2 s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
PARAMETER E_B {{ VALUE: {E_B}; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
PARAMETER BA0 {{ VALUE: {BA0}; UNIT: cm s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
PARAMETER E_BA {{ VALUE: {E_BA}; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
# The constants above are already specific to the crystal face, so no further factor is applied.
PARAMETER r_or {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: 1; STATUS: constant }}
# The paper sets the time offset to zero and reports a native oxide of about 2 angstrom.
PARAMETER x_i {{ VALUE: 2.0e-8; UNIT: cm; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Song et al., J. Appl. Phys. 95, 4953 (2004), native oxide about 2 angstrom, agent-read }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY {trajectory_id} {{
    RUN Oxidation DURATION {duration_s} TEMP {T_C}->{T_C} AMBIENT dry_O2
}}
# dx/dt = B / (2x + A), with A = B / (B/A).
OPERATOR F_LinearParabolicGrowth : OxideGrowth {{
    EQUATION: d(x_ox)/dt = (p_O2 / p_ref) * B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (2 * x_ox + B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (r_or * BA0 * exp(-E_BA / (k_B * T))))
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Oxide_Thickness {{
    TARGET: Thickness
    UNIT: cm
    EQUATION: Thickness = x_ox
}}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT {contract_id} {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/song_2004_sic_thickness.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{dataset_sha256}
    SPLIT: {split}
{targets}
}}
```

No time zero fixes the thin regime: the program's linear rate B/A (Song's, from ex-situ points at 0.25 h and later) is the program's rate at zero thickness, and it is 1.5 to 2.7 times what was measured in situ at 1 to 10 nm. This is the opposite of the chapter's own finding against its own Deal-Grove fit (rates above D-G below 20 nm): the two laboratories' B/A differ by about a factor of 2 on the C face (Song 108 nm/h at 1090 °C; Hijikata's solid line in Figure 1 starts near 50). Alignment cannot touch B/A or B: they are sourced constants.

The Si face needs a different B, not a different history: with Song's B the program cannot grow at a constant 4 nm/h from 10 to 40 nm at 1100 °C. Song's Si-face activation energy for B (3.12 eV) rests on three temperatures.

Two-stage deceleration (break at 7 nm, initial rate independent of oxygen pressure, Figures 7 and 8) needs a program with a second growth channel; the Si and C emission model the chapter describes is one, and the language can write it (below).

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **A rate observable, measured at a thickness, not at a time.** The chapter's data are dX/dt against X. The language observes states at the end of a history, so the rate had to be integrated to thickness against a reconstructed time with an unknown time zero. An observation of a state's time derivative (`EQUATION: Growth_rate = d(x_ox)/dt`) and a result indexed by a state (`MEASURE Growth_rate AT Thickness = 1.0e-6`) would compare the rates directly and need no time zero; it would also have answered “is the initial rate enhanced?” in one row.
- **A measurement during a RUN** (`MEASURE <observable> DURING <run> EVERY <s>` or in situ), so an in-situ ellipsometry run is one history with many measured points instead of a curve sampled by re-running the history to each time.
- **Clock reference** (as for Song 2004): `TIME_ZERO at_gas_on | at_first_point`, so an in-situ run's time axis can be stated rather than searched.
- **Predictor: a thin-oxide channel in the SiC growth program**, with its own pressure law. The Si and C emission model is writable today: FIELDs for Si and C interstitials and oxygen in the oxide on a grid travelling with the interface, FLUX with D_Si, D_C, D_O, BOUNDARY fluxes ν_Si k C_O, ν_C k C_O and (2 − ν_Si − ν_C − α/2) k C_O at the interface (Eq. 10), an OxideGrowth rate k0 (1 − left(C_Si)/C_Si⁰)(1 − left(C_C)/C_C⁰) C_O / N0 (Eq. 8). Table 1 gives the oxide-side constants; ν_Si, ν_C and k0 per face and temperature exist only as points on Figure 6, so they would be graph reads. Its grid would need to follow the growing oxide (no grid of varying length today).
- **Predictor: the program scales its whole rate by p_O2.** Hijikata's initial rate R_r is nearly independent of pressure while R_g is proportional (Figure 8). Any SiC program used for low-pressure or ramp-in-diluted-oxygen histories needs the two separated.
