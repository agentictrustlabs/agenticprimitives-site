Every value from the paper was read by an AI agent and has not been checked by a person; the sample and history were re-read from the PDF alone by a second agent that had not seen our earlier transcription.*

## The study and why we depend on it

**S. K. Gupta and J. Akhtar, “Thermal Oxidation of Silicon Carbide (SiC) – Experimentally Observed Facts”, in M. Mukherjee (ed.), Silicon Carbide – Materials, Processing and Applications in Electronic Devices, InTech (2011), chapter 9, pp. 207–230.**

A second laboratory for the SiC growth program. Song 2004's constants (the only SiC growth constants we hold) are run on this chapter's dry-oxygen thickness curves for the Si and C faces at 1000, 1050, 1110 and 1150 °C (48 thicknesses read from Figure 10), and the chapter's own rate constants are set beside Song's. The wet-oxygen curves have no SiC program to run.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Moderate.** The furnace cycle is described better than in most papers: load and unload at 800 °C in nitrogen, ramps at 5 °C/min, a dry or bubbler-wet oxygen hold. What is missing is the gas during the ramps, the flow of dry oxygen, and a single statement of the oxidation times: the text, Figure 3 and Figure 10 give three different sets.

| | |
|---|---|
| Sample described | 2 of 5 properties we need are stated and encoded the same |
| History described | 2 of 8 steps are stated and encoded the same; 3 are stated by the paper and absent from our history; 1 are ours because the paper is silent |
| Timing | 2 of 4 simulated steps have a stated duration; 2 of 4 other steps have none; 6 timing gaps listed below |
| How the results are given | Thickness only on Figure 10 (read from the page, about ±1 nm on the Si face and ±3 nm on the C face); rate constants and activation energies in printed tables without units (µm²/h and µm/h from the axes of Figures 15 and 16) |
| Repeatability stated by the authors | Four samples per condition, “statistically plotted”; no scatter or error bars given |
| Internal consistency | Fair. The fourth temperature column of Tables 1 and 2 is printed 1050 for 1150 °C; the oxidation times differ between text and figures; the Si-face B/A at 1150 °C (0.58 µm/h) gives A ≈ 1 nm, which the straight lines of Figure 16 do not show |
| Is our model independent of this paper's data? | Yes. Nothing in our SiC programs was fitted to this chapter. The growth program uses Song 2004's tabulated constants, so every number here tests whether Song's constants transfer to another laboratory's furnace |
| Outcome | 17 of 48 numbers within tolerance |

The grade is ours and is about the paper as a source for simulation, not about the quality of the experiment: **high** means the sample, every step and its timing are stated well enough that a simulation needs no choices of ours; **moderate** that some choices are ours and are listed; **low** that the result depends on choices the paper does not constrain.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- The gas during the 5 °C/min ramps (40 to 70 min each way): not stated.
- Whether the oxidation time includes any of the ramp: not stated; Figure 3 draws it as the plateau only.
- The oxidation times: the text says 30 to 180 min in 30 min steps, Figure 3 says 0.5 to 6 h in 0.5 h steps, Figure 10 plots 0.5, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 h.
- Time at 800 °C after loading and before unloading, and the cooling from 800 °C: not stated.
- Time from the last HF dip to loading: not stated.
- Dry oxygen flow: not stated (wet: 0.4 LPM through the bubbler).

### What the second reading changed

- The project held this chapter as “skimmed”, with the tables transcribed in `tables_agent_read_UNVERIFIED.csv` and not used “until the units are settled from the chapter's figures”. The units are settled by Figures 15 and 16 (X0 in µm, t/X0 in h/µm): B in µm²/h, B/A in µm/h. The transcription's values agree with the second reading's, entry for entry.
- The transcription keeps the printed “1050” for the fourth column; the text (p. 15, p. 19) and Figure 10 make it 1150 °C.
- No stored recipe exists for this chapter: there was no dataset. The thicknesses scored here are the second reader's readings of Figure 10, not a project dataset.
- Our sample has Song's 0.2 nm native oxide at the start, since the SiC growth program does not read STARTING_OXIDE_NM.

## Their terms and ours

| The paper says | We write | Kind in our vocabulary | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| X0: oxide thickness | `Thickness` | observable (target: layer_thickness) |  |
| B: parabolic rate constant; B/A: linear rate constant (Tables 1, 2) | `B, B_over_A` | PARAMETER (would be STATUS sourced) | Not used by any program: the program uses Song's. Units not printed; µm²/h and µm/h from the figure axes. |
| τ fixed to zero | `INITIAL x_ox` | INITIAL of the state | Same convention as Song. |
| Si-face, C-face (“terminating faces”) | `ORIENTATION: 4H_SiC_Si_face, 4H_SiC_C_face` | sample field; process factor orientation |  |
| dry oxidation, continuous flow of dry oxygen | `dry_O2` | ambient | Flow not stated and not expressible. |
| wet oxidation: O2 through a DI-water bubbler at 85 °C, 0.4 LPM | `mix:H2O=0.57,O2=0.43 (saturated at 85 °C, ours)` | ambient by partial pressure | No SiC program reads water. |
| load/unload at 800 °C in 1.9 LPM N2 | `EVENT Load KIND load; RUN … AMBIENT inert_N2` | event; ambient |  |
| ramp 5 °C/min | `RUN RampUp DURATION … TEMP 800->T` | a RUN; the rate is the duration | No statement for a rate. |
| GRMF (growth rate multiplication factor) | `(ratio of two Thickness observables)` | not in vocabulary | A derived quantity. |
| initial growth rate, average growth rate (eq. 6) | `(none)` | not in vocabulary | A rate against thickness is not an observable of ours. |

The model itself, `experiments/song_sic/oxide_growth_sic.isl.template`, as it is written. Every equation the simulation uses is in this text; the runtime holds no physics.

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: DRY THERMAL OXIDATION OF 4H-SiC (growth sector on a second material)
#
# The OPERATOR and OBSERVATION_MODEL blocks below are copied character for character
# from experiments/vitkavage_dit/oxide_growth.isl.template, the silicon program; a test
# checks that they are identical. Only the declared parameters differ.
#
# Song et al. (2004) fit their SiC data with the same linear-parabolic law, reading its
# two constants as including out-diffusion of the carbon product. The constants here are
# Arrhenius fits to that paper's Tables I and II, per crystal face
# (scripts/build_song_dataset.py). Nothing is fitted by us.
# Fields in {{braces}} are filled by scripts/run_song_sic.py.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE {sample_id} {{
    FAMILY: SiC_SiO2
    ORIENTATION: 4H_SiC_{face}_face
}}
STATE x_ox {{
    UNIT: cm
    INITIAL: x_i
    MIN: 0
    MAX: 1.0e-3
}}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_O2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER B0 {{ VALUE: {B0}; UNIT: cm^2 s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
PARAMETER E_B {{ VALUE: {E_B}; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
PARAMETER BA0 {{ VALUE: {BA0}; UNIT: cm s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
PARAMETER E_BA {{ VALUE: {E_BA}; UNIT: eV; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: {source} }}
# The constants above are already specific to the crystal face, so no further factor is applied.
PARAMETER r_or {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: 1; STATUS: constant }}
# The paper sets the time offset to zero and reports a native oxide of about 2 angstrom.
PARAMETER x_i {{ VALUE: 2.0e-8; UNIT: cm; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Song et al., J. Appl. Phys. 95, 4953 (2004), native oxide about 2 angstrom, agent-read }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER k_B {{ VALUE: 8.617333262e-5; UNIT: eV K^-1; STATUS: constant }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY {trajectory_id} {{
    RUN Oxidation DURATION {duration_s} TEMP {T_C}->{T_C} AMBIENT dry_O2
}}
# dx/dt = B / (2x + A), with A = B / (B/A).
OPERATOR F_LinearParabolicGrowth : OxideGrowth {{
    EQUATION: d(x_ox)/dt = (p_O2 / p_ref) * B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (2 * x_ox + B0 * exp(-E_B / (k_B * T)) / (r_or * BA0 * exp(-E_BA / (k_B * T))))
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Oxide_Thickness {{
    TARGET: Thickness
    UNIT: cm
    EQUATION: Thickness = x_ox
}}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT {contract_id} {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/song_2004_sic_thickness.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{dataset_sha256}
    SPLIT: {split}
{targets}
}}
```

The Si-face deficit is in the constants, not the history: no offset or ramp gas reaches 10% on the Si face without putting the C face 10% high. The program has one set of Si-face constants, from Song's three temperatures on on-axis wafers. This chapter does not state its off-cut (Figure 1(a) has no text layer); Goto and Hijikata's 8° off-axis Si face is also faster than Song's (see their page). OFFCUT read by the program, with constants per off-cut, is what this would need.

The 0.5 h points are 10 to 30% low on the C face and 42 to 64% low on the Si face: the linear-parabolic law with Song's B/A has no fast initial regime. A thin-oxide term (Massoud's, or Goto and Hijikata's R_S exp(−X/L_S)) is the structural change this asks for; no history change reaches it.

Wet oxidation has no SiC program. The chapter gives what one needs: B and B/A for both faces at four temperatures in 85 °C-bubbler wet oxygen (Tables 1 and 2). Its Si-face wet constants at 1150 °C (B = 1.2×10⁻³ µm²/h, B/A = 0.885 µm/h) give 48.3 nm after 2 h; Yano et al. 2002 measured 45 nm after 120 min of wet oxidation at 1150 °C on (0001), another laboratory. A wet SiC program would be the growth operator with these constants, scaled by water pressure the way the silicon wet operator is (`WET_GROWTH` in isl_harness/predict.py), with the bubbler temperature written as the water partial pressure. The (03-38) and a faces would still have none.

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- OFFCUT read by the SiC growth program (constants per face and off-cut). Song's Si face is on axis, Goto and Hijikata's is 8° off, this chapter's is unrecorded, and the Si-face constants do not transfer between them (28 to 64% low here). The field exists on samples and no program reads it.
- A ramp rate as a statement (`RAMP RATE 5 C/min FROM 800 TO 1150`), so the chapter's stated rate is written as the paper gives it, not as a duration we derive.
- The sic_growth program's scope check labels a history outside its tested scope whenever any step is not dry oxygen, so a load and ramp in nitrogen, which grows nothing, marks every number here “outside the tested scope”. The check should look only at the steps that grow oxide.
- Bubbler temperature as an ambient statement (`H2O FROM bubbler AT 85 C`) that the runtime turns into a water partial pressure, so a wet recipe is written as the paper states it rather than as our computed mixture.
- A cumulative or replicate series: `REPLICATES 4` on a measurement, so the four samples per point are part of the record (and their scatter, if a paper gives it, becomes the tolerance).
