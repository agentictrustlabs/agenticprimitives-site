## The study and why we depend on it

**B. J. O'Sullivan, P. K. Hurley, C. Leveugle and J. H. Das, “Si(100)–SiO2 interface properties following rapid thermal processing”, J. Appl. Phys. 89, 3811–3820 (2001).**

The paper that says why dangling-bond states are seen after some thermal steps and not after others: an inert anneal above about 500 °C takes the hydrogen off the Pb centres, and whether they are passivated again depends on the cooling rate and on hydrogen or moisture in the cooling ambient. That is a statement about the hydrogen step of the trap-density program run backwards, and about the history around it: a 10 s RTA in nitrogen at 925 to 1050 °C, the last step, raises the midgap density two- to three-fold, and a 60 min alloy in 5% H2 at 440 °C removes it. It is the test of whether our language can carry the hydrogen a history brings (silane, POCl3, moisture during a slow pull) and whether the predictor has a reverse of the capping reaction.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Moderate.** Oxidation temperatures, anneals, the RTA (10 s in nitrogen) and the RTO conditions are stated, with the forming-gas alloy's gas, temperature and time. Not stated: any oxidation time for the furnace oxides, the polysilicon deposition and POCl3 doping conditions for process 1 (and the POCl3 for process 2), the RTA ramps, the withdrawals after the 850 and 900 °C anneals, and how long each sample waited before measurement. The midgap densities exist only on log-axis figures; the printed numbers are band-edge peaks after a background is subtracted.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- Oxidation time at 850 °C (120 Å) and at 950 °C (260 Å).
- Process 1: the gas during the 3 °C/min ramp, the boat travel during the 8 cm/min pull from 600 °C, and so how long the wafer spends between 600 °C and room temperature.
- Process 1: polysilicon deposition temperature and time; POCl3 temperature and time (both processes).
- The withdrawal after the 900 °C (process 1) and 950 °C (process 2) nitrogen anneals.
- RTA heat-up and cool-down rates for processes 1 and 2 (only “10^1–10^2 °C/s” cooling in general).
- RTO: start temperature of the heat-up and its gas; the temperature at which the RTP cycle ends.
- Ramps into and out of the 440 °C alloy.
- Time between the last step and measurement (the RTA samples are said to be stable for six years).

### What the second reading changed

- No dataset and no script uses this paper; SOURCE.md says the figures and Table I were not digitized. The midgap values here are new graph readings and Table I is transcribed here for the first time.
- ontology/library.json gives the source's conditions as “pull: rapid thermal, 10 to 100 C/s” and “metal: mercury probe, no gate”. That describes processes 3–5 only. Processes 1 and 2 are polysilicon-gate capacitors whose oxides were furnace grown and pulled slowly (3 °C/min to 600 °C, then 8 cm/min, for process 1); only their last step is rapid.
- SOURCE.md says the note added in proof cites evidence that vacuum anneals create additional centres. The note says post-oxidation vacuum annealing (600–1100 °C) can generate additional Pb1 while Pb0 stays constant, and that this matters only if Pb1 is electrically active; the authors keep their conclusion that RTA creates no more states than a furnace anneal.
- SOURCE.md quotes peak densities 3e12 to 1.7e13; these are after subtracting a hydrogen-passivated background, not total densities.

| RTO, 100% O2 | `RUN RTO … AMBIENT dry_O2` | a RUN; process factor oxidation | The heat-up is written in nitrogen so that the trap-density program reads the hold as the oxidation. |
| steam-diluted RTO, 2.25 slpm H2O / 2.75 slpm N2 | `AMBIENT mix:H2O=0.45,N2=0.55` | ambient by partial pressure | Flows taken as partial pressures at 1 atm. |

| Pb0 / Pb1 silicon dangling-bond defects (Si3≡Si•) | `N_Pb (a STATE of the trap-density program)` | STATE | Ours counts one population that relaxes and one that hydrogen removes; Pb0 and Pb1 are not told apart. |
| hydrogen desorption from pre-existing Pb (Si3≡SiH → Si3≡Si• + H, above 500 °C) | `F_HydrogenRelease` | operator kind Passivation | N_SiH returns to N_Pb at k_d0 exp(-E_d/kT), Brower's k_d0 = 1.2×10^12 s⁻¹ and E_d = 2.56 eV (quoted by Stesmans and Afanas'ev 1996): 2×10⁻³ s⁻¹ at 600 °C (2% in 10 s), 20 s⁻¹ at 925 °C and 210 s⁻¹ at 1050 °C (all of it in 10 s). It needs H2 in the gas; it does not read water, so moisture while cooling does nothing. |
| cooling rate 10^1–10^2 °C/s; 3–10 °C/min furnace ramp; pull 5–20 cm/min | `RUN … TEMP T->T_end with a duration` | a RUN | A pull speed is not a cooling rate; the boat travel is not stated. |

| QSCV with the Berglund integral; 8 kHz HFCV; mercury probe | `Dit at midgap` | observable (target: trap_density) |  |
| short-loop experiment | `(none)` |  | A history that ends before the steps that would have brought hydrogen. |

The model itself, `experiments/atomistic/hydrogen_passivation_equilibrium.isl.template`, as it is written. Every equation the simulation uses is in this text; the runtime holds no physics.

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: HOW FAR HYDROGEN CAN CAP SILICON DANGLING BONDS AT EQUILIBRIUM
#
# Si* + 1/2 H2 <-> Si-H. The energy of the reaction is a COMPUTED parameter: a Quantum ESPRESSO
# calculation on a small cluster (atomistic/calcs/si_dangling_bond_hydrogen.json), not a measurement
# and not a fit. With the free energy of hydrogen gas it gives the equilibrium constant, and so the
# share of dangling bonds that hydrogen at a given temperature and pressure would leave uncapped if
# the anneal were long enough to reach equilibrium.
#
# What this is for: a bound. The trap-density program has a fitted share of sites that the
# hydrogen anneal does not remove. If equilibrium already leaves that share, the residue is
# thermodynamic; if equilibrium leaves far less, the residue is kinetic or is another defect.
# What it is not: a rate. How fast equilibrium is approached is the hypothesis k_eq below.
#
# Used by isl_harness/predict.py only when computed parameters are switched on (ISL_USE_COMPUTED=1
# or --computed). Fields in {{braces}} are filled from evaluations/atomistic/<id>.json.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE {sample_id} {{
    FAMILY: Si_Planar
}}
# Fractions of the sites: uncapped (electrically active) and capped by hydrogen.
STATE N_free {{ UNIT: 1; INITIAL: 1; MIN: 0; MAX: 1.000001 }}
STATE N_capped {{ UNIT: 1; INITIAL: 0; MIN: 0; MAX: 1.000001 }}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_H2 {{ UNIT: atm }}
```

What alignment cannot fix. **The reverse of hydrogen capping has nothing to act on.** The paper's central result is that an inert anneal above about 500 °C takes hydrogen off Pb centres that earlier steps passivated, and fast cooling in a hydrogen-free gas leaves them bare. Since 11 November the trap-density program has the reverse, F_HydrogenRelease: N_SiH returns to N_Pb at 1.2 × 10^12 exp(-2.56 eV/kT) s⁻¹ (Brower, as quoted by Stesmans and Afanas'ev 1996; the paper cites Stesmans 1996 for dissociation above 500 °C). In a 10 s RTA that releases 2% of the capped sites at 600 °C and all of them at 925 and 1050 °C. But N_SiH is zero when every RTA here starts, because no earlier step can be written with hydrogen (next paragraph), so the RTA still only lowers the density, by relaxation (4.68 to 4.67 × 10^11 at 1050 °C). The release is first order and does not use the computed Si–H energy (-1.28 eV per ½H2), and the program's second population has no release.

**No hydrogen before the alloy.** For the RTA to expose states, earlier steps must have passivated them: silane during the polysilicon deposition at 620 °C, POCl3 doping, and moisture during the slow pull at 600 °C. None of these can be written, so the predictor's control is an unpassivated interface that happens to match the measured 4.4 × 10^11.

**The capping rate's temperature dependence is Brower's, applied to a polysilicon gate.** The rate is fitted at 400 °C on Vitkavage's aluminium-gate wafers; Brower's E_f = 1.66 eV makes it 5.0 times faster at 440 °C (1.3 × 10⁻⁴ of it at 240 °C, 320 times it at 570 °C), so the 60 min alloy in 5% H2 caps as much as 2.5 h in 10% H2 at 400 °C would. The program now gives 4.68 × 10^9 after the alloy against 1.7 × 10^11 measured (-97%); before the 11 November change, with no temperature in the rate, it gave 9.65 × 10^10 (-43%). The paper says a lightly doped polysilicon gate needs a longer alloy; nothing in the program slows hydrogen through a gate.

**No wet oxidation and no thin RTO.** The trap-density program returns nothing for the steam RTO (it needs a dry-oxygen RUN) and does not read water. The dry RTO at 1000 °C, 8 s, is predicted at 3.7 × 10^11 against 2.5 × 10^12 measured (-85%): the program's second population is set by temperature alone, and its time scale is an hour. The thin-oxide program grows 2.2 nm in 8 s at 1000 °C against 4.0 nm printed and 6.2 nm in 30 s at 1100 °C against 7.5 nm, outside its fitted 800 to 1000 °C and its fitted times.

**Peaks and energies.** Nine printed numbers are band-edge peak densities after subtracting a hydrogen-passivated background. No program gives a distribution over energy without hydrogen, and no observation statement says that a measured value is the difference between two samples.

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **Hydrogen release tied to what the history put there (predictor).** The program now releases hydrogen (Si–H → Si• + H at Brower's rate), but a release can only raise the density where an earlier step capped sites, and on this page none can. It also has no release for the second population and does not use the computed Si–H energy against the gas's p_H2. Until the hydrogen sources below can be written, an RTA, a vacuum anneal and a rapid pull still look the same as no step.
- **Hydrogen brought by a step that is not a gas anneal.** Polysilicon from silane at 620 °C, POCl3 doping, plasma steps and moisture during a slow pull passivate the interface (the paper, PDF p. 9-10). The language records them as EVENTs that do nothing. A `SOURCE hydrogen` clause on a deposition EVENT, or an AMBIENT for a deposition (`mix:SiH4=…`), and a stated moisture on pulls, would let a program see them. The vocabulary of gases is Ar, H2, H2O, N2, O2; silane, POCl3, NH3 and N2O are refused.
- **Which step is the oxidation.** The RTO heat-up had to be written in nitrogen: the trap-density program takes the oxidation's temperature and time from the first dry-oxygen RUN, so a heat-up in oxygen from 25 °C would be read as an oxidation at 25 °C. A `ROLE oxidation|anneal|ramp|pull` on each RUN (as Hung's page asked) would remove the trap.
- **A gate on the sample.** Polysilicon, aluminium and a mercury probe behave differently under hydrogen (the paper: lightly doped polysilicon needs a longer alloy). GATE is recorded here as a sample field no program reads. A gate field in the ontology, read by the hydrogen operator as a barrier or a source, would let processes 1-2 and 3-5 differ.
- **A pull as a speed.** “a pull from the furnace at 600 °C at 8 cm/min” is a speed, not a duration or a cooling rate; the boat travel is unknown. A `PULL SPEED 8 cm/min FROM 600` statement with a furnace profile would turn it into a temperature against time.
- **A measurement that is a difference.** Table I's peaks are densities after subtracting a background measured on another (hydrogen-passivated) sample. An observation statement naming the reference sample would let such numbers be compared with a difference of two simulations.
- **An HF dip as a clean.** CLEAN accepts named cleans only; a bare pre-oxidation HF dip (process 3) had to be written not_stated.

## With Quantum ESPRESSO switched on

This page was simulated with parameters from atomic calculations switched on (`ISL_USE_COMPUTED=1`). They come from Quantum ESPRESSO (the discovered-materials dm_qe build), run locally on CPU and on an H100 on RunPod, recorded in `evaluations/atomistic/` with the code version, inputs, pseudopotential checksums and convergence, and carried into ISL as `STATUS: computed`. They never replace a fitted or sourced number: the rows scored above are the same with the switch off. Each computed row sits beside the measurement it bears on.

What is computed so far is one reaction: Si• + ½H₂ → Si–H for a silicon dangling bond with three silicon back-bonds (the local structure of a Pb centre), on a 14-atom cluster. With the free energy of hydrogen gas it bounds the share of dangling bonds that a hydrogen step could leave uncapped if it reached equilibrium, and so the lowest interface state density that step could reach. A measurement far above the bound means what remains after the hydrogen step is not limited by the strength of the Si–H bond: the anneal is far from equilibrium, or the traps that remain are not dangling bonds hydrogen can cap.
