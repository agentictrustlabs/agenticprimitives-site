*One of the [studies we reproduced](/writing/interface-studies-reproduced) for [Questions we need Emily to answer](/writing/questions-for-emily-testing-a-theory-of-the-interface), the companion to [the essay](/writing/testing-a-theory-of-the-interface-with-an-ontology). ## The study and why we depend on it

**C. Krzeminski, X.-L. Han and G. Larrieu, “Understanding of the retarded oxidation effects in silicon nanostructures”, Appl. Phys. Lett. 100, 263111 (2012); arXiv 1203.2803 (the version read).**

The second nanowire dataset, from the same laboratory (LAAS, Larrieu) eleven years before Müller 2023, and the one the radial program is tested on with nothing refitted. Figure 1(b) gives the oxide at mid-height on six wires and on nanobeams after 10 and 20 min of wet oxidation at 850 °C; Figure 1(a) gives two wires and two rings against time. Run unchanged, the predictor's radial program passes 4 of 12 wires (`evaluations/krzeminski_transfer_report.json`): it predicts too little slowdown on wires of 70 nm and below. `scripts/search_nanowire.py` then fitted the form of the curvature slowdown on Müller's five largest wires and this paper's six 10 min wires together and held out the 20 min wires; it chose a curvature length (exp(−ℓ/a), ℓ = 6.4 nm) over the viscous shell. The predictor still runs the shell with Müller's stress time, so on this page the 10 min wires are not fitted by the program that runs; they are the rows the alignment chooses its unknowns on.

## How well can this study be reproduced?

**Low.** One sentence describes the process: etched structures “wet oxidized at 850°C” for 10 and 20 minutes. No furnace cycle, no steam source, no wafer orientation or doping, no clean, no statement of whether each time is a fresh sample. Every curve starts at about 5 nm at t = 0 without explanation. Its value is the nanobeams: a flat sidewall oxidized beside the wires in the same run, which gives the rate a nanowire program needs without knowing the furnace.

#### Timing the paper does not give

- No furnace cycle at all: load temperature, ramp, gas before and after the steam, cool-down and unload.
- Time zero: whether 10 and 20 min count from steam on, and what grows the 5 nm present at t = 0.
- Whether each time is a fresh sample or one sample oxidized again.
- Steam source and partial pressure: “wet” only.
- The 30 and 40 min points of Figure 1(a) exist for two wires only.

### What the second reading changed

- The stored recipe is one steam step of 600 or 1200 s at 850 °C. The paper states 850 °C, “wet” and the two times, and nothing else; the stored recipe adds pure steam at 1 atm. Our history adds an assumed ramp and an assumed oxide at time zero.
- Every curve of Figure 1(a) starts at about 5 nm at t = 0. The radial program starts from bare silicon, so either the program misses 5 nm or the paper's times do not start at the start of oxidation. Writing that oxide as 90 s of steam before the stated time makes every wire thicker by 1 to 5 nm, which is the wrong direction for this paper.
- The predictor uses Müller's flat-surface rate (1.46 nm/min on bulk times a sidewall factor of 2.32, about 3.4 nm/min on a flat sidewall) and grows linearly in time. This paper's nanobeams, the flat sidewall of this run, have 32.2 nm at 10 min (3.2 nm/min) and 42.3 nm at 20 min (2.1 nm/min on average): the furnace's flat rate falls with time, and the program's does not. At 20 min the program's flat sidewall would carry 68 nm.
- The stored ORIENTATION is Si100. The paper never states the wafer orientation; the planar line it compares with is (110).
- The nanobeams and nanorings are not in the stored recipes. The beams are in the dataset as rows with no program; the rings are not in the dataset.

## Their terms and ours

| The paper says | We write | Kind in our vocabulary | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| SiNWs (convex nanostructure), diameter d | `SHAPE: nanowire; DIAMETER_NM` | sample fields (not in ontology/isl_vocabulary.json) |  |
| nanobeam of previous width L | `SHAPE: nanobeam; WIDTH_NM` | sample fields; no program | A flat sidewall in a nanostructure: the quantity the radial program takes from Müller as its sidewall factor. |
| nanoring, inner diameter (concave) | `(none)` | no shape the language or a program has | The paper's minus sign in Eq. 1. |
| wet oxidized at 850°C | `AMBIENT steam` | ambient | p_H2O = 1 atm is ours; the source of the steam is not stated. |
| oxide thickness measured in the middle of the structures | `Thickness (target: oxide thickness)` | observable (target: layer_thickness) | At mid-height of a 240 nm wire; the program is an infinite cylinder. |
| retarded oxidation | `F_ShellFlowStress: exp(-stress)` | operator kind Relaxation; parameter stress_time (fitted on Müller) | The paper's mechanism is reaction-limited by radial stress for convex wires; ours is a lumped form of that. |
| radial stress σ_r, activation volume V_k | `stress (a state, dimensionless)` | STATE | Ours is σ_r V_k / k_B T lumped, with no units of pressure. |
| hydrostatic pressure on diffusion, V_d | `(none)` | not in the program | The paper says this dominates for concave rings. |
| shear-dependent (plastic) viscosity | `(none)` | not in the program | Ours is a linear viscous shell. |
| Si bulk (110) | `(none)` | a planar reference not encoded | Possibly a calculated curve. |

The model itself, `experiments/muller_nanowire/radial_oxidation.isl.template`, as it is written. Every equation the simulation uses is in this text; the runtime holds no physics.

```isl
# ==============================================================================
# ISL MODEL: OXIDATION OF A SILICON NANOWIRE (challenge system 2)
#
# Data: Mueller et al., Nanotechnology 34, 105303 (2023), Figure 5: vertical silicon
# nanowires oxidized wet at 850 C, oxide thickness and remaining core against the
# as-etched diameter.
#
# The wire is a cylinder of silicon of radius a inside an oxide shell of outer radius b.
#   - Oxide forms at the silicon surface at the planar linear rate, times a sidewall
#     factor, reduced by diffusion through the shell in cylindrical geometry
#     (the linear-parabolic law written for a cylinder, after Kao et al.).
#   - New oxide takes more room than the silicon it replaces, so the shell must flow
#     outward. In a viscous shell that puts the silicon surface under a radial
#     compression proportional to the growth rate and to (1/a^2 - 1/b^2). The reaction
#     slows exponentially with that compression. One parameter, stress_time, lumps the
#     oxide viscosity and the activation volume of the reaction.
#   - The stress is carried as a state that follows its viscous value with a short lag,
#     because the viscous value depends on the rate it reduces.
# The outer radius is not a state: it follows from the silicon consumed.
#
# Not included: the effect of pressure on oxidant diffusion and solubility, stress-
# dependent viscosity, and faceting. Fields in {{braces}} are filled by
# scripts/run_muller_nanowire.py.
# ==============================================================================
SAMPLE Wire_{tag} {{
    FAMILY: Si_Nanowire
    CLEAN: not_stated
    ORIENTATION: Si100
    SHAPE: nanowire
    DIAMETER_NM: {diameter_nm}
}}
STATE a {{ UNIT: cm; INITIAL: a_0; MIN: 1.0e-9; MAX: 1.0e-4 }}
STATE stress {{ UNIT: 1; INITIAL: 0; MIN: 0; MAX: 50 }}
CONTROL T {{ UNIT: K }}
CONTROL p_H2O {{ UNIT: atm }}
PARAMETER a_0 {{ VALUE: {a_0_cm}; UNIT: cm; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Mueller et al. 2023 Figure 5, half the as-etched diameter }}
# ---- taken from the paper's own planar and flat-sidewall measurements --------------
PARAMETER rate_planar {{ VALUE: {rate_planar}; UNIT: cm s^-1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Mueller et al. 2023 Figure A1, slope of bulk (100) oxide thickness against time at 850 C wet }}
PARAMETER sidewall {{ VALUE: {sidewall}; UNIT: 1; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Mueller et al. 2023 p. 8, nanosheet oxide 44.1 nm over bulk oxide 19 nm in the same run }}
PARAMETER A_dg {{ VALUE: 1.1e-4; UNIT: cm; STATUS: sourced; SOURCE: Deal and Grove 1965 wet oxidation, A extrapolated from 920 and 1000 C to 850 C }}
# ---- fitted to the larger wires -----------------------------------------------------
PARAMETER stress_time {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: s; STATUS: fitted }}
# ---- assumptions --------------------------------------------------------------------
PARAMETER lag {{ VALUE: 2.0; UNIT: s; STATUS: hypothesis }}
# Growth is switched off smoothly as the core vanishes, so a fully consumed wire stops instead of going negative.
PARAMETER a_end {{ VALUE: 2.0e-8; UNIT: cm; STATUS: hypothesis }}
PARAMETER expansion {{ VALUE: 2.27; UNIT: 1; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER consumed {{ VALUE: 0.44; UNIT: 1; STATUS: constant }}
PARAMETER p_ref {{ VALUE: 1.0; UNIT: atm; STATUS: constant }}
QUANTITY b {{ UNIT: cm; EQUATION: b = sqrt(a * a + expansion * (a_0 * a_0 - a * a)) }}
QUANTITY growth {{ UNIT: cm s^-1; EQUATION: growth = (p_H2O / p_ref) * sidewall * rate_planar * exp(-stress) * (a / (a + a_end)) / (1 + (2 * a / A_dg) * log(b / a)) }}
QUANTITY viscous_stress {{ UNIT: 1; EQUATION: viscous_stress = stress_time * (1 - consumed) * a * growth * (1 / (a * a) - 1 / (b * b)) }}
CONTROL_TRAJECTORY History_{tag} {{
{runs}
}}
# Silicon is consumed at 0.44 of the oxide thickness formed at its surface.
OPERATOR F_RadialOxidation : OxideGrowth {{
    EQUATION: d(a)/dt = -consumed * growth
}}
# The compression at the silicon surface follows the value a viscous shell would carry.
OPERATOR F_ShellFlowStress : Relaxation {{
    EQUATION: d(stress)/dt = (viscous_stress - stress) / lag
}}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Oxide {{ TARGET: Thickness; UNIT: cm; EQUATION: Thickness = sqrt(a * a + expansion * (a_0 * a_0 - a * a)) - a }}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Core {{ TARGET: Core_diameter; UNIT: cm; EQUATION: Core_diameter = 2 * a }}
OBSERVATION_MODEL Obs_Consumed {{ TARGET: Consumed_substrate; UNIT: cm; EQUATION: Consumed_substrate = a_0 - a }}
EVALUATION_CONTRACT Contract_{tag} {{
    FROZEN: true
    DATASET: data/derived/muller_2023_nanowires.csv
    DATASET_DIGEST: sha256:{dataset_sha256}
    SPLIT: {split}
    TARGET: Thickness = {oxide_cm} +/- {oxide_tol_cm} abs
    TARGET: Core_diameter = {core_cm} +/- {core_tol_cm} abs
}}
```

### What our language and ontology cannot yet say

- **A reference sample in the same run.** The nanobeams were oxidized beside the wires; their oxide is the furnace's flat-surface rate. The language cannot say that two samples shared a furnace run, nor let a program take its rate from a measured co-processed sample. Here it had to be smuggled in as a steam fraction per case. A `BATCH` of samples under one trajectory, with a `REFERENCE` sample whose measured value is an input, would make that explicit and stop the steam fraction meaning two things.
- **A starting oxide on the sample.** The ~5 nm at t = 0 has no place: the radial program starts from bare silicon, and writing it as extra steam time grows it radially with the wire's slowdown. A sample field `OXIDE_NM` read as the initial shell (b > a at t = 0) would let the 5 nm be tested as native or ramp oxide.
- **Shapes beyond a cylinder.** Nanobeams (flat sidewalls) and nanorings (concave) are half of this paper's data and have no SHAPE a program reads. A `SHAPE: nanobeam` should select a flat-sidewall program, and a `SHAPE: nanoring` with an inner radius should select the concave sign of Eq. 1; today a beam stored with DIAMETER_NM is silently run as a cylinder.
- **Temperature and gas in the nanowire program.** Its rate depends on p_H2O only, so ramps, dry oxygen and the 850 °C itself are invisible. An Arrhenius rate and a dry-oxygen channel would let the furnace-cycle unknowns mean something.
- **Which form runs.** The predictor runs the viscous shell fitted on one paper while the project's own search selected another form on two. A program registry that records which form a search selected, and runs that one, would make the page and the search agree.
